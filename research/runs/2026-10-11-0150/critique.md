Retain failures/empty libraries as proposed. 2. **note — The sample is sensible, but its precision is a scenario.** With CV 1.15 and weak pairing, 1,536 jobs per arm plausibly give roughly 8–10% relative interval half-width before acquisition heterogeneity. That can distinguish a 20% saving from the 10% threshold, or exclude a 15% saving near no effect. The 24 independent acquisitions remain the inference unit; neither 1,536 searches nor the calibration's 91% motif rate guarantees that precision. Report the realised build spread and resolution price for both R-lib/R-tree and R-lib/R-size. A broad interval spanning no gain and a useful gain, or a clear baseline win with an unresolved donor-control contrast, would leave the central content question unanswered. No automatic top-up is warranted.

Matching node count and compiled length controls size, not token frequencies or subtree dependencies separately. A two-arm run is cheaper but loses the size explanation, while fewer target repeats risk another inconclusive cycle. Keep all three arms. At the proposed 2.9M acquisition evaluations, a 20% search saving repays after roughly 160 searches per library, and a 10% saving after roughly 320, before extraction overhead. Report uncertainty and measured seconds, including extraction/control construction; a search-only pass should recommend consideration of transfer, not assert economic usefulness. DG remains development data; later transfer requires a fresh bank frozen with the method.
