This is a feasible, decision-relevant test of whether useful fitting data precede exact solutions, with implementation and interpretation details to settle in `plan.md`.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible, but correct collection arithmetic and price the instrumentation.** I checked 1246's raw `search.jsonl`: G4's cumulative solve rates are 2.10%, 6.28%, and 14.87% at the proposed checkpoints; training G4 solved 174/256 and exact-fit C 936/1024 at full cap. Thus roughly 31, 30, and 27 of the 32 sources per cell should contribute at each checkpoint, using pooled historical rates. The 2,048 sources and 5,120 scoring searches are counted correctly. However, `2048 × 3.3 / 9.7` is **697 seconds, about 12 minutes**, not 2 minutes. The scoring assumptions give about 1.6 h centrally and 2.9 h with all three partial arms capped, before collection, fitting, diagnostics and overhead; the stated total queue envelope remains plausible. Partial-arm solve rates and variance are unknown: measure these in the smoke, include D1331 archive scoring and serialization in the projection, and freeze confirmation seeds separately. Retain the 3.5 h infeasibility gate and 120-minute preparation limit.

2. **note — Specify the checkpoint at the cap precisely.** In `45b2bdb`'s `composition_search.search`, exact checks precede reproduction, but `if evaluations == cap: break` also precedes lexicase parent draws. There are therefore no existing parent picks for the last evaluated population. Define checkpoints by evaluated population count (64/128/256, avoiding zero-based generation ambiguity), archive S and P from the same evaluated population, and specify a terminal lexicase draw using a copied selection state or independent instrumentation stream. Do not substitute the previous generation's parents or advance the search to obtain them. Replay must preserve search results; independently verify every archived tape is non-exact. Also state the weighting across checkpoints within each equally weighted source and whether parent slots are sampled with replacement.

3. **note — Worth running; the contribution is a controlled acquisition test.** The closest technique is correctly identified as EDA-GP, particularly [N-gram GP](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/DagSemProc.08051.5), which estimates instruction-triplet distributions. PIPE is an appropriate broader precedent. This is not a new learning principle: its contribution here is testing pre-solution data, identical-data C/T fitting, independent restarts and additional parent-selection enrichment. A positive would remove the exact-solver prerequisite for this collector; a bounded negative would stop funding this particular feedback route. The best prepared alternative is scoring the saved K tables on 1548's bank, at roughly 40–60 minutes of queue plus agents. That would clarify emitted-frequency versus order effects, but would not answer the present acquisition bottleneck. I see no clearly cheaper design with the same decision value; C_P and the positive control justify their modest additional cost.

4. **note — Keep routing and scope narrower than the motivating story.** State that “useful” means resolved C_S/T_S and C_S/G4 improvements with a worthwhile 1.20× effect still plausible, not that a 1.20× gain is established. A C_S/G4 interval crossing 1 means usefulness is unresolved, not disproved. C_P has already undergone evolution and selection, so C_S/C_P measures only extra checkpoint parent enrichment. Similar partial and exact fits would show no resolved performance loss at these acquisition budgets, not that complete solvers add nothing. This is training-cell reuse on a development bank; any later transfer claim needs a fresh bank frozen with the partial-fitting method before scoring.

5. **note — Name the uninformative outcomes and preserve their uncertainty.** An interval spanning both no C/T gain and 1.20×, or resolved C/T with unresolved C/G4, leaves the feedback decision unsettled. If both partial arms almost always cap, their penalized-cost equality says little about their relative search behaviour; report solves and sensitivity estimates before interpreting it. The ×1.12/×1.17 precision forecasts are conditional on exact-corpus variability carrying over. At the allowed 12-corpus prefix and SD 0.30, the half-width is about ×1.21, so even an estimate exactly at 1 need not exclude 1.20. Price further independent corpora if unresolved; do not read this as evidence that partial programs generally lack useful information.
