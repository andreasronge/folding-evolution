# Decision: run 2026-10-11-0652 (question 48, slot 40 of root 10)

**Close [48](../../questions/10-compositional-map-transfer/48-tree-donor-label-supply/question.md) and return
to strategy.** No proposal is written for 0833.

**Why close.** The primary landed well inside its pre-set loss branch. On gate-sum B/C, donors that keep the
learned skeletons, widths, sizes and multiplicities but draw labels uniformly within compatible classes cost
**2.07× [1.68, 2.61]** of the count-preserving rearrangement (R-perm) and 1.95× [1.64, 2.32] of intact
libraries. The rule was lower > 1.10 and point ≥ 1.25. Both validity guards passed (R-perm and R-lib about
0.37× of R-tree), reliability did not differ (1,725 vs 1,727 of 1,728), and the loss holds in 23/24 builds,
12/12 cells and all three realizations. The question as asked is answered for this replacement: with the
shapes held fixed, the learned label law is needed for most of the B/C gain. R-unif still beat plain tree GP
(0.755× [0.645, 0.884]), but kept only about 40% of R-perm's per-search saving and was not resolved from
random size-matched donors (R-size ÷ R-unif 1.095× [0.864, 1.367]). More seeds would only tighten that
skeleton contrast (up to about 30% is not excluded), and the result would not change the next acquisition
choice.

**What stays open (not a reason to continue 48 now).** The uniform law changed ADD share, ANY share,
constants and finite-library dependencies all at once, so which label component matters is unidentified.
Single-component replacements are worth running only when a statistical generator is actually proposed
and has to choose what to fit. That is now 48's reopen condition. Running them now would be another local
ingredient test of the kind strategy 0652 asked us to stop.

**Why not propose.** Root 10's allocation is spent (40/40), roots 01 and 23 have no budget left, and strategy
0652 said to return after this result: "worthwhile loss prioritizes preserving learned label supply in the
next acquisition comparison. Neither automatically funds another control or a learner."

**Recommendation to the strategist.** The result rules out "learn the shapes only, use a flat label
prior". Any next acquisition should keep empirical label supply: either intact libraries, or a generator
that fits size-conditioned label frequencies. Of the candidates in strategy 0652, I would rank
**different-source tree acquisition** first (7–10 h, planning estimate, unmeasured). It asks whether the
extractor learns a useful label law from sources other than the repetitive DG cells, and that is what
decides whether this library approach is portable. Second is the **unfamiliar-content boundary** (5–8 h).
It no longer bundles an unknown label-supply question, because we now know the label law is needed.
I would not fund a PIPE-like build before portability is known.

**Parked questions.** None reopens. 47's condition (rearranged donors losing resolvably) is not met: the
contemporaneous rerun gave R-lib ÷ R-perm 1.06 [0.88, 1.29]. Root 23 has no new measured selectable signal,
and nothing here touches root 01's conditions.

**Digest check.** Critique notes 6–7 applied. The digest now says the predicate was removed "almost
entirely". For 47's log, a dated correction was appended (the log is append-only) replacing "no matching
change in mean cost" with "no resolved mean-cost difference".

**Analysis error noted.** analysis.md §4 says uniform relabelling "discards about 85%" of the saving. Its
own numbers (17 k kept of 44 k) give about 60% lost, or about 70% on the log-ratio scale. The log, question
and digest use the corrected figure. The decision does not depend on it.
