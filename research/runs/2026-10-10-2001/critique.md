The saved-build crossing is a feasible, useful test of which component carries the DG advantage, provided plan.md narrows the attribution labels to what its contrasts actually establish.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; record the solve counts and calibrate the unknown arms.** [1717 analysis](../2026-10-10-1717/analysis.md) supports the quoted timings: DG D/T solved 307/384 and 187/384; TS D/T solved 364/384 and 372/384. Thus the primary roster has substantial separation and enough successes to measure search cost. The 4,608-search count and roughly 78–141-minute scoring estimate are arithmetically plausible at 9.8 workers, with preparation and reporting additional. But hybrids and bare fitted tables are **not bounded** by G4's observed runtime: different search trajectories and verifier tails can exceed it. In plan.md call this a costing scenario, identify the fixed calibration builds/cells/seeds, and admit each scoring entry only after sustained-load timings plus overhead fit its timeout. The explicit operator mode, separate RNG streams, native replay and initialization checks are appropriate; the existing code already separates ordinary variation and fragment RNGs.

2. **note — R measures replacement value, not every meaning of portability.** `cost(T/T)/cost(T/D)` correctly tests whether the D library improves on the T library under a fixed T table. An upper bound below 1.5 excludes a worthwhile **replacement increment**, not a useful portable library: both libraries could substantially outperform T/∅. Likewise, a large D/∅ versus D/D gain together with small R does not establish dependence on the native pair; D/T might work just as well as D/D. Address this in plan.md using the already-funded contrasts T/∅ versus T/D and D/T versus D/D. Keep table-carried and library contributions nonexclusive, and reserve native-combination language for evidence of dependence on library identity under the table. No added arm or elaborate testing scheme is needed. Define “library sufficient” explicitly as the proposed within-1.5× replacement tolerance, not complete explanation of the original gap.

3. **note — Precision is a scenario, and the expected result may remain indecisive.** With pair SD 0.6 and 24 pairs, a normal approximation gives an interval factor about 1.27, consistent with the proposal's 1.3; a point near 2 could clear 1.5. The assumed transplant/pair heterogeneity is unmeasured, however. R around 1.6–1.8 with its interval crossing 1.5, G also crossing 1.5, and weak bare-table separation would leave the representation choice unresolved. State that outcome explicitly, alongside mixed component contributions or failure of the fresh native references to reproduce the historical gap. Retain the promised resolution price and strategy exit. Freeze donor permutation and seed values before scoring; inference is conditional on these rosters and this donor assignment.

4. **note — Worth running at this price.** This changes a consequential belief: whether useful family-dependent information can travel as a library independently of its acquired decoder. Bare-table ablation alone is cheaper but cannot answer that; another G4 confirmation or TS precision run answers less. A fresh-bank comparison against a stronger synthesis baseline is the best alternative for practical value, but would not locate the component to learn next. The six-arm design is justified, including fresh native references. The TS half is secondary: specify what retention loss would change the next acquisition choice, as the concept plan requests, without turning it into another reciprocity gate. Both banks are development data; “portable” here means between saved decoders, not demonstrated fresh-bank transfer.

5. **note — Prior-work framing is substantially right.** O'Neill et al.'s common-subtree transfer is a reasonable close comparator; PIPE is a distribution-learning precedent rather than this estimator's exact implementation. Add the already tree-known [Keijzer, Ryan & Cattolico, *Run Transferable Libraries—Learning Functional Bias in Problem Domains* (2004)](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/papers/3103/31030531.pdf). The contribution is attribution of this learned family effect by crossing frozen literal libraries and context tables, not invention of reusable learned libraries. [Lenc & Vedaldi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5908) is a useful analogy, with the distinction that their stitching introduces a connecting layer; this intervention uses a shared token interface without fitting an adapter. External fitting, not inheritance, is correctly named.
