4. **note — A bounded gain does not identify shape specificity.** Rule 2's “advantage is then tied to the fitted shapes” and the final next-action sentence are stronger than this contrast supports. The new shape also selects tie-heavy F>m and M>F gates and a different behaviour distribution. Even D positive/F bounded would establish a boundary on this bank, not isolate branch placement as its cause. Use “gain below 10% on the frozen then-addition bank at this cap; consistent with shape specificity.” Give the low-solve guard precedence over mechanistic interpretation, and report per-arm/per-cell solve counts. Both arms mostly capped can yield a narrow ratio near one without answering whether useful uncapped transfer exists; 25% is an interpretive flag, not a guarantee that censoring is harmless above it. K remains unscored, so C/T is superiority to the restricted token-fitting procedure, not an isolated token-order effect.

5. **note — Retain the informative secondary comparison.** Restore row D's matched-versus-mismatched family analysis and own-family holdout/training shrinkage from the earlier proposal. Training scores each corpus on its own family, whereas D pools both families; pooled shrinkage alone mixes family mismatch with withholding compositions. F has no matched training shape, so its BE-minus-PA contrast is a source-family comparison. A wide F interval spanning no gain and a useful gain, insufficient completed pairs, or severe shared censoring would leave the central transfer question unresolved. C beating T while both lose to G4 would show relative fit superiority without useful adaptation against the hand-set baseline.

6. **note — Worth running; precedent is appropriately named.** Reusing the 32 frozen tables makes this more informative than another training replication or feedback iteration: it can close a real fresh-bank evidence gap. F alone is the cheaper alternative, saving about 66 expected queue minutes, but D earns its cost by distinguishing failure on withheld within-shape cells from failure on the new bank. I see no clearly cheaper design with the same discrimination. [Poli and McPhee's N-gram GP](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/9722/1/ces-479.pdf) remains the closest instruction-distribution precedent; it learns trigrams through iterative EDA updates, while PIPE uses position-specific tree probabilities. [Ardeh et al.](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Ardeh_2020_CEC.html) is an appropriate source-to-target distribution-transfer precedent. The contribution here is the frozen external-fit comparison against a same-corpus token control over independent corpora and prospective targets, not a new learning technique. No additional missing precedent is necessary to justify this boundary test. The corpus interval conditions on this bank; it does not establish transfer across arbitrary shapes.
