**What the program has learned.**

- **Bias matters, but random frequency is not a general explanation of search.**
  It predicts easy behaviours better than hard discoveries. Folding's tested
  fixed-target advantage largely tracked solver supply; cheap joins explained
  the tested composition advantage, while lineage mixing blocked rare shared
  forms. These are useful constraints on a learner, not a general developmental
  advantage ([01](../../questions/01-map-bias/question.md)).
- **Programs found by evolution contain reusable search information.** External
  solver-fitted context beat token fitting, including **2.12× [1.86, 2.41]**
  on a fresh then-addition shape. Pooled/positional frequency matching and
  undirected mutation widening did not reproduce it. Fragments and cheap
  feedback improved the complete procedure. DG versus TS acquisition produced
  useful family dependence in one direction, carried by both table and library.
  Outer-selected token weights helped; the contextual selection procedures
  tested did not add a resolved increment
  ([10](../../questions/10-compositional-map-transfer/question.md)).
- **Strong comparators changed what is worth acquiring.** Restarted tree GP
  needed about a fifth fewer DG evaluations than A8, although A8 used fewer
  worker seconds. Acquired tree libraries then reduced cost on a fresh shape,
  but supplied sum donors beat TS acquisition, and libraries harmed near-solved
  TS tasks. Learning is useful in specific comparisons, not automatically
  necessary or beneficial ([44](../2026-10-11-0024/analysis.md),
  [46](../2026-10-11-0348/analysis.md),
  [49](../2026-10-11-0833/analysis.md),
  [51](../2026-10-11-1019/analysis.md)).

  New acquisitions by the frozen recipe cost **0.577 [0.47, 0.72]** on protected
  signed-gate cells. Transported order libraries gave no resolved saving there

**The new observation and why it matters.** The
[2044 probe](../2026-10-11-2044/analysis.md) found a real difficulty boundary:
R-tree solved only 30% on its first attempt, capped 5.6%, and averaged 159k
evaluations. R-all/R-tree was **0.79 [0.60, 1.03]**, unresolved; R-gate/R-tree
was **0.30 [0.23, 0.38]**. These remain development observations. The apparent
coverage gradient is confounded, and does not identify missing helpers as the
cause. Depth and input sharing changed together.

The point saving is about 33k evaluations per search, against 216k acquisition:
repayment near **seven searches**, with uncertainty extending to never, versus
about 66 on the easy signed-gate cells. A smaller relative gain can therefore
have greater practical value. That, and testing frozen reuse on eighteen
unsearched compositions, earns confirmation. Passing feasibility flags alone
would not. The supplied prior's large advantage also prevents a modest acquired
win from being mistaken for an adequate learning method.
