**What the program has learned.** The answer to “how does the map bias discovery?”
is substantially stronger than the answer to “can that bias evolve to fit a family?”

- **Supply, discovery and persistence are different.** Random frequency predicts
  easy tasks better than hard evolutionary outcomes. Cheap joins explained the tested
  composition advantage; mixing between lineages blocked rare seeded shared forms.
  Self-mating removed that establishment barrier while still permitting discovery.
  Natural B-helper arrival and single-copy fate remain unresolved
  ([root 01](../../questions/01-map-bias/question.md)).
- **Selection acquires useful token bias, but specificity is unresolved.** Outer-selected
  token weights transfer roughly 2× to withheld compositions. Both initial programs
  and the decoder used during search contribute. The tested contextual selection
  procedures have not established a reproducible increment; calibrated continuation
  bounded C/T at 0.967× [0.871, 1.073] for its procedure and starts
  ([root 10](../../questions/10-compositional-map-transfer/question.md)).
- **Useful structure can be fitted from evolved solvers.** Exact-source context gave
  C/T 3.11× on comparison-gate training cells, 2.60× on protected holdouts and
  2.12× [1.86, 2.41] on then-addition when that bank was fresh. The cross-shape
  shrinkage is resolved. Tested pooled and positional frequency projections do not
  reproduce C; random recoding to match its mutation width makes Q slower at unchanged
  random-program supply. This limits those replacements, not all frequency learners
  or variation explanations ([1548](../2026-10-08-1548/analysis.md),
  [0537](../2026-10-09-0537/analysis.md)).
- **Fragments and chain edits both help under the fitted decoder.** On then-addition,
  F/C is 1.47× [1.38, 1.56], F/W 1.20× [1.11, 1.29], and W/C 1.23×.
  Fragments are literal shared syntax; this is externally fitted reuse on development
  data, not semantic modularity. The F/W primary interval clears 1.10, but its
  both-solved sensitivity does not establish that worthwhile increment. Joint content
  versus changed token supply remains unseparated on this shape
  ([1036](../2026-10-09-1036/analysis.md)).
- **Suffix protection is unnecessary at the tested resolution.** W/R is
  0.954× [0.903, 1.007], where greater than one favors repair; R/C remains
  1.286× [1.208, 1.370]. The repair's worthwhile 1.10× benefit is excluded under
  every reported sensitivity and family split. This does not establish equality or
  that ripple helps. Chain proposals work without containment; their length law and
  token supply are still bundled, so the operator mechanism is not fully identified
  ([1606](../2026-10-09-1606/analysis.md)).
- **Cheap partial material has not replaced the successful pipeline.** Partial-tape
  context improves fresh training search over G4 about 1.62×; feedback adds a modest
  gain over equal-allocation one-shot collection, resolved on BE only. The tested
  pre-solve fragment library adds no worthwhile pooled increment over matched chain
  blocks, E/W_E 0.981× [0.911, 1.057]; PA alone remains unresolved at 1.10.
  That experiment retained expensive exact-source C. Missing `gt` windows suggest a
  source difference, not its cause ([2116](../2026-10-08-2116/analysis.md),
  [1350](../2026-10-09-1350/analysis.md), corrections in
  [1606 decision](../2026-10-09-1606/decision.md)).
- **Inherited frequencies have not supplied useful frozen bias.** Root 23's tested
  procedure is worse than uniform on sum; on max a gain above 1.06× is excluded.
  Persistent ancestry helps relative to shuffled ancestry on max without establishing
  usefulness. This is a limit on one rule, not self-adaptation generally
  ([root 23](../../questions/23-heritable-variation-bias/question.md)).

**Current line against different mechanisms.** Literature searched for this review
places the fragment procedure near [Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico, *Run Transferable
Libraries* (2004)](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/papers/3103/31030531.pdf),
which carries learned functions across GP runs. Our extracted windows are narrower
than callable functions. [Ellis et al., *DreamCoder* (PLDI 2021)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/asolar/papers/EllisWNSMHCST21.pdf)
learns symbolic libraries and a search policy together. My inference is that we should
price and test both learned components together; those papers do not predict how few
solver tapes suffice here. A full abstraction engine is unnecessary for that question.

The mechanistically different owner candidate is **indirect selection on inherited
variation parameters**. [Stephens et al., *Self-Adaptation in Evolving Systems*
(1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9847423/) analyzes encoded mutation/crossover
probabilities favored through their reproductive consequences. This differs from
corpus fitting and from outer-loop selection on repeated search costs. It addresses
the evolutionary half more directly, but a new inheritance law currently has neither
a demonstrated useful signal nor a priced complete evaluation. The new sparse-source
study addresses an acquisition-cost prerequisite and must not be called map evolution.
