The matched continuation comparison is feasible and addresses the missing learned-context question; the researcher should address the calibration, precision and interpretation notes below in `plan.md`.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; make the solve-count assumptions explicit.** The proposal states measured throughput and work counts: 334,080 learning searches at 23/s take 4.03 h, consistent with the roughly 5.7 h total and an eight-hour queue. The supporting evidence is stronger than the proposal's sparse solve-count discussion: [0132 analysis §2.3](../2026-10-06-0132/analysis.md) reports M solving 1,753/1,800 training searches by 65k, 1,799/1,800 by 524k, and 1,194/1,200 holdout searches by 524k. Frozen G solved 194/200 holdouts; the difficult BE reference was 42/50 in 0001. These support usable cost comparisons, although perturbed R and especially R_abl may be slower. Include representative R and ablated-table timings in the projection, and reserve time for testing each completed pair before starting another. Sampling has a reasonable descriptive size: G produced 13–180 hits per cell per 10⁸ genotypes in 0001; learned-map rates remain unknown and zero counts must remain bounds.

2. **note — This is the right main comparison, with a bounded interpretation.** Frozen M alone would confound context with further optimization; M+ removes that problem. Equal search counts, matched starts and fresh shared training seeds make R versus M+ a useful comparison of learning procedures. Twelve pairs over 35 generations is a defensible substantial run, and the cheap frozen-M off-family check belongs alongside it. A pure contextual third arm or a new family would cost more and answer a different question; neither is a clearly better replacement. Equal search counts are not necessarily equal inner evaluations or wall time, so report those costs as proposed. R/R_abl is a useful dependency check, not proof that the residuals are uniquely contextual information that no retuned token map could reproduce.

3. **note — Calibrate the actual residual operator, and do not equate effect spread with beneficial signal.** The [probe code](steward_probes/probe.py) perturbs already water-filled integer M counts; R instead perturbs residuals on G counts × multipliers before water filling. Those differ for floor-clamped entries. Stage 0 should construct children through the actual R implementation and verify that zero residuals exactly reproduce each saved M table. Its variance subtraction is an approximate diagnostic: children share parent/seed noise, and the six task cells are fixed strata. Report the signed child effects and uncertainty alongside the estimated spread; large harmful effects alone can pass the 0.15 gate. A failed gate means this calibration did not establish usable variation, not that row moves have none. No additional elaborate gate is needed.

4. **note — Size against six starts and shared test seeds, not twelve independent starts.** The proposed bootstrap preserves the important clustering, but the power sketch treats twelve pairs as if their differences were independent. The two continuations share an inherited M map, and test-seed variation shared across all maps does not necessarily average away with more trajectories. In `plan.md`, give a conservative sensitivity calculation with six start clusters and the shared-seed term retained; distinguish added continuations from added starts when sizing a follow-up. Keep the planned twelve pairs and 200 seeds per holdout unless this calculation shows they cannot resolve the 1.25× decision. The principal uninformative outcome is intervals spanning both 1 and 1.25 on either holdout, especially if training and ablation are also unresolved: that leaves useful context versus inadequate optimization/precision unsettled. A deadline-truncated run must report its actual complete-pair coverage and precision, not inherit the full-run power claim.

5. **note — Narrow the outcome language without changing the design.** Row 3's “otherwise row moves found no useful context” incorrectly includes an unresolved R/R_abl contrast; say “no resolved residual benefit,” and distinguish a bounded small effect from an unresolved one. Row 2 bounds holdout gains below 1.25×; it does not establish no transfer. Likewise a BE upper bound below 1.25 rules out a gain of that size, not all extension to BE. Row 1 primarily supports allowing contextual moves as a learning procedure; if the residual ablation is unresolved, their contribution remains unresolved. Report whether a statistically resolved gain also reaches the stated practical scale: “faster” can mean well below 1.25×. Finally, specify that a failed contextual gate leaves only frozen maps available for stage 5, and that a harness mismatch stops interpretation rather than producing an ordinary off-family result.
