The constant-threshold revision makes the reciprocal sampling test feasible at a proportionate cost while preserving its ability to distinguish useful family specificity from generic enrichment.

**Choice.** Keep this experiment. The [1510 review](../2026-10-05-1510/code_review.md) supports changing the tasks and enlarging the fitting pools; it does not supply the held-out comparison already. Matched versus mismatched fits on the same >2 tasks is the decisive contrast, with uniform measuring absolute benefit and prune measuring how much simple junk removal buys. A hand-set aggregator bias is cheaper but does not test learning a bias from examples. Evolution should remain gated: a sampling-positive result still leaves A versus B in [08](../../questions/01-map-bias/08-evolve-bias/question.md) unresolved. Retain the inherited aggregator-swap diagnostic; it offers a cheap mechanistic check using this run's fitted vectors.

**Interpretation limit.** Both families now withhold the same constant. This controls the threshold across the reciprocal comparison, but a D result cannot distinguish failure to learn useful aggregator preferences from suppression of CONST_2 during fitting. Report its fitted weight and the swap results alongside D; constant overfitting is a possible explanation, not an identified cause. Parking this particular frequency-fitting approach is a reasonable stopping decision, but would not establish that frequency adaptation generally cannot transfer or that decoder changes are necessary. Likewise C rules out the chosen worthwhile specificity (3×), not all family-specific information. One holdout per family supports only this split.

**Size and feasibility.** The full caps total roughly 2.8 billion tapes, including three fits and validation: about 85 minutes at the reported uniform throughput before enrichment and exhaustive-check overhead. A three-hour ceiling with benchmarking and optional pools cut first is reasonable. At rates near 1e-6, 200M calibration and 500M transfer per arm yield hundreds of hits; further blanket enlargement is unnecessary. Ten million tapes per fitting iteration gives roughly ten elites at uniform rates, not a guarantee of ten; keep the low-elite fallback and report which tasks actually update.

Treat the 5M pre-queue probe as reconnaissance, not the inherited ≥10-hit eligibility gate: at 1e-6 it expects only five hits. Low counts there must not alone trigger parking; the planned 200M calibration is the eligibility decision. The probe is evidence for feasibility even though excluded from fitting and inferential pools.

**What leaves us no wiser.** Failed fit validation, insufficient fresh elites, or U bounds at the cap do not separate C from D. A result near the 3× boundary may remain U even at these sizes; preserve that outcome rather than extending automatically. Descriptive one-ADD zeros also say little about transfer. Successful sampling transfer advances the question but says nothing yet about evolutionary benefit.
