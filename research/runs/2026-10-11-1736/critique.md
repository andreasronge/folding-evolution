This is a feasible, bounded test of whether inherited operator choice improves fresh search, provided the plan calibrates clustered precision and confines a negative conclusion to this particular procedure.

1. **note — Feasibility is plausible; make the measured anchors explicit.** The grid is consistent: 432 exposure populations; 5,184 endpoint searches plus 3,456 shared reference searches = 8,640 jobs. At ten workers, 34,000 worker-seconds is about 57 minutes, and scaling 1427's exposure gives about 10–11 minutes; 150 minutes of queue timeouts leaves reasonable slack. In [1427](../2026-10-11-1427/analysis.md), readout solves were 287/288 for ordinary search, 288/288 for the initial law, 287/288 L-final, 286/288 S-final and 288/288 R-all. Exposure solve shares ranged from 32% in N to 54% in L. These support feasibility, but neither the new q₀ hit rate nor 50% solving under recurrent low-frequency exposure is measured. Put these counts and that uncertainty in plan.md; do not describe the fitted low-dose runtime as an observation. The prepare checks and complete-cost ceiling are appropriate.

2. **note — Calibrate precision at the unit actually being deployed.** CV ≈ 1.5 and 1,728 jobs give roughly ±10% only under an independent-job approximation. There are 144 acquired endpoints per arm, clustered in 24 libraries, and reference rows are shared. Before queue admission, use saved readout rows and an explicitly labelled endpoint-variance sensitivity calculation to check that the proposed nested, paired analysis can distinguish the forecast 0.95 from a 0.80 ratio. Keep each endpoint's matching q₀/reference seed block attached when resampling; do not duplicate shared references as independent observations. Specify the fixed exposure, scoring and bootstrap seed namespaces. No new efficacy pilot or extra statistical gates are needed. Broad uncertainty is an unresolved result, not evidence against useful inheritance.

3. **note — Worth running as a mechanism test, not as a competitive search method.** The important addition is recurrent selection followed by deployment of each population's own law. A resolved gain over q₀ and both controls would fill a real gap between externally fitted useful content and indirectly selected use of it. A tight small-effect result can stop this candidate. Another order-helper ablation would refine an already large intervention effect; it would not answer that inheritance question. A fresh-family acquisition test is the strongest alternative, but currently lacks a concrete, measured source/destination design. I see no clearly cheaper test of the same endpoint question; fixation counts or another pooled-frequency readout would not substitute for it. This approval does not justify a mutation-rate sweep.

4. **note — Freeze the interpretation of uninformative outcomes.** Resolve the tension between “headroom … deciding nothing” and “below 1.25 the test is uninformative”: report inadequate headroom as a limitation of this candidate, not a general failure of inheritance. Likewise, allele movement alone, beating only a degraded shuffled control, or a primary interval spanning both worthwhile and negligible savings leaves usefulness unresolved. Name the second branch “20% saving excluded for this procedure”; it can still contain a 10% saving. The forecast's ~100 distinct parents is not a measured effective population size, and the extrapolated selection coefficient/dose curve does not identify how strong a future learner *must* be. Replace the proposed root-23 strength conclusion with a bound on this fixed mutation, exposure and deployment rule. Keep acquisition cost and the much better supplied R-all/R-order alternatives visible.

5. **note — Prior-work placement is sound.** [Stephens et al. (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9847423/) addresses indirect selection of encoded variation probabilities; [Serpell and Smith (2010)](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/975827) addresses inherited mutation-operator choice. These are appropriately closer than credit-based adaptive operator allocation. Describe Kim et al. as online reward-based operator-rate adaptation rather than implying a separate outer evolutionary search. The contribution here is a GP mechanism/boundary test with linkage and neutral controls and a frozen fresh-search readout, not a new self-adaptation technique, learned library content, or fresh-bank transfer.
