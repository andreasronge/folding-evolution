This is a feasible, consequential test of whether a cheap generic restart policy removes A8's remaining evaluation-cost advantage, provided the plan qualifies its projections and deployment conclusions.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; correct the job-count wording.** Run [2254](../2026-10-10-2254/analysis.md) supports the stated early hits (tree 179/384, A8 174/384 by 65,536), full-cap solves and measured evaluation rates. The proposed DG arithmetic gives about 17,300 worker-seconds, or 32 minutes at concurrency 9.1 before restart overhead; Stage 0's sustained-job timing and 75-minute admission ceiling are sensible. There are **96 jobs per cell per restarted arm, 768 per family**; F-A8 has 48 per cell, 384 per family. State these correctly in plan.md and assert complete row counts. Give Stage 0 separate seeds and include them, validation/replay seeds and all scheduled attempt seeds in the audit; shared seeds between the two primary arms are the intended exception.

2. **note — The A8 projection and power claim do not yet model the deployed procedure.** The question's log explicitly says the simulation pools within cells and omits build heterogeneity, while a restarted A8 job retains its build. With build-specific success probabilities, averaging `1-(1-p)^8` is not equivalent to applying it to the average p; pooling generally overstates reliability. My read-only calculation from 2254 reproduces 110,563 evaluations / 98.68% when pooling by cell, but gives 123,714 / 96.44% when pooling by build. Neither pooling scheme captures both cell and build effects, and two observations per build × cell cannot estimate that interaction precisely. These are sensitivity calculations, not corrected forecasts. In plan.md, qualify the 99% expectation and the claimed 90% resolution probability, and price a sensitivity retaining build heterogeneity and the reliability decision. Four repeats improve scoring precision but do not increase the 24 acquisition replicates. A low observed A8 solve rate would not itself demonstrate dependent attempts: heterogeneous fixed-build success probabilities can explain a miss of the pooled forecast.

3. **note — Keep the purchasing conclusion tied to its cost unit and reliability criterion.** The −0.03 guard establishes no more than noninferiority within three percentage points, not “equal reliability.” Likewise “reliability fails” can mean its interval is unresolved, not demonstrated inferiority. Specify the paired build-bootstrap interval for this guard. If R > 1, report A8's reliability alongside its repayment horizon rather than calling cheaper capped expenditure alone a win. “Stop buying A8 here” must mean on the evaluation-cost criterion for this roster and schedule: at the projected R ≈ 0.78 and historical 1.6× tree cost per evaluation, tree could still consume about 1.25× A8's worker time. Report acquisition-plus-search curves in both units, labeling historical acquisition timing, before making a practical deployment recommendation. No transfer or optimal-policy conclusion follows.

4. **note — Worth running; closest prior work is correctly named.** [Luke's *When Short Runs Beat Long Runs* (2001)](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gecco2001/d01.pdf) directly treats short repeated GP runs; [Luby, Sinclair and Zuckerman (1993)](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~diz/pubs/speedup.pdf) supplies the broader restart framework. This fixed cutoff is not their universal schedule. No essential closer technique is missing. The contribution is a baseline boundary test for externally fitted bias, not a new restart mechanism. Restarting both arms makes the comparison more useful than restarting only tree GP. The best alternative, tree-native fragment learning, addresses acquired bias more directly but requires a new source/extractor procedure; settling its generic comparator first is worth this smaller run. The claim that 65,536 “does not favour tree GP” is too strong: the shorter-cutoff observation only shows that one inspected alternative favoured it more.

5. **note — Preserve the bounded exit.** An interval spanning R = 1 with unresolved reliability leaves the acquisition decision open; a resolved evaluation win with a seconds loss leaves it dependent on the owner's objective. Neither is equality or evidence that learning is useless. The proposed resolution price and return to strategy are appropriate. Saved rows already provide the cheapest historical full-cap reference; fresh F-A8 rows buy a contemporaneous schedule comparison, not primary precision. Retaining that modest secondary cost is reasonable; do not expand it or add a cutoff sweep. No clearly cheaper design answers the symmetric primary with comparable evidence.
