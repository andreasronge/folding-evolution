The reciprocal sampling experiment is the right next step, but the pruning control and negative-outcome gates can still turn an ambiguous result into a reason to stop.

Keep the shared alphabet, exhaustive exactness check, uniform-only task selection, reciprocal transfer matrix, and separate evolution gate. These address the previous critique; a different research direction is not needed. Revise three points before running:

- **Make pruning a credible generic control.** As written, deletion being harmless in 95% of the pooled training-perfect tapes can label an essential minority-task op irrelevant: an op needed by every solver of a rare fitting task can still be absent or unused in almost all of the pooled tapes. Separate deletion effects by fitting task and distinguish absence from harmless use. A conservative, cheap control would suppress only ops with no observed causal contribution on any fitting task, retaining uncertain ops and reporting support counts. Check the resulting joint pruning, since individually harmless deletions need not be harmless together. Merely finding prune within 3× of matched also does not establish generic dilution when matched still beats mismatched reciprocally; report specificity and the additional benefit over pruning separately.
- **Require exact fitting improvement before interpreting D.** The validation rule measures both probabilities but leaves “≥3× uniform” unspecified. Require it for P(exact), with uncertainty reported, on every retained fitting task. A rise only in P(train-perfect) could be learning shortcuts; failed exact transfer after that would not separate optimizer failure from D. Weight each task equally in the elite update, or explain how the pooled update serves the stated mean-log objective: otherwise abundant easy-task elites can overwhelm the rare ones. No validated exact fit remains inconclusive.
- **Make C/D depend on excluding worthwhile effects.** “Under 3×, or the CI includes 1” is not evidence of equivalence, and D currently uses point estimates alone. For example, a ratio interval of 1.6–5 admits a worthwhile effect but escapes the explicit inconclusive rule requiring it also to admit <1.5. Use an upper confidence bound below 3× to rule out the chosen worthwhile gain; otherwise extend within budget or mark unresolved. Apply uncertainty gates to uniform and prune comparisons too, and specify that inconclusive takes precedence. Ten exact hits is a minimum count, not sufficient precision for every decision.

**What would leave us no wiser:** fits that improve training-case success without exact success; sparse cells or intervals spanning the worthwhile threshold; or a deficient pruning control that makes ordinary op retention look like a gain beyond removing junk. Calibration also needs an explicit stop if fewer than two fitting tasks per family survive or the fixed holdout substitutes are exhausted. None of these outcomes supports parking as clean C/D.

**Size and cheaper alternatives:** a sampling-only queue capped at five hours is proportionate. As itemized, the base allocation is about 170M tapes (30M calibration, 45M fitting, 45M validation, 50M transfer), rising to about 260M if uniform and both single-family transfer pools reach 40M. Benchmark fitted distributions and exhaustive-check costs as well as uniform throughput; enrichment changes those costs. Extend according to decision uncertainty, including prune when decisive, rather than only counts below ten. A useful cheap diagnostic is to swap the fitted total SUM/REDUCE_ADD mass with REDUCE_MAX mass, leaving other weights fixed: the current families also differ in threshold construction, so reciprocal transfer alone does not isolate the aggregator as its cause. This is optional for the broad transfer question, but sharper than adding evolutionary runs now.
