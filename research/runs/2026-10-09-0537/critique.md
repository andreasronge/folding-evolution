This is an affordable, interpretable test of variation at fixed uniform-prior program supply, provided plan.md fixes the ratio notation and retains the strategy's narrower causal interpretation.

1. **note — Feasible; make the throughput assumption explicit.** [0306](../2026-10-09-0306/analysis.md) supplies substantial occupancy: Q solved 1,514/2,048 (73.9%) and C 1,771/2,048 (86.5%), at a 524,288-evaluation cap. At 11.7 worker-seconds per search, 4,096 searches on ten workers cost 80 minutes; at the proposed 25.7-second capped price, 175 minutes. The 200-minute scoring timeout and 230-minute total timeout are plausible. New-arm solve rates are unknown, and the expected slowdown makes 80 minutes optimistic. Identical lookup dimensions do not guarantee identical runtime: permutation construction, inverse encoding and memory traffic also count. Measure recoded throughput in preparation, including setup, and admit against the stated timeout without selecting realizations by performance. Retaining 16 corpora is sensible; the inherited interval-width forecast remains an assumption.

2. **note — Worth this run; cheaper alternatives are less informative.** Exact row-count preservation preserves every complete tape's probability under independent uniform alleles; the context-dependent inverse transformation also preserves paired initial programs. This directly tests whether changing variation can improve search without improving random solver supply, a useful acquisition-target decision for the core question. The strongest prepared alternative, [executable fragments](../../plans/learned-executable-fragments.md), costs an estimated 11–16 hours and needs semantic extraction/control infrastructure. R30 alone would halve scoring, but R100 cheaply distinguishes a failed calibrated dose from a useful stronger recoding within the same implementation cycle. Increasing mutation rate is simpler but changes direct-hit frequency and mutation count rather than reproducing downstream coupling. I see no clearly cheaper, equally decisive substitute. Hash actual lookup/permutation arrays, freeze the k-to-search-seed assignment, and retain the proposed replay and initial-token gates.

3. **note — Test recoding, not mutation width alone.** Restore the [strategy plan's](../../plans/distribution-preserving-variation.md) explicit warning: this changes edit correlations and potentially crossover as well as width. C-matched means and tail probabilities do not match the transition kernel, especially on selected populations. Specify conditional-uniform encoding for the solver-tape audit and measure the complete offspring operator as promised. A gain establishes an effect of this representation/variation change at fixed *uniform-prior* supply; it does not partition C's original advantage. Failure bounds these recodings, not the usefulness of wider mutation generally, nor the necessity of C's conditional rows. Then-addition is correctly treated as development data; this run cannot establish transfer or acquisition.

4. **note — Fix ratio direction before implementation.** The primary formula correctly measures cost_Q/cost_R30, but calls it Q/R30, reversing 0306's convention that A/B means cost_B/cost_A. C/R30's “upper bound ≤ 1.5” appears to use the old convention, and the written gap-share mixes the two. Define explicit quantities: G = exp(mean(log cost_Q − log cost_R30)); H = exp(mean(log cost_R30 − log cost_C)); descriptive gap share = log(G)/log(2.41), recomputing the denominator on the same roster. Apply 1.20 to G and 1.50 to H. Gap share is arithmetic closure of a performance gap, not causal mediation.

5. **note — Name the outcomes that do not resolve the choice.** A G interval crossing 1.20 leaves usefulness unresolved; failure to bound H leaves “approaches C” unresolved. A null with poor transport of the footprint match to solver tapes says little about the width hypothesis, though it still evaluates the fixed recoding. R100 beating R30 while both lose to Q does not make coupling a useful lever. Report its interval, and only call “no useful gain at either dose” when both upper bounds meet the stated threshold. No extra multiplicity machinery is needed for this descriptive secondary arm; broad intervals should return to strategy with the promised resolution price.

6. **note — Prior work is appropriately close, with a mutation-specific addition.** GE ripple/locality is the right analogy, not an identical algorithm: this is a fixed-length previous-token decoder, not variable grammar derivation. [O'Neill et al. (2003)](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/crossover-in-grammatical-evolution/) studies ripple crossover; [Stephens (1999)](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0006051) addresses operator effects among equivalent genotypes. Add [Byrne, O'Neill, McDermott and Brabazon, *An Analysis of the Behaviour of Mutation in Grammatical Evolution* (2010)](https://ncra.ucd.ie/papers/60210014.pdf), which separates mutation's structural and nodal effects. The contribution here is the exact distribution-preserving intervention against saved Q/C benchmarks, not discovering ripple or showing it is generally beneficial.
