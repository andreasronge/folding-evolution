2. **note — Size matching is a useful control, not an exact rejection or robustness control.** `tree_library.controls` matches each entry's node count and compiled length. That matches oversize acceptance conditional on the same recipient and insertion site; L and Z will evolve different recipient trees and sites. Their realized rejection rates can therefore diverge. Accepted random donors also differ in semantic neutrality and damage. Keep the arm, but call it size-matched, report the proposed rejection counters by state and pre/post-solve phase, and replace “Only (b) fails: robustness selection” with “content advantage over this size control unresolved.” Even equal rejection rates would not isolate all robustness effects. The historical five-percentage-point clone calculation is plausible (0.5 × 0.5 × [0.547 − 0.363] ≈ 0.046), but is not a measured rate for this exposure.

3. **note — Keep the continuation thresholds advisory and preserve inconclusive outcomes.** Failure of (a) does not identify B/C: strong drift, early loss without bit mutation, or insufficient exposure can leave selection unresolved. A neutral-arm interval excluding 0.25 is a diagnostic alarm, not proof of broken bookkeeping; validate alignment and modifier-independent random streams directly. L≈S with a broad interval, or L≈Z, would leave the selection/content mechanism unresolved. Passing (a–c) while the frozen L-final law offers no useful saving would likewise leave the core usefulness question unanswered. The strategist should inspect the already-funded readout and uncertainty before buying confirmation. End this procedure for investment reasons if appropriate, without recording those decisions as biological or algorithmic absence claims. These are descriptive observations, not a confirmatory outcome table.

4. **note — Correct technique and appropriately narrow contribution.** Inherited operator choice is self-adaptation/selection on a variation modifier, as in [Stephens et al. (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9847423/) and [Serpell & Smith (2010)](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/975827). The addition is testing an externally acquired structured GP donor law with measured population benefit, then testing an extracted global mixture after discarding programs. Averaging four final frequencies tests that aggregate deployment rule, not the expected performance of deploying one evolved population's law. State this explicitly. As requested for `kind: probe`, I judge cost and correctness, not require a new-technique or comparative-value case. No clearly cheaper design preserves all of the linkage, drift, size-control and frozen-readout observations; an allele census alone would omit usefulness. No further literature requirement is needed for this bounded probe.
