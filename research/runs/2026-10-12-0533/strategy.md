**What we have learned.** Map bias affects both arrival and subsequent search.
Random-genome frequency predicts easy tasks better than hard discoveries;
folding's fixed-target advantage largely tracked supply. Cheap joins, and the
loss of rare shared forms through lineage mixing, explain specific earlier
results without establishing a general advantage for developmental encoding
([01](../../questions/01-map-bias/question.md)).

Evolution's solutions contain reusable information. Externally fitted context
beat token fitting **2.12× [1.86, 2.41]** on one fresh expression shape;
frequency matching and undirected mutation widening did not reproduce it.
Cheap feedback and fragments improved the recipe. Outer selection learned
token biases, but the contextual procedures tried produced no resolved
increment. Stronger baselines mattered: restarted tree GP used fewer DG
evaluations than A8, while A8 used less worker time. Tree libraries subsequently
reduced search costs, including **0.778 [0.696, 0.870]** of ordinary tree search
on protected signed-chain cells, repaying acquisition after roughly five to ten
searches. Supplied priors remained cheaper, and libraries harmed easy TS tasks
([digest](../../digest.md), [2150](../2026-10-11-2150/analysis.md)).

**Useful inherited learning remains the gap.** Root 23's token-frequency rule
failed its frozen-usefulness test. Root 55 selected a useful supplied library
dose, but its latest content selector cost **0.991 [0.926, 1.060]** of the starting
mixture despite useful pure-library headroom. Post-solve relaxation is a

([0250](../2026-10-12-0250/decision.md)). These results concern small synthetic
banks; external acquisition, outer selection and individual inheritance remain
different mechanisms.

**Mechanistically different candidates.** Papers below were found in this
review's literature search; their model results are motivation, not effect
forecasts for this repository.

- **Continue 55 with discovery-only exposure:** approximately **7–10 hours**
  for development, additional work for transfer. It might prevent maintenance
  from erasing preference, but still chooses externally supplied tree content.
  Defer: it misses the new note's own-map requirement.
- **Evolve developmental decoding directly: selected.**
  [Watson et al. (2014), *Developmental memory*](https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.12337)
  models selection on developmental interactions that also change future
  phenotypic correlations. Our candidate lets small inherited context changes
  affect the resident chem-tape program, giving selection a direct signal rather
  than relying only on future offspring quality.
  [Parter, Kashtan and Alon (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000206)
  demonstrates facilitated variation under systematically changing goals in
  circuits and RNA. The repository addition is explicit transfer of only the
  decoder, with programs discarded and acquisition charged. Whether this works
  in a discrete contextual decoder is untested.
- **Parameterized library abstraction:** **12–18 hours** in the existing
  [plan](../../plans/corpus-abstraction-transfer.md).
  [Bowers et al. (2023), *Top-Down Synthesis for Library Learning*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16605)
  learns abstractions through corpus-guided synthesis. This could improve
  representation beyond literal reuse, but remains external fitting; defer per
  the owner. Inherited folding chemistry is another direct-development route,
  but `develop_with_dev` uses Python and lacks Rust support; its complete
  transfer cost is unmeasured. Prefer the existing chem-tape executor first.
