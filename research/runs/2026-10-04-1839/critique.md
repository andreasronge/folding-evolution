Self-mating is the most direct affordable intervention on the proposed hybrid mechanism, and the existing controls make this a worthwhile final seeded-establishment test.

- **Choice and cheaper alternative.** Keep the self-mate contest and the one-crossover census. Duplication-only random starts answer a different question; a schedule changes the operator over time and is less diagnostic. If compute needs cutting, the four 1/10 cells (120 runs) are the strongest first test: crossover-off wins are 29/30 against duplicated and 19/30 against partly shared, versus zero at selected-mate 0.7. The 1/32 cells add a useful rarity check but have a weaker reference, especially 8/30 against partly shared. At the proposed cost, all 240 runs are reasonable.

- **Size and interpretation.** Thirty seeds per cell can separate a large rescue from near-zero establishment; they cannot establish equivalence to crossover off or make the “two thirds” boundary precise. Treat that boundary as an exploratory decision aid. A rescue at just one rate would already demonstrate an operator setting compatible with discovery and seeded establishment; requiring rescue at both rates is stronger than the coexistence question needs.

- **The outcome that leaves the mechanism unresolved.** Few final wins, but shared individuals still persisting or increasing at generation 300, would confound slow establishment with failure. Report final mixed/unresolved populations and late trajectories, not only wins and early decline. Likewise, low wins without excess shared-form damage in the census would leave B unsupported. If a follow-up is needed, extend the unresolved runs or census their evolved genomes according to which ambiguity occurs, rather than automatically repeating the grid. In the proposed census, distinguish exact conversion to another form from loss of exactness, and retain clone frequencies: self-crossover often does nothing (§32 review, finding 4). Initial seed fragility alone need not explain a later evolved population.

- **What closing 06 would mean.** A rescue supports a mixing-dependent barrier for these hand-built layouts. It does not establish that natural shared helpers are rare *because they rarely arrive*: 32 or roughly 102 seeded copies are not a newly arisen single mutant, and evolved B-helper layouts differ from the seeds (§31 G). A failure shows that removing mixing is insufficient here; it does not erase the observed hybrid damage under selected mating. Parking the broader line is reasonable in either case, with those uncertainties retained.

- **Parked question.** No ignored reopen condition is established. [02](../../questions/01-map-bias/02-fixed-target-sampling/question.md) requires both a stalled shared-helper line **and** an owner desire to settle fixed-target map bias. This proposal still offers a discriminating test, and the second condition is not documented. Reconsider 02 when this line stops; it need not displace this experiment.
