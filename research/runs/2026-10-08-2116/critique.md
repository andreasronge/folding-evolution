The equal-allocation comparison can decide whether partial-program feedback earns its machinery, at a plausible cost; resolve the decision-rule and collection details below in `plan.md`.

1. **note — Feasible, with modest timing corrections.** The 1831 timing file totals 521 s for 2,048 sources and 6,623 s for 5,120 scoring searches. At those rates, 12,288 new sources cost about 52 min, round-1 replay another 9 min, and 6,144 scores about 132 min: roughly 3.2 h before additional orchestration and smoke. Thus “≤50 min” is optimistic, but the five-hour timeout and four-hour smoke projection gate are credible. State the worker count (1831 used ten), include replay/verification/fitting in the projection, and reserve reporting time. Prior scoring solved 746/1,024 C_S, 674/1,024 T_S, 624/1,024 G4 and 925/1,024 C_exact searches: enough headroom and enough successes for this endpoint. These are useful anchors, not measured F/O/TF solve rates; report the new smoke's counts explicitly.

2. **note — Make the practical decision consistent.** The stated 1.15× margin says smaller gains do not repay the machinery, yet the first branch adopts feedback whenever LB > 1. An interval such as [1.02, 1.12] therefore adopts a method whose worthwhile gain is excluded. In `plan.md`, give the small-gain branch precedence and state how adoption depends on the point estimate versus 1.15 when the interval crosses that margin. A statistically resolved improvement and an established worthwhile improvement are different labels; no elaborate new testing machinery is needed.

3. **note — Equal allocation is the right estimand; data quantity is not identical.** First-solve stopping changes actual evaluations, contributing sources and checkpoint tapes across F, O and TF. Replace “identical … data quantity” with identical source allocation, estimator and normalized fitting mass. Pool equally weighted *contributing sources* across rounds before cell rescaling; do not accidentally give each round equal weight despite different yields. Define an empty new round as contributing zero new counts while retaining earlier counts. Round-1 hash failure or an empty round-1 cell should stop preparation, not trigger a replacement. Preserve all 16 lineages and disjoint smoke/scoring seeds. A win identifies the feedback collection procedure, with yield and tape content bundled, not a pure improvement in each tape's teaching quality.

4. **note — Worth this run, with a bounded contribution.** Unlike another C/T replication, F/O tests whether changing the acquisition distribution helps beyond buying more G4 data; F/R checks whether the extra acquisition is worth retaining. Independently updating TF also fixes question 22's token-control limitation. The strongest ready alternative is scoring K on then-addition (roughly 4–5 h total), which would separate emitted frequencies from order on that bank but would not choose an acquisition route. I favour this run for the current decision. Executable-abstraction learning could change the representation more substantially, but presently lacks a priced, validated implementation. Training-only conclusions on this development bank are appropriate; any later transfer claim needs a new bank frozen with the final method.

5. **note — Prior-work attribution is appropriate.** [PIPE, Salustowicz and Schmidhuber (1997)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10021756/) already iteratively refines a program distribution using the best current program. [PBIL, Baluja (1994)](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/population-based-incremental-learning-a-method-for-integrating-genetic-search-based-function-optimization-and-competitive-learning) is relevant ancestry, rather than an exact description of accumulated-count refitting. The contribution here is a controlled acquisition/retention test using pre-solve populations and frozen reuse in this system, not a new EDA principle or individual inheritance. No missing prior work changes that assessment.

6. **note — Size, cheaper option, and the uninformative outcome.** With SD 0.22–0.24 log and 16 lineages, the t half-width is approximately ×1.12–1.14, not uniformly ×1.12; F/O's variance is still unmeasured. This can resolve a substantial gain or bound a near-zero one, but an interval spanning both 1 and 1.15 leaves the route undecided. Near-universal capping would likewise say little about search improvement. Keep the solve counts and penalty sensitivities, and price further resolution without calling an unresolved result equality or a plateau. Dropping descriptive C_exact saves about ten minutes and is the first sensible reduction; no clearly cheaper design preserves the main comparison and its acquisition controls.
