**What we have learned.**

- **Bias affects supply, variation and establishment.** Random-genome frequency
  predicts easy tasks better than hard discoveries; folding's tested fixed-target
  advantage largely tracks solver supply. Cheap joins explained the tested
  compositional advantage, and lineage mixing can prevent a rare shared form
  from establishing. These are constraints on a learner, not a general victory
  for developmental encoding ([01](../../questions/01-map-bias/question.md)).
- **Evolved programs contain reusable information beyond token frequencies.**
  Externally fitted context transferred across a fresh shape, **2.12× [1.86,
  2.41]** over token fitting. Pooled/positional frequency replacements and random
  mutation-width recoding did not reproduce its performance. Fragment reuse and
  cheap feedback improved the complete procedure. Outer selection learned useful
  token weights, but its contextual procedures added no resolved increment.
  DG/TS acquisition showed useful family dependence in one direction, with both
  table and library contributing ([10](../../questions/10-compositional-map-transfer/question.md)).
- **Structural baselines and supplied priors matter.** Restarted tree GP beat
  A8 in DG evaluations, though A8 used fewer worker seconds. Learned tree donors
  then cut costs substantially, including on a fresh shape. A supplied sum prior
  removed the average practical case for TS acquisition on B/C, while libraries
  harmed near-solved TS targets. Acquisition is useful in several settings;
  superiority to informed domain knowledge remains unshown
  ([44](../2026-10-11-0024/analysis.md), [46](../2026-10-11-0348/analysis.md),
  [49](../2026-10-11-0833/analysis.md), [51](../2026-10-11-1019/analysis.md)).
- **Acquired conditional computations help, with a large remaining prior gap.**
  Order libraries cost **0.37 [0.33, 0.42]** of R-tree on held-out cells;
  rearranging helper entries removed most development saving, against mostly
  trivial replacements. The frozen recipe also acquired useful signed-gate bias
  on a fresh content bank: **0.577 [0.47, 0.72]** of R-tree, with transported
  order libraries giving no resolved saving. Rectifier acquisition instead lacked
  reusable source content in its probe. On harder signed chains, saved gate
  libraries cost **0.778 [0.696, 0.870]**, repaying acquisition after about
  **5.4 [3.8, 9.7] searches**. The chain result is held-out-cell evidence from
  a development bank ([56–60 in the digest](../../digest.md)).
- **Input rebinding is a modest increment, not the missing acquisition answer.**
  Latest R-bind/R-all is **0.853 [0.785, 0.927]**: a saving is resolved, the
  pre-set 10% materiality label is unresolved. The supplied prior remains
  **2.5× cheaper**. Rebinding gives nearly every build every helper binding but
  preserves total helper mass; its coverage gradient is consistent with a
  reach/concentration trade-off, not an isolated causal demonstration. A precision
  top-up needs roughly 6–7 queue hours and would decide a label, not our route
  ([61 decision](../2026-10-11-2353/decision.md)).
- **Inheritance has selected a supplied dose, not family-dependent content.**
  Root 23 did not acquire useful token frequencies under its one tested rule.
  Root 55's recurrent selector rose above shuffled and neutral controls; frozen
  laws cost **0.805 [0.704, 0.913]** of the start, unresolved at its 10% bar.
  The three arm-average costs roughly followed endpoint-dose predictions; fixed
  R-all stayed 1.71× cheaper. This is evidence for indirect selection of an
  available law, with content learning and transfer still open
  ([23](../../questions/23-heritable-variation-bias/question.md),
  [55](../../questions/55-inherited-library-choice/question.md)).

**Current line against different mechanisms.** Papers below were found in this
review's literature search. They motivate candidates, not expected effects here.
Prices include implementation, validation, queue, review and analysis.

| Candidate | Decision, cost and scientific value |
|---|---|
| Continue external extraction/rebinding; mix literal and rebound donors | **Defer, 5–6 h for development alone.** The current line is closest to [Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico, *Run Transferable Libraries* (2004)](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/papers/3103/31030531.pdf), which transfers acquired GP functions across runs. A fixed literal/rebound mixture might improve search, but does not preserve full literal probability at fixed total donor mass: adding reach spends probability elsewhere. It is a new policy comparison, not clean dose isolation. A convincing continuation would also need separately frozen transfer, roughly another 5–8 h. That 10–14 h package is poorer value now than the inheritance question. Reconsider when broader deployment or a funded fresh bank makes policy selection consequential. |
| Inherited choice between order and gate libraries at fixed library rate | **Pursue, 8–9.5 h; ceiling 10 h.** [Stephens et al., *Self-Adaptation in Evolving Systems* (1997 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/adap-org/9708002) studies indirect selection of encoded variation parameters; [Serpell and Smith (2010)](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/975827) studies inherited mutation-operator choice. Here program ancestry selects among fixed acquired repertoires. It could establish useful family dependence without external selection of the modifier. A negative with headroom would locate a limitation of this inheritance rule. It still would not invent the contents. |
| Inherited intact versus helper-scrambled order library | **Defer, 6–8 h.** It has a measured fixed-law contrast, but most scrambled helpers became projections. Choosing the intact library could largely repeat the lesson of turning useful content on. Prefer two independently useful repertoires and test both family directions. Reconsider if that comparison lacks headroom and the narrower content-quality discrimination would actually decide whether to build an inherited repertoire. No automatic fallback. |
| Corpus-guided abstraction synthesis | **Defer, estimated 12–18 h complete.** [Bowers et al., *Top-Down Synthesis for Library Learning* (POPL 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16605) learns abstractions from corpus structure, evaluated by compression. It could generalize beyond input renaming, but integration plus search-value validation exceeds this window; compression alone is not our endpoint. Reconsider with a bounded adapter and a complete costed search test. |
