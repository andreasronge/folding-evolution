The bounded cap intervention is feasible and worth testing before buying a new abstraction method; resolve the overlapping decision branches and keep the conclusion at the procedure-and-development-roster scope in `plan.md`.

1. **note — Feasible, with credible headroom.** Recomputing the saved 1129 rows gives 384/384 source solves, 201,536 evaluations and 11.36 worker-seconds per acquisition. Destination solves were R-tree 96/96, R-order 96/96 and R-S 93/96; mean worker-seconds were 5.44, 0.37 and 7.63 respectively. These support the proposed 1,152 source and 2,304 destination jobs. Substituting R-sum's 10.56 seconds for R-all gives approximately 13.8k worker-seconds; substituting 38 seconds gives approximately 29.6k. Thus 25–51 minutes on ten workers and a 90-minute scoring timeout are plausible, not guarantees. Include the destination solve counts in the plan, retain all failed acquisitions, and stop on resource failure rather than analyzing only completed, fast jobs. The approximately eight-search evaluation repayment at a 0.67 ratio checks out.

2. **note — Make routing unambiguous before scoring.** An interval such as [0.70, 0.78] satisfies both “useful acquisition” (upper < 0.80) and “end this recipe” (lower > 0.67). It also excludes the stated worthwhile one-third saving. Choose one worthwhile threshold for both decisions, or explicitly distinguish evidence of a smaller saving from qualification for further investment and assign precedence. This is a repair to the decision rule, not a reason to change the experiment. A broad interval spanning both thresholds would leave the investment decision unresolved; more helper entries or an improvement over R-S alone would not settle it.

3. **note — Worth this run; contribution is a boundary test.** Question 52's changed-extractor reopening condition is now met by the funded, pre-stated development test. Forty-four admissible recurrent helper–build cases were discarded by the cap, so this intervention addresses an observed obstruction rather than another arbitrary library variant. Fresh acquisitions test repeatability across source searches; reusing the eight pilot libraries would be cheaper but would only answer a conditional question about the selected pilot corpora. A parameterized-abstraction adapter such as Stitch is the strongest different next use of the budget identified in the strategy, but is substantially more expensive and unnecessary to test whether the already available repertoire suffices. I see no clearly cheaper, equally decisive substitute for this four-arm comparison.

4. **note — Prior work and interpretation.** [Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico's Run Transferable Libraries](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/tocs/t3103.htm) is the appropriate closest technique; the proposal also identifies the semantic-library precedent in [Pawlak, Wieloch and Krawiec](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Pawlak_2014_ieeeEC.html). This is externally fitted subtree reuse, not a new library-learning principle or inherited adaptation. Its contribution is whether this recurrence-only semantic recipe produces useful search bias on order compositions after dilution and acquisition are charged. R-all/R-S isolates the *cap-removal intervention*, not the causal value of min/max helpers: entry sizes, other content and sampling probabilities change together. Keep helper-coverage splits descriptive. A win would support this procedure on six development cells, not general transfer or superiority to R-order. A later transfer claim needs the fresh-bank/method freeze required by the task-bank rule; untouched cells inside an already used bank do not by themselves establish that qualification.

5. **note — Precision is adequate for a large effect, uncertain near the boundary.** I obtain a sample SD of 0.455 for the eight pilot per-build log arithmetic R-S/R-tree ratios, close to the stated 0.47. Holding that variability fixed gives roughly ×/÷1.20 at 24 builds. The ×/÷1.15 end assumes useful variance reduction from extra repeats and is not established for R-all. Bootstrap the ratio of pooled arithmetic means with equal cell weights and paired whole builds, rather than averaging build ratios or treating 576 jobs as independent acquisitions. The proposed size can discriminate a strong saving from little benefit; a marginal effect near the thresholds can remain unresolved. No additional statistical gates or automatic top-up are needed.
