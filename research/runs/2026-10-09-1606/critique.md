The matched boundary intervention is feasible and can inform the next acquisition baseline, provided the plan makes its resolution limits and implementation checks explicit.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible, but state the solve scenarios.** [1036's analysis](../2026-10-09-1036/analysis.md) reports W solving 3,643/4,096 then-addition searches (88.9%) and 986/1,024 holdouts (96.3%), versus C's 3,566 and 950. Those are useful anchors missing from the proposal's feasibility paragraph; R's rate is unmeasured. In `plan.md`, include an adverse scenario such as 80% primary solves (about 3,277), and make the timing smoke an admission check at the intended concurrency. The stated normal cost checks out: `(4096 × 6.20 + 1024 × 3.37)/10` is 48 minutes, about 55 with margin. The fresh-W/R fallback is also plausibly about 99 minutes. Historical timings are anchors, not a guarantee that ripple leaves search cost unchanged; specify what happens if the complete roster cannot fit the timeout. Three hours of agent work is plausible for this narrow extension of existing code.

2. **note — Correct contrast; validate the actual decoded suffix.** Source inspection at `e9a04f8` confirms that W repairs one boundary allele after adding blocks to ordinary variation. R's conditional neutral refresh is a sensible control for that refresh operation. Extend the forced-edit audit explicitly to tape-end edits, no-op blocks and unchanged final block tokens, and check pairing metadata over the full historical roster as well as representative replays. The old operator's `desired` array and change counter assume suffix preservation: R diagnostics must decode the resulting child and count actual suffix changes. “Nothing beyond the boundary changes” must mean **alleles**, not decoded tokens. Identical block proposals are an invariant on an identical input child with shared draws; they are not guaranteed along divergent evolutionary trajectories. This tests the boundary policy, including its downstream effects, rather than isolating semantic locality or a numerical fraction of W/C's gain.

3. **note — The expected result often will not decide the operator choice.** At the projected ×1.07 half-width, rule 3 requires a point estimate below `1.10/1.07 ≈ 1.028`; rule 2 requires a point at least 1.10. Thus nearly all the stated expectation, 1.03–1.10, lies in the unresolved region. For example, 1.05 with roughly [0.981, 1.124] leaves both no benefit and a worthwhile benefit compatible with the data. Say this in `plan.md`, retain the incumbent repair if unresolved, and report the resolution price without automatically extending the run. W/C's precision is only a scenario for W/R, not a measured variance for this contrast. Rule 2 supports retaining repair under the chosen decision rule; it does not establish a true gain above 1.10 or that preservation is necessary. A null near 1 with upper bound below 1.10 is informative at that resolution, not equality.

4. **note — Worth running as one mechanism study.** The result can change whether a future acquired decoder needs protected block boundaries. A `gt`-fragment knockout is the strongest nearby alternative, but currently changes replacement content and token supply and lacks a concrete cheaper source that its answer would select. A complete few-solver acquisition study would address the core question more directly, but has no measured complete cost yet. Neither is clearly more decisive at this price. Reusing W/C after validation is already the main economy; omitting descriptive holdouts saves only about six scoring minutes, so is an optional saving rather than a redesign requirement. Keep the one-run exit. These used banks support a mechanism result only; fresh-bank transfer requires a new bank frozen with the method before scoring.

5. **note — Prior-work framing is substantially right.** Representation locality/ripple in GE is the appropriate precedent, with external fitting correctly distinguished from inheritance. Replace “they disagree on the sign” with “effects depend on operator and representation”: [Rothlauf and Oetzel](https://d-nb.info/1189914964/34) studied mutation-based locality, whereas [O'Neill et al.](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/crossover-in-grammatical-evolution/) studied crossover. A relevant omitted precursor is [Byrne, O'Neill, McDermott and Brabazon, *An Analysis of the Behaviour of Mutation in Grammatical Evolution* (2010)](https://ncra.ucd.ie/papers/60210014.pdf), which separates structural and nodal mutation components. The contribution here is a matched boundary-policy test in this fitted previous-token decoder, not discovery of locality or a general resolution of the GE literature.
