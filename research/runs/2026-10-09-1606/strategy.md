**What we have learned.** The program now separates useful bias, how it acts during
search, and how it is acquired. Evidence for the first is much stronger than for the last.

- **Random supply does not determine search or establishment.** Root 01 found that
  random frequency predicts easy tasks, while harder discoveries and their persistence
  depend on selection and variation. Cheap joins explained the tested composition
  advantage; lineage mixing explained the barrier to rare seeded shared forms.
  Natural B-helper arrival and single-copy fate remain unresolved. These results
  constrain mechanism claims, rather than justify another helper study
  ([root 01](../../questions/01-map-bias/question.md)).
- **Selection can acquire transferable token bias.** Outer-selected token weights
  improve withheld-composition search about 2×; initialization and ongoing decoder
  use both contribute. Family specificity remains unresolved. The tested contextual
  selection procedures have not established an additional reproducible benefit;
  calibrated continuation bounded C/T at 0.967× [0.871, 1.073] for its procedure and
  token-tuned starts ([root 10](../../questions/10-compositional-map-transfer/question.md)).
- **Externally fitted structure is a useful target.** Solver-corpus context gives
  C/T 3.11× on comparison-gate training cells, 2.60× on protected holdouts, and
  2.12× [1.86, 2.41] on then-addition when that bank was fresh. The cross-shape
  shrinkage is resolved. Tested pooled and positional frequency projections leave
  C about 2.4× faster; random recoding that matches its mutation width hurts Q at
  exactly unchanged random-program supply. This rules out those replacements,
  not all positional learners or all variation explanations
  ([1548](../2026-10-08-1548/analysis.md), [0537](../2026-10-09-0537/decision.md)).
- **Both a repertoire and an operator contribute.** On then-addition, exact-solver
  fragments give F/C 1.466× [1.380, 1.558], F/W 1.195× [1.111, 1.286], while
  C-chain blocks alone give W/C 1.227× [1.148, 1.311]. The primary F/W interval
  clears 1.10, but the both-solved sensitivity does not. These are useful procedure
  results on development banks, not semantic modularity or evolutionary acquisition.
  Joint content versus changed token supply remains unseparated across shape
  ([1036](../2026-10-09-1036/analysis.md)).
- **Cheap early material has a limited signal.** Full-tape pre-solve context improves
  training search over G4 (1.62×), and feedback gives a modest improvement over
  equal-allocation one-shot collection (1.18×), resolved on BE only. Yet the latest
  pre-solve fragment library adds no worthwhile increment over matched chain blocks:
  E/W_E 0.981× [0.911, 1.057], below the prespecified 1.10 bar, and E/F 0.843×
  [0.795, 0.892]. That ends this source/extractor, not pre-solve learning generally.
  Missing `gt` windows are a hypothesis about the difference, not its identified cause
  ([2116](../2026-10-08-2116/analysis.md), [1350](../2026-10-09-1350/analysis.md)).
- **Per-individual inheritance has not delivered useful frozen bias.** Root 23's
  one tested frequency-modifier procedure is worse than uniform on sum; on max a
  gain above 1.06× is excluded. Persistent ancestry helps relative to shuffled ancestry
  on max without establishing usefulness. This limits that procedure, not
  self-adaptation ([root 23](../../questions/23-heritable-variation-bias/question.md)).

**Current line versus different mechanisms.** Literature searched for this review
supports three distinct precedents, not a novel-technique claim. The current frozen
fragment procedure is closest to [Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico, *Run Transferable
Libraries* (2004)](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/papers/3103/31030531.pdf),
which transfers learned functions between GP runs. Our literal insertion windows are
narrower than callable functions, and are extracted externally.

The alternative pursued here changes **variation locality** without acquiring more
content. [Rothlauf and Oetzel, *On the Locality of Grammatical Evolution*
(2005 working paper; EuroGP 2006)](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/1255/1/ww_11_2005.pdf)
studies how nearby genotypes can map to distant programs and impair mutation search.
Our inference is narrower: the combination of harmful random recoding and helpful
suffix-preserving chain blocks makes containment worth testing in this decoder.
Neither result already establishes its benefit, and token locality is not output locality.

The owner's alternative changes **how variation laws receive evolutionary credit**.
[Stephens et al., *Self-Adaptation in Evolving Systems* (1998; 1997 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/adap-org/9708002)
studies encoded mutation/crossover probabilities whose reproductive consequences
permit indirect selection. This is mechanistically different from fitting a corpus
or manually altering an operator. It remains scientifically important, but another
modifier-noise trial lacks a new evidence-based reason to expect useful acquisition.
