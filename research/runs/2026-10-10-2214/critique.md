This is a worthwhile, plausibly affordable test of whether acquired tape bias survives a conventional structural baseline; plan.md must separate its search-cost verdict from acquisition repayment and make calibration operational.

1. **note — Feasibility is plausible, conditional on Stage 0.** The 1,792 scored searches add up correctly. On these rosters, [2001](../2026-10-10-2001/analysis.md) observed native A8 solving 312/384 DG and 363/384 TS searches; [1717](../2026-10-10-1717/analysis.md) observed G4 at 59/384 and 266/384. Those support the stated A8/G4 rates. There is no measured tree hit rate: “most DG searches” is an expectation, not an admission fact. At 51 seconds for every tree search, the scored work is approximately 47 minutes DG and 38 minutes TS at ten effective workers, before reporting overhead; calibration alone is about 22 minutes. Thus the four-hour envelope is credible, but 25 ms/generation is an assumption, not a bound. In plan.md specify entry timeouts, sustained-concurrency timing including full-cap tails, exact-check overhead and the 80% admission calculation. The 25-minute preparation allowance has little worst-case slack. A failed timing/build gate should return to strategy as proposed.

2. **note — Good purchase relative to the alternatives.** A new G4-relative confirmation or five-pair component top-up would refine effects already sufficient to retain both components. This comparison can instead change which search procedure deserves broader testing. The full native cohorts, fresh paired case draws, separate TS reading and fixed development-bank scope are appropriate. A modest G4 reference is affordable; dropping it saves little compared with the implementation/review cost. No clearly cheaper design answers the same practical question as well. Any subsequent transfer claim still requires a fresh bank frozen together with both methods before scoring.

3. **note — Correct the economic interpretation, without changing the primary contrast.** An interval inside [0.67, 1.5] supports “no difference exceeding the chosen geometric-cost margin,” not “acquisition is not repaid.” A smaller positive arithmetic saving can repay at a long horizon, and geometric and arithmetic rankings can differ. Conversely, Q > 1.5 does not itself establish wall-time repayment. Keep the proposed arithmetic acquisition-plus-search curves and N* separate, charge all acquisition/fitting/extraction, distinguish actual capped expenditures from the artificial 2× failure penalty, and label nonpositive estimated savings “no estimated repayment,” not an established “never.” Report uncertainty in savings and conditional repayment, including an unbounded horizon when compatible with zero savings. No assumed deployment horizon is needed.

4. **note — Closest technique and implementation freeze.** With only integer-consuming functions, the effective comparator is ordinary closure-based subtree GP with readout terminals and lexicase, as the proposal itself acknowledges. Add [Koza (1992)](https://www.genetic-programming.org/gpbook1toc.html) alongside [Montana (1995)](https://davidmontana.net/papers/stgp.pdf); the latter is relevant background, but this does not test the benefit of strong typing. PIPE-like distribution fitting plus fragment reuse is a reasonable description of A8, not an exact PIPE reproduction. The contribution is the scoped procedure comparison and acquisition accounting, not a new GP mechanism. Freeze terminal/function sampling, subtree-point selection, initialization size rejection, crossover offspring handling, calibration aggregation/ties and disjoint calibration/scoring seeds in plan.md. Treat the 25% solve flag as a diagnostic, not permission to tune until the baseline wins or proof that it is competitive when it passes.

5. **note — State where the experiment may leave the choice unresolved.** Historical A8 build SD alone cannot promise Q's ×/÷1.33 interval: tree seed variability and paired covariance also matter. Use the calibration to check that precision assumption before target scoring. With that width, Q near 1 can resolve the bounded-difference branch, but roughly Q = 1.2–2.0 can remain unresolved against 1.5. A weak or heavily size-rejected tree implementation would also leave us little wiser about a strong structural baseline, even if A8 wins. Preserve the proposed resolution price and strategy exit; report rejection rates and reliability so these outcomes are recognizable rather than interpreted as learning superiority.
