End this autonomous run and wait for the owner. Root [01-map-bias](../../questions/01-map-bias/question.md)
has spent all six experiments, and every child is closed or parked. `research.py status`
confirms zero remaining budget throughout the tree. More importantly, the available
follow-ups now refine narrow effects without resolving the largest gap in the
[core question](../../../README.md#core-question): whether an evolving map acquires useful
task-family structure. I am not opening a new root; the next direction needs a stronger
transfer target before it warrants an experimental budget.

**What the program has learned.** Random-genotype frequency is a useful baseline for
arrival, but it does not by itself explain evolutionary outcomes. Folding's fixed-target
advantage is consistent with making exact solvers more common. Evolution mostly lost to
matched-budget sampling in that harness, whereas TAG lexicase on the recent thresholds
beat random search computed from sampling rates. These different tasks and search regimes
do not isolate a general advantage of either map. Selection, assembly and establishment
matter alongside initial supply: cheap joins explain the tagged recombination advantage,
and self-mating removes the establishment barrier for seeded shared forms. Naturally
arising B-helper establishment remains unresolved; the single-copy study observed a
different kind of shared child. See [findings](../../../docs/map-bias/findings.md),
[06 analysis](../2026-10-04-1839/analysis.md) and [07 analysis](../2026-10-04-2135/analysis.md).

Part 2 has a useful, bounded positive. Externally fitting token frequencies on two
thresholds improved sampling on a held-out constant and sped median evolutionary solving
about fourfold over uniform. A hand-set INPUT/GT/aggregator vector performed comparably;
family-specific speed gains over the other family's fit remained unresolved against the
chosen 2× bar. On max>2, raising INPUT/GT reproduced the generic speed-up, while also
raising exact-solver supply. The full vector's nearly unchanged supply concealed opposing
component effects. This establishes useful interventions under a fixed decoder, with
initialization and mutation changed together. It leaves heritable map adaptation, transfer
beyond constant substitution, and savings after fitting cost untested. See
[1558](../2026-10-05-1558/analysis.md), [1705](../2026-10-05-1705/analysis.md) and
[1814](../2026-10-05-1814/analysis.md).

The final [1957 pilot](../2026-10-05-1957/analysis.md) leaves one interesting residual:
R speeds sum>2 evolution despite lower measured exact-solver supply. Exact max>2 was the
solver's immediate parent in 55/60 exposed ordinary runs; vetoing it still allowed all
100 veto runs to solve, with near-max alternatives more common. Its contribution to R's
advantage is unknown. The main stage never ran. The failed, unstable power gate supplies
neither a null on the stepping-stone explanation nor evidence that a decisive experiment
is unaffordable. Stopping here follows the prior commitment and the question's diminishing
strategic value; it does not close the mechanism question.

Older tracks give the same warning about transfer. Folding's strongest scaffold result
ultimately depended on reachable structures, preserved inventory and compatible
partial-credit scoring. Chem-tape's slot transfer depended on specified shared bodies;
its breadth checks and plasticity results did not establish general family learning.
CA's spatial and temporal specialization improved parity performance, while developmental
repair remains a separate question. The Elixir coevolution designs exposed role conflict
and constant-output collapse, without demonstrating an adaptive map. These are useful
constraints on a future design, not grounds to resume all tracks. Sources:
[folding assessment](../../../docs/folding/findings.md#8-the-complexity-ceiling-revised),
[chem-tape findings](../../../docs/chem-tape/findings.md),

**Which questions matter now.** There is only one existing root. Within 01, the question
behind 08—learning transferable bias—has the highest remaining value for the core
question. The evidence already shows that changing frequencies can help; the missing
distinction is what family experience teaches the map beyond a manually chosen scaffold.
09's sum>2 mechanism is secondary until that distinction requires it. The fixed-target
rarity ladder in 02 would sharpen part 1, but would leave part 2 untouched. Shared-helper
refinements have no new B-helper arrival or replay evidence to justify reopening. CA
robustness is a coherent alternative project, with a less direct connection to the current
family-bias question.

**What the steward should work on next, when the owner resumes the program.** Ask:
**Can a map adapted across related tasks help fresh program populations solve unseen
compositions, beyond what a fixed token-frequency scaffold provides?** This targets what
is retained in the map after the previously evolved programs are removed. A useful next
target would change the arrangement or combination of operations across family members,
rather than only a constant or a task-bound slot. It must be expressible and tractable,
and allow comparison with a simple fixed bias and the cost of adapting the map. Establish
that target in a short concept plan before building heritable-decoder machinery. There
is no such demonstrated target in the current record, so this is a restart direction,
not a queued experiment or an automatic reopening of 08.
