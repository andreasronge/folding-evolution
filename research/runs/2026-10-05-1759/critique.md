Keep the four-arm experiment, but fix the reversed performance gate and make its decisions identify vector components rather than mechanisms the measurements cannot distinguish.

The factorial is a good bounded follow-up to [1705](../2026-10-05-1705/analysis.md) and follows the [strategy](strategy.md): it replicates X on fresh seeds and asks whether INPUT/GT mass or the conditional remainder suffices. Reusing old X runs would weaken it; decoding only final solvers cannot replace it. I would retain both tasks and all four arms, without adding an initialization/mutation sweep or a fifth arm.

Before approval:

- **Correct “carries the gain.”** With time ratio X÷arm, an upper bound below 1.5 does not establish that the arm is at most 1.5× slower than X. An arm three times slower has ratio 1/3 and can pass. Require the **lower** bound of X÷arm to exceed 1/1.5, or the upper bound of arm÷X to be below 1.5, alongside resolved improvement over U. Freeze the direction and margin before running.
- **Decide component sufficiency, not G1 versus G2 causally.** IG also suppresses all other ops, while R changes constants and useful aggregators as well as irrelevant ops. Even on max>2, these contrasts do not isolate junk suppression from other remainder effects. An IG win establishes that the IG intervention suffices; it does not establish increased partial-scaffold supply “without supplying whole solvers,” since IG's exact-solver sampling rate is unmeasured. Keep that mechanism open rather than adding a costly sampling study to this run. Likewise, 0/100 training-perfect sum>2 proxies excludes that observed perfect-proxy route, not every useful non-perfect intermediate. Shortcut presence and first training-perfect time cannot establish an ancestral stepping stone; the proposal's descriptive-logging caveat should also govern its outcome decisions.
- **Specify unresolved outcomes explicitly.** Failure to resolve U÷X is not evidence that the old effect was noise. Failure of an arm to pass the corrected carry gate is not evidence it cannot carry the gain. Include both arms carrying, neither improving despite X replicating, and intervals too wide to decide. A replicated X with inconclusive IG/R comparisons leaves the mechanism unresolved, not demonstrated “diffuse.” Stop and park the unresolved question as the strategy requires.

**Size and cheaper work.** Eight hundred runs with the inherited cap is reasonable given 1705's 20-minute runtime and light censoring. The risk is precision at the new 1.5× margin, not queue capacity: 1705's 100-pair sum matched÷hand interval was 0.61–1.57. Before fixing n, cheaply use the saved paired times to assess expected precision under a true ratio near one and the intended multiplicity correction; this is a planning diagnostic, not a new experiment or a guarantee of power. Do not repeat 1705's pilot. The least informative plausible result is X replicating while both component intervals straddle the carry boundary; more milestone logging will not resolve that comparison.
