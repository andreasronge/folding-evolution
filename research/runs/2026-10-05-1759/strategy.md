The newest result is a narrow positive for fitting bias. Fitting token frequencies on two thresholds increased exact-solver sampling on a held-out threshold by 4.9×/8.9×; evolution with those frozen vectors reached its median exact solve about 4× sooner than uniform. A simple hand-set scaffold performed comparably. Family specificity in evolutionary speed remains unresolved against the chosen 2× bar (estimates 1.66×/1.80×). The other family's fit already sped evolution 3.3×/1.9× despite sampling ratios of only 1.02×/1.33×. Sources: [1558 analysis](../2026-10-05-1558/analysis.md), [1705 analysis](../2026-10-05-1705/analysis.md).

This does not yet show that a developmental map evolves to learn a task family. The intervention fitted frequencies externally, then changed initialization and mutation together under a fixed decoder. Transfer changed only a threshold constant, with one fitted vector per family. The reported speed-up also excludes fitting cost. Heritable map adaptation, richer transfer and an overall compute advantage remain open.
