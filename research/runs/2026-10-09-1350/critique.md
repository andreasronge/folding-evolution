This is an affordable test of whether pre-solve material supplies useful fragments with C held fixed, and its narrow acquisition relevance justifies the run if the following notes are addressed in plan.md.

1. **note — Feasible, but state solve-count scenarios.** The probe supplies credible library yield: 32/32 in all 16 corpora, with 331–359 source tapes and at least 11 distinct searches per retained fragment. [1036](../2026-10-09-1036/analysis.md) supplies the missing search baseline: W solved 3,643/4,096 (88.9%), F 3,719 (90.8%), C 3,566 (87.1%). Those rates make this a well-populated comparison, not a near-universal-cap experiment. They are scenarios for E/W_E, not measured rates for the new arms. Include expected counts and a slower/harmful-E timing scenario in the plan. The 85-minute arithmetic is correct; 98 minutes with margin and 180 minutes of queue timeouts are plausible. Freeze a representative smoke roster across both families and cells, use only timing for admission, and include replays and full extraction/validation in preparation cost. The two-second probe is not the full prepare price.

2. **note — Worth running at this scope; retain the staged decision.** Existing partial-context results do not answer whether short active windows retain useful material that a full-tape fit dilutes. E versus its own length/start-matched W_E is the relevant intervention; rerunning F/W or adding more exact-source precision would answer less. The strongest cheaper alternative is the strategy's roughly 3–4-hour suffix-preserving point-edit test of W's advantage. That would identify an operator mechanism, but would not test availability of useful pre-solve fragments. Reusing the archives and historical references makes this source test reasonably competitive. A complete early-data pipeline would be more decisive about acquisition cost, but changes both decoder and library and is priced at another 6–9 hours. Do not skip the intervening strategy review. Charge the extra partial collection separately from the exact-solver corpus shared by C and W; a geometric penalized-cost ratio is not an end-to-end repayment calculation.

3. **note — Pin the extractor, including a discrepancy with the concept plan.** The probe ranks by source-cell count and then raw occurrence count (`len(p)`); distinct source searches (`len(srcs)`) are reported but do not determine ranking. Multiple positions and checkpoints from one search can therefore raise a fragment's rank. The concept plan instead asks to count recurrence across distinct searches. Resolve this explicitly before scoring: either retain the old occurrence-ranking heuristic and describe its weighting honestly, or freeze a distinct-search ranking and re-report the resulting libraries. This does not invalidate E/W_E, but “prepare re-derives these libraries” must identify one algorithm. Also pin activity inputs, duplicate treatment, training-cell padded-solver exclusion, and empty-library fallback laws. Revalidate checkpoint-before-first-solve and non-exactness rather than relying only on the archive label. Knockout activity means output influence, not beneficial contribution.

4. **note — Name the result as a procedure comparison, not a join mechanism.** A win would show that this externally extracted pre-solve library helps on then-addition under exact-solver C. It would not show a solver-free system, selection acquiring a map, or fresh-bank transfer. E/F changes source allocation, content and length law; E/W_E lacks a marginal-content control. Thus E near F cannot identify shared reducer/add syntax as the cause, and E near W_E cannot identify missing gate joins as the cause. Keep those as hypotheses even if the expected ordering appears. On any historical replay failure, demote historical W as well as F/C; the newly paired E/W_E remains interpretable. Before investing in stage two, inspect E/C and W_E/W to check whether a relative E/W_E win also has practical value against the existing procedures.

5. **note — The likely unresolved outcome needs an explicit precision caveat.** With half-width ×1.08, a point of 1.05 gives approximately [0.97, 1.13]: neither benefit nor exclusion of a worthwhile increment, leaving the main decision unsettled. At the same precision, rule 3 requires a point below about 1.019; much of the stated 1.0–1.10 expectation therefore routes to “unresolved.” This is acceptable for a first bounded assay, but do not sell it as likely to close the source question. E's between-corpus variance is unmeasured, and the 12-seed fallback further weakens precision. State both-size scenarios, define the speed-ratio direction explicitly, and make the four rules ordered (harm also satisfies rule 3). Price resolution from observed variance without assuming that more seeds remove genuine corpus heterogeneity. An unresolved E/F contrast is not recovery/equality; no outcome here cleanly settles the join explanation.

6. **note — Prior-work positioning is substantially right; correct the bibliographic detail.** Repeated-pattern analysis, cross-run libraries and ARL are appropriate neighbors, and the contribution is a controlled boundary test in this representation, not a new learning paradigm. [Burlacu et al. (2015)](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/building-blocks-identification-based-on-subtree-sample-counts-for/) count recombination samples included in surviving offspring, not simply archived substring frequency. [Rosca (1995)](https://cdn.aaai.org/Symposia/Fall/1995/FS-95-01/FS95-01-011.pdf) discovers subroutines using fitness differences and activation; this experiment does not implement that adaptive credit mechanism. The cited Langdon–Banzhaf journal paper, [*Repeated Patterns in Genetic Programming*](https://www0.cs.ucl.ac.uk/staff/W.Langdon/ftp/papers/langdon_2005_NC.pdf), was published in 2008; the distinct 2005 paper is [*Repeated Patterns in Tree Genetic Programming*](https://www.cs.mun.ca/~banzhaf/papers/eurogp_repeated05.pdf). No missing closer precedent identified that changes the run's value.
