**Current line versus a different mechanism.** I searched the literature for this
review. [Salustowicz and Schmidhuber, *Probabilistic incremental program evolution*
(1997)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10021756/) generates programs from an adaptive
distribution and updates it from successful search. Our corpus fitting is a related
external distribution update, with a separate fragment procedure and frozen reuse.
It is not the same algorithm and is not evidence of inherited decoder evolution.

The owner's alternative is indirect selection on inherited variation parameters.
[Stephens et al., *Self-adaptation in evolving systems*
(1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9847423/) studies encoded mutation/crossover
probabilities whose reproductive consequences permit adaptation. That mechanism puts
credit through descendants, rather than through an external corpus estimator. Its
importance to the core question is high; the repository's negative frozen-bias result
means another mutation-noise trial has little support. A changed inheritance or
exposure mechanism needs a concrete selectable signal, not just more time.

The positive reason to continue the fitted line is **feedback under scarce sources**.
On the older bank, a large exact-source feedback refit beat a fresh G4 refit 1.33×
[1.26, 1.40] on withheld cells ([21](../../questions/10-compositional-map-transfer/21-iterated-solver-corpus/question.md)).
Partial-program feedback was much weaker and uneven across families. Neither test
started from the new cheap C4+F4 procedure. My inference is that source feedback has
enough prior evidence to test whether it survives this bottleneck; it could instead
reinforce missing syntax. This is a boundary and acquisition-policy question with a
useful answer in one cycle, not a prediction of success supplied by the literature.
