This is a feasible, useful boundary test of sparse adaptive acquisition, with interpretation and accounting notes to address in plan.md.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; make the scoring roster unambiguous.** The collection pilot's 117/128 solves predicts about 936/1,024 new solvers, or 14.6 per build; adding the old 9.6 gives the stated roughly 24 versus 19 for static acquisition. These are forecasts, not guarantees, especially for the six empty-cell observations. At 4.5 worker-seconds, collection needs about 77 worker-minutes; eight wall-minutes is plausible at roughly ten effective workers. [1743's analysis](../2026-10-09-1743/analysis.md) confirms 8,192 scoring searches in 104 minutes and 85.9% sparse versus 90.8% full-F solves. Those rates are reasonable evaluation anchors (about 3,519–3,719 solves per 4,096-row arm), not measurements of A8/S8. However, “16 seeds per build-block” would require 32,768 searches. The intended 8,192-row roster is 16 corpora × 4 blocks × 16 cells × **4 seeds per block** × 2 arms; the 12-seed fallback has three per block and 6,144 rows. State this explicitly, retain the historical seed/block assignment, and admit using measured final-arm timing with margin before efficacy inspection. Archive the collection pilot's seed manifest and output for reproducibility. The stated preparation and queue allowances are plausible with the reused runner.

2. **note — Keep the primary speed decision separate from economic usefulness.** An upper bound below 1.10 establishes no worthwhile *search-speed increment over S8 at this threshold*, not “feedback is not worth it”: A8 could search equally fast, cost much less to acquire, and beat keeping C4+F4. Conversely, A8 beating S8 does not establish that either is worth acquiring. Retain the primary comparison, but report the already-proposed arithmetic A + N·S curves and uncertainty against S8, the retained seed and full F regardless of its outcome. An unresolved σ is not evidence that A8 “matches S8.” Label any cheaper-acquisition observation separately; economic preference needs the total-cost comparison at a stated reuse horizon. These are interpretation fixes, requiring no extra arm or formal equivalence test.

3. **note — Worth running, with a real risk of an unresolved policy decision.** The contribution is whether an initially sparse, incomplete fitted bias can improve its own acquisition, beyond simply doubling the source attempts. The large-corpus feedback result does not settle this boundary. A static eight/sixteen-attempt sweep is the strongest immediate alternative, but S8 already supplies its first useful point; a bare-C4 ablation would locate losses without deciding the acquisition policy. Reusing static sources and historical references is already the cheaper design. No clearly cheaper equally decisive replacement is apparent. The predicted interval [1.05, 1.26] crosses 1.10: if A8 versus the seed and the cost curves are also unresolved, we will remain unable to choose an acquisition policy despite learning yields. Show the resolution implied by 16 versus 12 seeds in plan.md; do not equate runtime admission with adequate precision or automatically top up afterward. Four nested builds remain acquisition replication within 16 corpus units, not 64 independent corpora.

4. **note — Prior-work attribution and scope are appropriate.** [PIPE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10021756/) is an appropriate distribution-update precedent; [DreamCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381) supplies the search/library-learning analogy. Here literal fragment reuse and a batched external decoder fit do not implement DreamCoder's abstraction learning. The addition is a priced sparse-source boundary comparison, not a new class of learning or inherited map evolution. I found no missing precedent that changes that assessment. Preserve the fixed pooling, equal-cell fitting mass, failures and empty-cell fallbacks; freeze disjoint continuation keys and new collection seeds before collection. All reused banks are development banks: this design supports excluded-composition performance under this policy, not fresh-bank transfer.
