This is the best bounded use of the last slot: the reproductive veto directly tests access to the exact max>2 intermediate, and the uniform control tests whether that access contributes disproportionately to R's advantage.

- **Choice of experiment.** The revision fixes the main problem in [1945](../2026-10-05-1945/critique.md): neither opcode redistribution nor a between-arm training-case change can explain the veto contrast. Four cells are justified; dropping uniform would leave a generic benefit of shortcuts indistinguishable from an explanation of R's gain. Ancestry counts alone would be cheaper but less decisive. Forced-admit sets make detection economical, and C1 appropriately gates extrapolation from the earlier natural-set result. I do not see a cheaper experiment with the same causal reach.

- **What it establishes.** A positive P_R and interaction identify a benefit of allowing this phenotype to reproduce in this harness. They do not by themselves prove that solver lineages pass through it: population composition and mating opportunities can mediate the benefit. The parent diagnostic helps, but a negative immediate-parent count does not exclude an earlier shortcut ancestor. Likewise, row 2 bounds the net median-speed cost of removing this exact phenotype; alternative routes may compensate, and approximate max-like intermediates remain. Keep those limits when closing the question. The proposal's restricted negative and mutually exclusive outcome labels are improvements.

- **Outcomes that leave the residual unexplained.** Failure of C1, an unresolved interaction, or similar substantial veto penalties in U and R would leave R's extra gain unexplained. Little exposure before solving would also make a null uninformative about max-like routes more broadly, though informative about the exact phenotype's contribution here. A pilot-only stop gives a feasibility result, not evidence against G3. These are acceptable stopping outcomes for this final slot.

- **Size.** A separate 50-seed pilot followed by 600 or 800 four-cell replicates is reasonable, with no extension. In the implementation plan, resample whole four-cell seed records for I, retain the inherited censored-median safeguards, and freeze the effect-injection procedure before examining the pilot. Identity before exposure does not guarantee high correlation after exposure; do not build that optimistic assumption into power. In particular, injected alternatives must respect the fact that unexposed pairs cannot acquire a veto effect. Report the probability of the complete route decision (C1, P_R and I passing together), as well as the specified marginal powers. Stage 2 should also require a conservative runtime estimate to fit the remaining three-hour deadline; deadline-truncated runs are infrastructure missingness, not cap-censored failures.

- **Implementation gate.** The proposed all-fail row plus fitness −1 is only an example, not a guaranteed exclusion mechanism. With fewer eligible individuals than elite slots, or all eligible case rows also all-fail, vetoed programs could still be selected. Explicit eligibility filtering for both parents and elites, including a one-eligible-individual case, is the safer implementation. Preserve the ordinary path exactly when no veto applies and verify the stated identity invariant. These are plan/code-review checks, not reasons to change the experiment.

- **Parked questions.** [09(c)](../../questions/01-map-bias/09-generic-bias-speedup/question.md) is met and addressed by the strategy. No ignored trigger is evident for 02, 04 or 07; 08 needs an actual result identifying useful heritable content, not merely this proposal. Keep the promised stop after this experiment, including an unresolved result.
