**What the program has learned.**

- **Supply, discovery and establishment are different.** Root 01 found that random
  frequency predicts easy tasks and is consistent with folding's fixed-target
  advantage, but does not generally predict evolutionary speed. Cheap joins explained
  the tested composition advantage. Mixing between lineages blocked rare seeded shared
  forms, whereas self-mating allowed discovery and establishment. Natural single-copy
  fate and B-helper arrival remain unresolved. These results do not establish a general
  advantage for folding or modularity ([root 01](../../questions/01-map-bias/question.md)).
- **Evolution can acquire transferable token bias.** Outer-selected token multipliers
  improve fresh search about 2× on withheld compositions, with benefits from both
  starting programs and ongoing decoder use. A useful training-family preference was
  not resolved. The tested contextual selection procedures added no reproducible useful
  increment; calibrated continuation gave C/T 0.967× [0.871, 1.073]. This bounds those
  procedures, not contextual learning ([root 10](../../questions/10-compositional-map-transfer/question.md)).
- **Externally fitted context transfers beyond a token fit.** Exact-solver context C
  versus the restricted token fit T was 3.11× on comparison-gate training cells, 2.60×
  on its protected holdouts, and **2.12× [1.86, 2.41] on one fresh then-addition bank**.
  The cross-shape shrinkage is resolved; its cause is not. One alphabet, finite domain
  and two gates on that fresh bank limit generality. All these banks are now development
  data ([1548](../2026-10-08-1548/analysis.md)).
- **Three replacements did not reproduce C.** On then-addition, C beats its pooled
  frequency projection K by 2.48× and its positional projection Q by 2.41×; independent
  positional draws P are worse still. Recoding Q to match C's mean mutation width, while
  preserving Q's entire uniform-prior program distribution and starting tapes, made
  search **1.17× [1.09, 1.25] slower**. Stronger random recoding hurt more. Thus the
  tested undirected coupling is harmful at fixed supply. C's conditional content and
  structured variation remain unseparated: the intervention changes correlations and
  crossover effects too ([0306](../2026-10-09-0306/analysis.md),
  [0537](../2026-10-09-0537/analysis.md)). The latest queue's failure mark is a final
  plotting error after complete results; it is not missing efficacy data.
- **Acquisition remains the main gap.** Partial-program context improves on G4 by about
  1.62× on training cells. Its feedback increment is modest, F/O 1.18× [1.01, 1.37],
  resolved on BE and unresolved on PA; improvement over retaining the first fit is
  unresolved. Root 23's inherited frequencies establish no useful frozen bias under
  the tested rule: uniform/inherited cost 0.33× on sum and 0.73× on max, with max's
  upper bound 1.06×. Linkage helps max relative to shuffled ancestry without establishing
  usefulness over uniform. Neither finding warrants another unchanged round
  ([2116](../2026-10-08-2116/analysis.md), [1046](../2026-10-08-1046/analysis.md)).

**Current line against different mechanisms.** I searched the literature for this
review. [Salustowicz and Schmidhuber, *Probabilistic incremental program evolution*
(1997)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10021756/) updates a program-generating
distribution using successful programs. Our corpus/feedback line likewise changes
probabilities over fixed primitives through external fitting. Another round could improve
that procedure but would leave the representation and inheritance questions largely intact.

[Ellis et al., *DreamCoder* (2020 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381) learns
symbolic abstractions together with a neural search guide. The fragment candidate takes
only the narrower idea of learning reusable executable units. It extracts literal stack
blocks and makes their coordinated introduction available during variation; it does not
implement DreamCoder, parameterized functions or its neural guide. This is mechanistically
different from changing next-token probabilities. The paper motivates the candidate;
it supplies no evidence of a benefit in this harness.

The positive case is **a possible gain beyond C**, not merely that its simpler controls
failed. A short previous-token table cannot explicitly preserve an entire multi-token
computation as one proposed edit. The saved training solvers provide potential material:
[strategy 0537](../2026-10-09-0537/strategy.md) observed recurring blocks, but did not
establish their active contribution. Testing whether such units help can redirect the
next acquisition mechanism toward a small reusable repertoire, or rule out this concrete
investment. It also faces strong contrary evidence: root 01's cheap-join explanation,
the adequacy of short context for substantial transfer, and the latest harm from
undirected wider edits. Hence the C benchmark and a joint-content control are essential.

This remains **external library extraction**, not inherited map evolution. I accept one
bounded representation test because it can change the acquisition target. It should not
become a sequence of increasingly elaborate externally supplied targets without such a
decision.

**Next question for the steward.** Do executable fragments learned only from training
solvers make fresh search usefully cheaper than fitted C, and does retaining their joint
content matter beyond matched block-level token supply? Answer first on training cells,
with independent source corpora and fresh populations. The
[updated plan](../../plans/learned-executable-fragments.md) provides the implementation
direction; the steward supplies the design, practical effect, fixed size and primary
comparison. A win only against a disrupted block control is insufficient. Equal edit
span does not guarantee equal realized token changes, and re-encoding can itself change
latent variation. Preserve these distinctions when interpreting a practical gain over C.

Useful improvement over C with interpretable content controls earns consideration of
frozen-library reuse on excluded compositions. A tightly small or harmful effect ends
this extractor/operator's expansion. A broad interval needs a resolution price. Success
on these reused banks would not be new-bank transfer or show that evolution acquired
the library. No outcome automatically earns a larger library or another learning round.
