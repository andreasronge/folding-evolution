This is a feasible, bounded intervention on a replicated saving that can inform the next acquisition design, provided its interpretation stays conditional on the particular replacement law.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; finish the accounting in plan.md.** [1228 analysis](../2026-10-11-1228/analysis.md) gives R-all 576/576, R-tree 574/576 and R-S 572/576 solves, with the quoted 2.14/5.31/6.39 worker-seconds per job. Include those counts explicitly. The audit's 177/177 supported replacements in 465 draws (maximum 16) makes construction plausible. The proposed 3,456 jobs cost about 15,944 worker-seconds at those rates, or 27.4 minutes at 9.7 workers; the 70-minute summed timeout and roughly 4–4.5-hour total are reasonable. However, R-S speed is a pessimistic scenario, not a worst-case bound for R-hx. Specify that timeout or incomplete construction produces an incomplete experiment, not an analysis of whichever jobs finished fastest.

2. **note — Freeze the construction details and retain the precision caveat.** Plan the per-entry draw limit, independent construction/search seed namespaces, four realizations and D625/VM validation before scoring; preserve duplicate slots without semantic re-deduplication. A single-input output is computationally trivial, but its tree can still supply useful subtrees through subsequent variation: avoid treating it as evolutionarily inert. The same-library split check measures search noise, not between-build variation in the intervention effect. Eight repeats and 24 build clusters are defensible for the anticipated Q ≈ 1.5–2, but do not promise an upper bound of 1.15–1.20. An interval crossing 1.25, or retention of Q without useful R-hx/R-tree performance, leaves the main decision unresolved; the proposed return to strategy is appropriate.

3. **note — Narrow the outcome language in plan.md.** Retention means “preassembled helper-valued donor entries are unnecessary for this saving at the registered resolution,” not “exact helper computations are not needed”: ordinary evolution may reconstruct them, as the proposed solver census recognizes. Loss establishes the benefit of the intact entries relative to mostly projection-valued replacements, conditional on this library and search regime. It does not establish unique min/max necessity or justify making intact computations a universal requirement for future acquisition. Keep the proposal's explicit caveat in the outcome and next-action text, not only its expectation paragraph. No fresh-bank claim is available.

4. **note — Worth running, with the right precedent.** [Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico's Run Transferable Libraries](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-24855-2_63) is an appropriate closest technique: knowledge acquired in GP runs supplies later runs. This adds a controlled mechanism comparison, not a new library-learning algorithm. Semantic backpropagation is related context; this uniform donor law does not perform target-directed semantic inversion. Relative to the proposed 7–9-hour fresh-family census, this cheaper intervention can change what that census should measure. Deleting helper slots is simpler but changes material and draw probabilities; another coverage analysis remains observational. I see no clearly cheaper equally decisive design for this narrow question. A large loss would still leave helper-versus-other-nontrivial-computation unresolved, but that limitation does not erase the identified contribution.
