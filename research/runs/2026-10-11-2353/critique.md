This is a feasible, decision-relevant test of a supplied rebinding rule on acquired content, provided the plan keeps its mechanism claims narrower than its procedure comparison.

1. **note — Feasibility is supported.** [2150's analysis](../2026-10-11-2150/analysis.md) reports R-tree/R-all/R-gate solves of 1,610/1,657/1,728 out of 1,728, first-attempt rates of 27.0%/35.5%/65.1%, and 11.56/8.80/3.18 worker-seconds per job. These are useful hit rates on a genuinely difficult tier. The proposed grid is 3,456 jobs per library arm plus 576 per reference arm, totaling 8,064. At the measured arm-specific rates and 7.98 workers, approximately 145 minutes is credible if R-bind costs what R-all does. The constructor fits the existing uniform donor-list interface; preserving duplicates preserves original-entry draw mass. In plan.md, make the reference grid explicit (24 independent seed groups × 24 cells, not 24 jobs copied across builds), state the timing-only smoke size and fixed r-selection rule, and include preparation timeouts within the 230-minute queue allowance. R-bind's runtime remains an assumption until smoke validation.

2. **note — Worth running, with an appropriate precedent and bounded contribution.** The comparison can choose whether to pursue argument reuse before collecting more literal examples. The best immediate alternative, relaxing recurrence, raises helper support but roughly triples entries and lowers helper draw share from 23% to 13% in the saved-corpus audit; it is not clearly more decisive or cheaper overall. Another unchanged-library confirmation would change less. Run-transferable libraries are the right general precedent; the already-cited [Murphy, Ryan & Howard (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1109/FBIT.2007.101), which expands schematic templates by argument count and order, is the closest specific antecedent. [Keijzer, Ryan & Cattolico (2004)](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/papers/3103/31030531.pdf) already treats reuse of acquired functions across runs. The contribution here is a controlled search-cost test of uniform input rebinding with original-entry weights retained, against both literal donors and a supplied prior; it is not a new general library-learning mechanism. No additional arm or abstraction engine is needed for that question.

3. **note — Preserve the symmetry argument, narrow attribution.** The complete permutation roster makes roster-averaged needed-helper draw mass equal, but does not hold each search's donor content or input supply fixed. All non-helper entries change too. State the search equivalence under input permutation as distributional, not identical-seed trajectory equality; the production-VM renaming check validates expression semantics. Replace “resolved harm, meaning dose matters” with “harm from uniform rebinding, consistent with dilution or other rebound-content effects.” A stratum-0 loss likewise does not isolate non-helper harm. “Only 8.6 helper types” conflates literal bindings with permutation classes: the audit finds all 24 rebound helpers in 23/24 builds, so the original binding count does not itself explain a residual R-gate gap. These are interpretation corrections, not reasons to expand the experiment.

4. **note — Freeze unambiguous decisions and conditional precision.** The rules can overlap: an interval such as [0.86, 0.89] satisfies both “material gain” and “small, none or harm.” Specify precedence in plan.md, or report separately that a >10% saving is established and a >15% saving excluded, with one routing decision. Preserve the numerical thresholds. Archive the pseudo-contrast calculation and describe 0.08–0.09 as a forecast: shifting existing builds does not measure R-bind-specific build heterogeneity, and repeats cannot necessarily remove it. A broad interval spanning useful improvement and little benefit—especially near the forecast true ratio 0.90—would leave the next representation choice unresolved. It must not become evidence for equality or for a particular bottleneck. The stated return to strategy without automatic top-up is appropriate.
