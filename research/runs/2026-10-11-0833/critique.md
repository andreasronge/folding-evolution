This is a feasible, useful test of acquisition from a second source family, provided plan.md keeps procedure portability separate from mechanism attribution and unresolved native performance.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; calibrate the actual sources.** The design has 768 acquisition jobs and 9,216 scoring jobs. [0024](../2026-10-11-0024/analysis.md) supports the stated TS anchor: 6,672 evaluations, 0.49 worker-s, 748/768 first-attempt and 768/768 eventual solves. But these were TS **target** cells, not the four acquisition sources; likewise the pseudo-library probe used target solvers. Treat 0.21 M evaluations/build and 13× cheaper acquisition as projections. [0652](../2026-10-11-0652/analysis.md) actually measured B/C R-tree 6.48 worker-s, R-lib 2.37, R-size 5.24, with 1,724/1,728 R-tree solves and 1,728/1,728 R-lib solves. Roughly 70 minutes at sustained concurrency ten is plausible, with ample scoring-timeout headroom. In plan.md specify a timing-only stage gate on actual TS-source searches and representative new-library scoring, with stop/report behavior if the envelope fails. Rebuild the worktree's Rust extension and verify canonical TS programs before accepting calibration: the log records a discarded incompatible-VM run.

2. **note — Worth one run; no clearly superior cheaper substitute.** All successful tree libraries came from one DG roster. Keeping the destination and extractor fixed while changing sources addresses a real limitation of the core adapted-bias claim. Native TS distinguishes destination failure from a broader limitation, while the TS-specific size control prevents importing DG's content attribution. The strongest alternative is a fresh unfamiliar-content bank: it tests a different, important boundary but cannot establish that acquisition works from another source family and needs new bank validation. A hand-set pair-sum arm would be cheaper to construct but tests mechanism or acquisition necessity, not recipe portability. The present size is reasonable for the expected 0.4–0.6 ratio; historical precision does not guarantee resolution near 0.80 or with more variable TS libraries. Keep the fixed size rather than extending automatically.

3. **note — Closest technique is named correctly.** [Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico's Run Transferable Libraries (2004)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-24855-2_63) explicitly transfers knowledge between GP runs. Frozen recurring-subtree mutation donors are appropriately described as external acquisition and a boundary test of that established idea. The contribution is source-roster portability and measured acquisition repayment in this harness, not a new learning mechanism or inheritance result. No additional precedent is necessary to justify this experiment.

4. **note — Remove the skeleton inference from plan.md.** “R-TS ≥ 0.8 with R-DG ≈ 0.4” would not show that DG skeletons rather than pair-sum supply carried B/C. The source change also changes which pair sums recur, their sampling probabilities, library sizes, labels, solve yield and insertion behavior. [0652](../2026-10-11-0652/analysis.md) explicitly leaves pair-sum supply a hypothesis. Replace that surprise interpretation with “the unchanged TS acquisition did not reproduce the DG procedure's B/C saving; the responsible content remains unidentified.” The read-only observation of no seven-node predicate applies to those pseudo-builds, not all future TS builds.

5. **note — Make the unresolved outcomes explicit.** In the native-TS reading, failure of the upper bound to fall below 0.80 is not evidence that the recipe lacks useful native benefit. Reserve that conclusion for an interval excluding the specified worthwhile saving; otherwise report native usefulness unresolved. Apply the reliability check before calling native benefit useful. Similarly, passing the B/C primary with an unresolved R-TS/R-sizeTS contrast supports complete-procedure usefulness, not a learned-content advantage. A ratio straddling 0.80, native uncertainty, or a failed reliability guard leaves the intended routing question unsettled: report the decision-relevant resolution price. A tightly bounded saving below 20% is still a possible saving. Keep every conclusion conditional on these development rosters; no fresh-bank transfer claim is available.
