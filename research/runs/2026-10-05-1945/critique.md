The question deserves the last slot, but crossing two nonspecific interventions does not isolate the max>2 route, and the proposed negative result cannot rule it out.

- **The cross does not cancel the side effects.** R−M changes 19 other op probabilities; B changes which partial programs lexicase rewards. Those changes can interact without any lineage passing through max>2. For example, the extra REDUCE_ADD in R−M may help more on B's small-valued separating cases than on A. That could produce D_A > D_B without the proposed stepping stone. Applying the same cases to both vectors does not make their selection effects equal. Likewise, more REDUCE_ADD need not monotonically improve evolutionary speed. The strategy explicitly requires separating these effects; the current cross does not satisfy that requirement.

- **The negative is especially weak.** D_A upper < 1.25 bounds the *net effect of this vector edit*, not the contribution of max>2. Improved alternative routes could compensate for lost shortcut supply. REDUCE_MAX also remains available at uniform frequency, and B still permits max>2 intermediates. Neither intervention removes the route. Even a precise null would leave G3 unresolved. The outcome rows also overlap: D_A could be significantly above 1 but below 1.25 while I is positive, awarding both “supported” and “ruled out.”

- **A more direct alternative:** use four cells, U/R × ordinary/shortcut-veto reproduction, on the same forced-admit A sets. In veto cells, programs computing exactly max>2 on all 10,000 inputs cannot be parents in either crossover role or survive through elitism; keep opcode vectors, cases and variation unchanged. Verify exact sum>2 before applying the veto. At `31d4408`, `first_exact` already exhaustively evaluates training-perfect candidates; on A this includes every max>2 program, so cache its phenotype as well as exactness. Freeze handling of an entirely vetoed population and measure implementation overhead before committing the queue. Compare the veto penalty within R and its interaction with U: this directly tests whether access to that reproductive intermediate helps, and whether it contributes disproportionately to R's advantage. Record ancestral passage through the shortcut to distinguish a stepping stone from other population effects. This remains a test of the exact max>2 phenotype, not every approximate max-based route; a null must retain that limit.

- **Size and stopping:** 600–800 pairs and a bounded queue are reasonable in scale, but the proposed precision check powers D_A while the decisive route claim requires I. Check power for the actual primary interaction, including pairing and censoring, before freezing n. Four cells would need 2,400 runs at n = 600 instead of 3,000, though veto overhead needs measurement. An unresolved interaction, failure to reproduce R's gain on A, or comparable veto penalties in U and R would leave the explanation of R's advantage open. Do not spend the last slot merely to estimate a well-powered opcode effect.

I find no ignored trigger for 02, 04 or 07. 08's condition requires 09 to reopen *and show* what a heritable bias needs to carry; proposing this test alone does not establish that. Keep the one-experiment stop, but do not close the mechanism question on the proposed compensated-null result.
