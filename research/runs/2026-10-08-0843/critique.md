1. **note — Feasibility is plausible; re-time the changed process as proposed.** The roster is correct: 80 acquisitions, 2,560 learned searches and 768 reference searches. Recomputing the old timing files gives about 31.7 minutes of learned scoring and 8.8 minutes of references on ten fully utilized workers; 80 × 260 seconds adds 34.7 minutes. These are four scoring seeds per target and one acquisition per arm, not reliable new-law hit-rate estimates. In plan.md report revised per-family/arm acquisition times, first solves/48, scheduled counts and verifier time. The old maximum verifier cost is not an upper bound after changing the population trajectory. State the exact timeout projection and return-to-strategy rule if cutting reference seeds still exceeds three hours; preserve the 120-minute preparation limit.

2. **note — “Unchanged except for fixed-duration episodes” cannot include the old analysis.** At `25f929e`, `inherited_bias.py:analyze` hardcodes 16 acquisitions, uses broken/inherited as primary, pools families for its verdict, and reports inherited/reference ratios. Update all four explicitly for the proposed 20 acquisitions, uniform/inherited primary and separate family decisions. Preserve shared scoring-seed resampling across every vector and acquisition pairing where the seed construction supplies it. The old code uses only the shared 16 reference seeds for contrasts; its extra 48 seeds are descriptive. Say whether that remains the plan. If so, the cheapest harmless saving is to drop those extra descriptive seeds (and optionally the historical fit), rather than reduce acquisition replication. The quoted precision is conditional on an assumed between-run SD and omits any additional shared-seed uncertainty; it is not a guaranteed interval width.

3. **note — Correct the scaffold ratio and continuation wording.** The displayed practical-value ratio is scaffold cost ÷ inherited cost, but “scaffold advantage lower bound < 2×” appears to mean inherited cost ÷ scaffold cost. Those are reciprocals: a scaffold five times faster gives the displayed ratio 0.2 and would trivially pass a literal `< 2` check. Define `S = inherited/scaffold` and use its interval consistently. A lower bound below 2 means a twofold scaffold disadvantage is *not established*, not that inherited is within twofold; the latter needs an upper bound below 2. Specify which uncertainty is sufficient to motivate slot 2, and retain the strategy review before consuming it. No additional statistical machinery is needed.

4. **note — Worth running, with a clear limit to what it can teach.** Discarding resident tapes and testing frozen distributions addresses an acquisition route absent from the current evidence. A fresh bank with several covered holdouts is the strongest alternative use of the block, but requires construction and screening; another old-bank corpus refit would mainly refine an already demonstrated external-fitting route. No clearly cheaper experiment answers this inheritance question as directly. Equal generations remove the stopping confound, not differences in realized lineage depth or selection intensity. Both learned arms censored leave linkage unidentified, although solving uniform/scaffold controls can still bound their capped usefulness. A broad interval spanning no gain and 1.5× leaves acquisition unresolved; equal improvements in inherited and broken do not identify drift or the support floor as the cause. Report those outcomes without promoting them to mechanism findings. A modest useful gain far behind the scaffold is informative acquisition, but supports ending expansion as proposed.

5. **note — The closest-technique framing is right.** [Stephens et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9847423/) study indirectly selected inherited variation parameters; [Angeline's *Two Self-Adaptive Crossover Operators for Genetic Programming*](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/1109.003.0009) is an appropriate GP precedent. The [Glickman–Sycara paper](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub2/glickman_matthew_2000_1/glickman_matthew_2000_1.pdf) supports considering premature suppression of variation. The contribution here is token-frequency self-adaptation assessed after a fresh start, with disrupted-linkage and supplied-scaffold controls, not invention of self-adaptation. I found no missing closer precedent that changes the decision. Maintenance after solving is correctly disclosed; trajectories alone will not establish maintenance as its causal explanation.
