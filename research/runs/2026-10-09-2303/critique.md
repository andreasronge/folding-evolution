Preserve all 16 corpus units and equal block weights. The stated precision factors are plausible scenarios, not guarantees for this harder bank. Passing the nine-token screen neither proves 16-token minimality nor excludes shorter 10–15-token solutions.

3. **note — Worth the run; retain the economical fallback.** This directly tests whether development-bank tuning made A8 look more reusable than it is, which can change whether subsequent work carries cheap or full acquisition. Another A8/S8 precision round on then-addition would answer less; the estimated 163-corpus resolution price makes it particularly unattractive. A bare-decoder or fragment intervention would identify components but leave the immediate fresh-target acquisition choice unanswered. No clearly more decisive test is available at the stated cost. For G4/A8, propagate uncertainty from G4 seeds within each fixed cell, sharing each baseline resample across corpus comparisons rather than inventing independent G4 replicates. This needs no extra searches. State explicitly that all intervals condition on the selected target roster. Also distinguish a resolved material A8 loss from “full acquisition earns its cost”: the latter additionally depends on full F being useful against G4 and on the acquisition-plus-search curves at a stated horizon. If retention is bounded but usefulness against G4 fails, report those as separate results rather than merely “otherwise unresolved.” Likewise, a material relative loss when both methods lose to G4 identifies a relative boundary without selecting full F for deployment. Keep the planned return to strategy for these outcomes; do not rescue them with another bank or post-score target filtering.
