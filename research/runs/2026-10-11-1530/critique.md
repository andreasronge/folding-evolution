The frozen-library comparison can cheaply close a specific held-out-combination gap, with plausible throughput and useful controls; clarify the decision and incomplete-run rules in plan.md before scoring.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; make the reliability anchor explicit.** [1228 analysis](../2026-10-11-1228/analysis.md) reports R-tree 574/576 solves, R-all and R-order 576/576 each, and 1,152/1,152 source solves. No acquisition discovery is needed now. The proposed 1,152 units × (5.31 + 2.14 + 0.27) worker-seconds = 8,893 worker-seconds, about 15.3 minutes at the measured 9.7 effective workers. A 90-minute scoring allowance is credible, though protected throughput and precision remain extrapolations. Include those solve counts and retain the six-hour complete-work ceiling. Charge extraction and validation as well as source search: 1228's analysis gives **13.3 worker-seconds/build including preparation**, versus the proposal's 13.1 from acquisition rows.

2. **note — Freeze decision precedence and the reliability denominator.** An interval such as [0.70, 0.78] satisfies both “useful” and “end expansion.” The earlier 1228 plan gave lower > 0.67 precedence; state whether that remains the policy, and distinguish excluding a one-third saving from establishing a smaller saving. Also, 12/1,152 is 1.04%, not 1%. Express the reliability rule as a rate or an exact count deliberately, and scale it for the 576-unit fallback. A failed reliability guard must prevent a useful verdict even with a low cost ratio. These are rule clarifications, not reasons to add statistical machinery.

3. **note — Timeout fallback must be a complete balanced block.** Analyze repeat 0 only if **all 24 builds × 24 cells × three arms** have completed; otherwise return an incomplete result without an efficacy verdict. Flush rows durably and verify exact keys, pairing and caps. Merely taking jobs finished before a timeout preferentially retains fast solves. Make repeat-major execution enforce a completion barrier before repeat 1, rather than relying on submission order. Report the fallback interval honestly; do not assume the two-repeat precision forecast survives.

4. **note — Worth one bounded run, with a narrow contribution.** The result can decide whether the revised acquisition recipe remains a useful reference outside the six cells that informed its revision. A helper-ablation experiment is cheaper (the strategy estimates 2–3 hours) and more causal, but it would leave this generalization gap open; a fresh content-bank experiment would broaden scope but also require new feasibility work. Neither clearly dominates this inexpensive reuse test. Retaining R-order prevents a misleading practical-superiority story. [Run Transferable Libraries](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225170970_Run_Transferable_Libraries_-_Learning_Functional_Bias_in_Problem_Domains) is the right precedent: the contribution is a boundary/replication result for externally extracted, expanded semantic donors, not a new library-learning principle. No missing prior technique changes this design. Keep “held-out combinations of a development bank,” never fresh-bank transfer, as the claim.

5. **note — State what would remain unresolved.** A broad interval crossing both decision thresholds buys little decision resolution; price further precision rather than automatically extending. Even a clean success will not identify helper causality or useful inherited acquisition. Conversely, upper ≥ 0.80 alone does **not** mean the development roster was favourable: sampling uncertainty can produce it without demonstrated attenuation. Report that as failure to establish the desired held-out saving unless a direct development/protected comparison supports shrinkage. Saving queue time by dropping a repeat is possible, but the queue is already cheap relative to agent work; the proposed two repeats are reasonable insurance against an uninformative answer.
