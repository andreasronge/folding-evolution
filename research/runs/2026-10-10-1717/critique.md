The crossed comparison is a worthwhile, plausibly affordable test of acquired family dependence, provided plan.md separates the interaction from useful reciprocal preference and treats precision and TS feasibility as provisional.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible, conditional on preparation.** [1536's analysis](../2026-10-10-1536/analysis.md) supports the quoted costs: 772 worker-s/build, 29.4 s/G4 search, 10.8 s/matched search and 24.0 s/O search. It also supplies the missing numerical hit-rate anchors: 64/384 first-batch and 218/384 adaptive source solves; protected A8 317/384 versus G4 63/384. The proposed count is correct: 1,536 fitted searches plus 768 G4 searches. At 9.8 effective workers, 22–30 worker-s/search gives 86–118 minutes, leaving reasonable room in 140 minutes. TS acquisition, crossed search and the unfinished alias screen remain unmeasured. In plan.md, record their measured development rates, yields, concurrency and full projected cost before admitting scoring; preserve every confirmation build and fallback. Bound screen/clique work explicitly within the preparation timeout. A failure to finish screening is failed admission, not permission to weaken it. Clarify how the 120-minute preparation envelope relates to the 80-minute queue entry.

2. **note — The interaction is useful, but does not cancel every dominating bias.** Only a *constant multiplicative* advantage on both rosters cancels. For example, P_DG = 4 and P_TS = 0.8 gives I = 1.79 while D is better on both families. Retain I as the primary interaction, but show both directional intervals and allow “dominant bias with an interaction” regardless of which I branch fires. Failure of a directional lower bound to exceed 1 means unresolved direction, not demonstrated one-directionality. Likewise, I's upper bound below 1.5 bounds the geometric interaction; it need not exclude a useful smaller reciprocal preference. These are interpretation fixes using the already planned measurements, not reasons to change the experiment.

3. **note — Useful specialization requires the absolute comparisons too.** A reciprocal crossed advantage can arise because each cohort harms the other family, even if neither improves its own family over G4. In plan.md, make the family-specific-acquisition next action conditional on own-family usefulness as well as the directional contrasts; keep acquisition economics separate from geometric search-cost ratios. Mostly capped fitted arms with I near 1 would leave us no wiser about shared useful bias or uncapped difficulty. Poor TS acquisition would establish a boundary of this recipe, not identify why DG acquisition is family-specific. Broad directional intervals should retain an unresolved interpretation and a resolution price.

4. **note — Precision is a scenario, not assured resolution at I = 2.** The stated half-width factors imply a lower endpoint of 2/1.45 = 1.38 to 2/1.30 = 1.54. Thus a point estimate of 2 only sometimes clears 1.5; a point of 1.1 has an upper endpoint of 1.43–1.60. DG's matched-arm build SD alone does not determine uncertainty in the within-build cross-roster difference, especially for the new TS cohort. Correct those planning claims, retain each build's rows across both rosters in resampling, and document how common search seeds are handled without treating the two independently acquired cohorts as paired acquisitions. The fixed 24-build size is defensible for a substantial effect; no automatic enlargement is warranted.

5. **note — Worth running before the nearest alternatives.** This changes the acquisition decision more directly than another G4-only replication or another fresh target shape. A saved-build context-only versus context-plus-fragments comparison is cheaper, but answers component value rather than family dependence; a stronger uninformed baseline answers practical competitiveness. Neither substitutes for this crossed test. Reusing the complete DG cohort is already a substantial economy. Keep the conclusion conditional on these source rosters and target cells: DG is a development bank despite its unused cells, and this is not broad fresh-bank generality or isolation of ADD placement.

6. **note — Prior work is broadly named correctly but incomplete for fragments.** [PIPE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10021756/) is an appropriate distribution-learning precedent; A8 remains an externally fitted decoder plus literal-fragment operator, not PIPE itself or inherited adaptation. Add the closer fragment-transfer precedent, Wild and Porter, [*Multi-Donor Neural Transfer Learning for Genetic Programming* (2022)](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/174982/?template=browse), which extracts code fragments from solved problems for injection into future ones. This run adds a controlled same-alphabet boundary test of the repository's cheap acquisition recipe, not the invention of learned distributions or fragment transfer.
