This is a worthwhile bounded test of inherited bias acquisition, provided plan.md makes the feasibility check operational and distinguishes useful learning from merely beating the shuffled control.

1. **note — Feasibility is plausible, but the quoted evidence is for different targets.** [1705](../2026-10-05-1705/analysis.md) solved 50/50 uniform sum2 and 47/50 uniform max2 searches at 262,144 evaluations; uniform sampling yielded 173 and 69 exact hits per 125M tapes. Neither establishes solve counts or verification cost for sum1/5 and max1/5 under heterogeneous modifiers. The acquisition ceiling is about 6.3M population evaluations per run: about 210 worker-seconds at the quoted 30k/s, making four minutes plausible **before** modifier overhead and expensive shortcut verification. The stated 1.1-hour worst case is not established. In stage 0, time both arms in both families, report solves/48 episodes, generations, verifier time and frozen-search censoring by target, then admit the full fixed roster only if a conservative projection fits the queue. This is a cost gate, not selection for a favourable effect. Validate all four target labels and exact checks: the legacy `evolve_bias.py` hardcodes threshold 2 in several places. Retain the strategy's 120-minute preparation stop.

2. **note — Freeze the shuffle and episode-boundary ordering.** Specify one population permutation per generation versus one per parent draw; these are different processes. Shuffle after the tapes have been generated/evaluated and before selection, including elite assignment, so a modifier cannot retain the fitness association created at a reset. State what happens when generation 0 already solves and whether the final extraction precedes another reset. Test bookkeeping with deliberately distinct modifier rows; identical-row replay alone cannot detect a broken shuffle. The contrast measures persistent program–modifier linkage under imposed resets, bundling initialization and ongoing variation; it does not isolate mutation alone. Record actual modifier-bearing lineage depth: 48 early-stopped episodes need not supply the assumed 2,000 generations.

3. **note — Keep acquisition, usefulness and practical value separate.** Broken/inherited > 1.5 can occur while both are worse than their uniform starting distribution. Call that a linkage advantage, not useful acquisition. Use the already-budgeted uniform comparison to establish the direction of improvement, and pre-state how the scaffold comparison and acquisition break-even cost inform slot 2. A broad interval, both arms near the censoring cap, or negligible modifier exposure leaves the central question unresolved; a tight small-effect interval bounds this particular procedure only. Likewise, both arms improving similarly would not identify drift or the floor as the cause. Save per-family results so a pooled gain cannot imply success in both families.

4. **note — The interval must reflect the shared scoring block.** Sixteen scoring seeds per target are reused across every vector. Resampling acquisition runs alone conditions on that small block and misses uncertainty from seed-by-arm effects. Use a crossed bootstrap of acquisition runs within family and shared seed indices within target, preserving all arm pairing. No extra queue is required. One timing run per arm cannot estimate between-acquisition-run SD; label the 0.3-log SD and ×/÷1.2 precision as assumptions, not stage-0 measurements. Report the endpoint explicitly as capped geometric search cost, with solve counts alongside it.

5. **note — Worth running; prior-work framing is appropriate.** Self-adaptation of inherited strategy parameters is the right comparison: [Stephens et al. (1997)](https://arxiv.org/abs/adap-org/9708002) explicitly encode mutation/crossover probabilities without directly rewarding them. The contribution here is testing an inherited token distribution after discarding its programs, against disrupted linkage and a supplied scaffold; it is a mechanism test, not a new class of adaptation. No missing citation I verified changes that judgment. A fresh multi-holdout bank is the strongest alternative allocation, but this small acquisition test answers the owner's previously untested selection route more directly; another feedback iteration on the existing bank would not. The cheaper improvement is staged admission and analysis of the controls already budgeted, not a larger sweep or an additional arm.
