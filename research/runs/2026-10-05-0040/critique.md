Testing fitted frequencies is a sensible next direction, but the proposed controls and stopping rules can mistake transferable bias for generic dilution, or an uninformative null for evidence against frequency adaptation.

The cheaper, sharper first experiment is the proposed sampling stage with a gate before evolution. Keep the direction; revise these points before spending the full queue:

- **Make family specificity the comparison.** The [question](../../questions/01-map-bias/08-evolve-bias/question.md) defines C as matched and mismatched fits helping equally; the proposal instead uses I-fit ≈ both-fit. A joint fit can learn useful biases for both families, so equality with it does not distinguish generic dilution from transfer. Look for reciprocal specificity in the stage-1 matrix: I-fit beats S-fit on I holdouts, and S-fit beats I-fit on S holdouts, with gains over uniform. Keep both-fit as a useful benchmark, not a veto on A. If the intended claim goes beyond removing irrelevant ops, add a simple, training-defined irrelevant-op suppression control. S-fit need not hurt I below uniform for this comparison to be informative.
- **Freeze a viable task split first.** Resolve the common alphabet and its op meanings before fitting, rather than leaving the families conditional. Choose members using prior evidence or a uniform-only calibration, then freeze them before inspecting fitted holdout performance. Stage 1 already contains held-out-task data; “before stage 2” is too late a boundary for selecting the tasks on which transfer will be judged.
- **Validate fitting before interpreting failure.** Use fresh sampling to verify improvement on fitting tasks, and give the optimizer a bounded evaluation budget and a few independent starts. A failed search, noisy rare-event objective, or improvement only on the ≥0.9 surrogate leaves D unidentified. The task definitions explicitly note strong near-linear AND/OR shortcuts, making that surrogate particularly risky here. A successful static random-sampling fit is a useful feasibility test; its failure cannot rule out weights optimized for evolutionary trajectories.
- **Gate and size stage 2 for a useful effect.** Proceed only with validated fits and tasks that permit a meaningful comparison. Thirty seeds per arm is reasonable for large gains, not for declaring “no effect” from nonsignificance. State a minimum worthwhile gain and report uncertainty on matched-versus-control differences, retaining seed pairing. Ceiling solve rates can still support a speed comparison; all-zero rates with censored times cannot. Use exhaustive checking of the 10,000 four-element intlists for “exact,” as prior work does, rather than equating a finite holdout pass with exactness.

**What would leave us no wiser:** no validated training improvement; zero or very sparse exact counts across fitted sampling cells; every evolutionary arm failing throughout; or differences whose intervals still admit a worthwhile benefit. These are inconclusive, not clean B/C/D outcomes. Parking for budget reasons remains legitimate, but should not imply that decoder changes are necessary.

**Size:** the roughly 2.5-hour cap is reasonable for a pilot, but a fixed 50M tapes in every cell plus all 240 evolutionary runs is premature. Use smaller sampling batches first, extending rare or decision-critical cells, and skip evolution if the gate fails. Include fresh fit validation and exactness checks in the runtime estimate.
