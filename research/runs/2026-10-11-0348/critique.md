This is a feasible, worthwhile test of whether the frozen library improves competent search on a new composition; the researcher should address the runtime, decision-rule and economic-reporting notes in `plan.md`.

1. **note — Feasibility is supported, with a conditional runtime projection.** Recomputing [probe rows](probe/rows.json) gives 30/48 R-tree and 46/48 R-lib solves by 65,536 evaluations. Mean truncated attempt costs are 33,856 and 8,528; a pooled independent-attempt projection gives 54,148 and 8,899 evaluations per restarted job, matching the proposal. Measured aggregate throughput is about 86 and 80 µs/evaluation. Thus 1,728 jobs per arm and roughly 30 minutes scoring at ten workers are plausible. But the probe used two cells and single long attempts, not restarted jobs, and did not measure R-size. Fixed-library/cell heterogeneity can invalidate the pooled restart extrapolation. Stage 0 should price the actual restart wrapper in all three arms across all four development cells, retain all 24 builds, and report projected solve counts and sustained wall time before admitting scoring. The 90-minute gate provides reasonable slack. Full cost is approximately 5–6 hours at expected runtime, nearer seven if implementation, agents and queue all consume their stated allowances; still inside the eight-hour envelope.

2. **note — Worth running; preserve the narrow transfer claim.** The important new belief is whether 0150's development-bank benefit survives a changed target composition with the same libraries and strong baseline. The best alternative is the cheaper DG ingredient comparison (pair sums, predicate-only and input-enriched donors), which could choose what to acquire but cannot answer that transfer question. I agree with testing the complete frozen method first. Eighteen balanced protected cells and all acquisitions buy more than another DG seed top-up; dropping R-size would save time but lose the donor-size reference. Freeze the admitted roster, exclusions, method and artifact hashes before protected scoring. Both probed cells must remain excluded from protection even if screening changes the first-four roster. The disclosure must survive into the result: this is protected-cell confirmation within a deliberately selected new shape, not a wholly unprobed family or broad task transfer.

3. **note — Prior work is named appropriately.** This is external subtree-library fitting and transfer, not a new acquisition mechanism. Of the cited precedents, O'Neill, Al-Sahaf, Xue and Zhang's *Common Subtrees in Related Problems* (2017) is particularly close to the cross-source recurrence rule ([author publication listing](https://homepages.ecs.vuw.ac.nz/~xuebing/Publications.html)); run-transferable libraries are the broader precedent. The contribution here is the controlled boundary test with expanded mutation donors, a restarted comparator, size controls and acquisition accounting. No additional missing precedent is necessary to establish that limited contribution. Neither a positive result nor R-lib beating R-size identifies structure beyond an input/ADD frequency shift.

4. **note — Resolve outcome overlap and separate search benefit from repayment.** An interval such as [0.86, 0.89] satisfies both “Transfers” and “Small or none.” State precedence in `plan.md`, including where a failed reliability guard routes; this is a labeling refinement, not a reason to redesign. The primary ratio excludes acquisition, so call it a search-cost transfer result. For deployment, show acquisition-plus-search costs at stated horizons and bootstrap the saved per-build acquisition costs jointly with target savings rather than treating 2.88 M as an exact constant. Include failed source attempts and extraction time; report an unbounded/no finite repayment interval when savings cross zero. This answers “after charging acquisition” without inventing a universal deployment horizon.

5. **note — State what remains unresolved.** A broad interval spanning both useful savings and little benefit, or a speed gain with an unresolved reliability guard, would not settle expansion and should return a priced resolution to strategy. Even the expected large gain leaves the carrying ingredient unknown. A pooled gain concentrated in the learned predicate's split would support limited reuse but not the predicted benefit across every split; the planned per-split reporting makes that distinction visible. No extra mechanism matrix or general-family inference is needed in this run.
