This is a feasible, useful replacement test that can guide the next acquisition design, provided plan.md limits its causal interpretation and makes the runtime fallback explicit.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; include the solve counts.** [0348's analysis](../2026-10-11-0348/analysis.md) gives B/C R-tree 1,150/1,152 solved and R-lib 1,152/1,152, with first-attempt success across the whole bank 53% and 86%. These support the restart schedule and capped-cost endpoint. The stated B/C means and worker times support roughly 37,000 worker seconds for the proposed grid when R-perm is priced as R-tree, hence about an hour at ten workers. That is a planning estimate, not an upper bound on rearranged-donor cost. Stage 0 should report R-perm solves, capped costs and sustained throughput across the frozen constructions. If four repeats still exceed the scoring allowance, stop as infeasible. For the fallback explicitly change realization assignment to repeat mod 2, keep the first two preselected realizations without performance selection, and reduce all arms together.

2. **note — Worth running as a replacement comparison, not a causal decomposition.** A cheaper sufficient representation of the donor pool would change what to acquire for the core map-bias question. This is a better immediate purchase than another familiar-parts transfer bank; different-source acquisition is the strongest alternative but requires new collection and separation of source and target content. No clearly cheaper test answers the same replacement question with comparable control: pair-only or predicate-only arms change size and primitive supply. Retain the development-bank label and charge acquisition to both library arms. However, replace “a gain from redistributed pairs” with “a gain from the rearranged-library procedure, consistent with redistributed pairs.” Permutation also changes operator placement, repeated-input expressions and other joint content. Likewise, an intact advantage is information beyond the matched properties, not proof that pair identity has been controlled merely because aggregate B/C pair counts increased. Retention supports this particular shape/count-conditioned replacement; it does not establish token-only sufficiency or cheaper acquisition.

3. **note — Precision is plausible, not demonstrated for this contrast.** Half-versus-half intact-library resampling measures search noise while cancelling shared library quality. It does not measure the new between-build treatment effects or permutation variability, so the extrapolated ±12–15% is provisional. In plan.md specify the paired build/construction resampling, retaining both repeats associated with a construction together and the corresponding reference rows; 72 constructions are not 72 acquisitions. Report the uncertainty achieved with either fixed grid and price an unresolved result rather than adding seeds after seeing it. The 25% retention margin is a reasonable decision threshold; additional testing machinery is unnecessary.

4. **note — Name what would leave the acquisition choice unresolved.** A B/C interval crossing the decision boundaries, failed R-lib replication, failed reliability guard, or retention accompanied by an A lower bound ≤1.5 does not choose between the proposed acquisition targets. The A result is a sensitivity check in a different split, not proof of selective destruction on B/C. Audit exact predicate survival under the actual frozen construction rather than relying on the approximate one-third statement. Keep the proposed conservative “control too weak” interpretation if its gate fails, while still reporting whether the replacement saves search cost. A passing A check does not remove the interpretation limits in point 2.

5. **note — Prior-work framing is appropriate.** [Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico's Run Transferable Libraries (2004)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-24855-2_63) is the right closest technique: knowledge acquired in one GP run biases another. [Angeline (1997)](https://gpbib.pmacs.upenn.edu/gp-html/angeline_1997_tcbbe.html) is a relevant donor-content analogy, not this matched permutation construction. [PIPE](https://gpbib.pmacs.upenn.edu/gp-html/Salustowicz_97ecml.html) is an acquisition alternative, not the method being tested. The contribution is a controlled replacement test within an externally fitted, frozen library procedure, not a new learning algorithm or inherited map. No missing precedent identified here changes that assessment.
