The reciprocal sampling experiment is the right next experiment, but its adaptive confidence gates need one repair before they can justify parking the question.

The substantive revisions address the earlier critiques: exact, equally weighted fitting; per-task and joint pruning checks; reciprocal specificity separated from benefit over pruning; and evolution deferred until transfer is demonstrated. Keep this design. A hand-set aggregator bias would be cheaper but would not test learning from family members, and evolutionary runs now would obscure whether fitting worked.

**Repair the stopping rule.** Step 5 repeatedly checks ordinary 95% Poisson intervals and stops sampling when they resolve. Those intervals do not retain their nominal coverage under that stopping rule. This matters particularly for N, which can terminate the research direction. The cheapest implementation is to choose final pool sizes from calibration and throughput, freeze them before transfer sampling, and classify once. Alternatively, keep the four scheduled looks but use intervals adjusted for those looks, or an anytime-valid procedure. Retain ordinary intervals as descriptive summaries if useful. No larger experiment is needed.

**Close two gate gaps while implementing.** Calibration permits one surviving holdout per family, whereas the verdict assumes two: explicitly use the retained set and label a one-holdout result accordingly. Also make every unlisted combination a mixed/unresolved result, rather than forcing C/D. For example, specificity G/gain N in one family and specificity N/gain G in the other supports neither family-specific useful transfer nor a clean family-wide no-transfer verdict. The broad “also D” clause should not erase that distinction. If fewer than 20 calibration solvers remain for a fitting task at the cap, report the pruning control as under-supported rather than silently treating its support requirement as satisfied.

**What would leave us no wiser:** failed exact-fit validation, insufficient counts to resolve the worthwhile threshold, or contradictory transfer across the retained members. The first two are already correctly treated as inconclusive. Report member-level reversals even when the mean log-ratio resolves: that mean estimates average transfer over these particular tasks, not dependable transfer to every unseen threshold. Sampling A still leaves explanation B—supply without evolutionary benefit—open for the next cycle.

Preserve calibration, fitting validation and the uniform/matched/mismatched transfer comparisons first. If runtime binds, cut the 30M descriptive both-fit/swap samples and additional prune sampling before sacrificing precision on those decisive comparisons; unresolved beyond-pruning need not block A/C/D.
