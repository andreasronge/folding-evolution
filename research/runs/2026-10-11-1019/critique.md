This is a feasible, decision-relevant control of the value of acquisition over a supplied prior; address the feasibility details and narrower interpretations below in plan.md.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible, but distinguish measurements from assumptions.** [0833's analysis](../2026-10-11-0833/analysis.md) gives B/C R-tree 1,723/1,728 solved (53% first attempt), R-TS 1,728/1,728 (78% first attempt), and 6.63 versus 2.40 worker-s/job. Include those counts in plan.md. The proposed 5,184 jobs require about 19.8–27.1k worker-s under the assumed hand-arm range, consistent with roughly 35–45 minutes at the measured throughput. However, R-hand ≤ 6.63 s/job is a scenario, not a measured upper bound: an ineffective donor law could increase restarts and failures. Specify the fixed stage-0 seeds, timing-only admission threshold, overhead allowance and stop-on-infeasibility action before running; do not select a donor variant from calibration outcomes. The constructor is small, the existing sampler preserves multiset multiplicity, and the required runners exist on research/main. The complete 4–5 h estimate is plausible.

2. **note — Worth running before the best alternative, an unfamiliar-content bank.** This directly asks whether externally acquired information buys enough over a strong human-supplied bias to remain the practical comparator. A new bank would test a more consequential generalization boundary, but without this control another library win could still be explained by supplying obvious small sums. The present fixed three-arm comparison is an economical prerequisite; no new acquisition or additional ingredient arms are needed. Replaying historical controls and scoring only R-hand would save compute, but would forgo fresh paired confirmation after the prior was chosen from those historical results, as well as contemporaneous worker-time comparisons. I do not see a clearly cheaper design with the same evidential value.

3. **note — Keep the answer at the substitution level.** Replace “the B/C case for learning reduces to supplied sums” with “this fixed, development-informed sum prior retains the B/C saving within the 1.25 margin.” It is source-free at execution, not task-blind or a fresh-bank transfer result. Retention permits a smaller learned advantage that can repay acquisition at longer horizons. Conversely, a loss rejects this prior, not all supplied priors. The proposed explanations “concentration … matters” and “breadth dilutes the useful pairs” are hypotheses: pair identities, constants, ANY/GT content, sizes and frequencies change together. Neither outcome isolates the responsible ingredient. Preserve the acquisition-plus-search curves and their uncertainty rather than treating the margin as the owner's deployment horizon.

4. **note — Size is reasonable; acknowledge the outcome that leaves the choice unresolved.** Resampling the 24 paired build blocks is appropriate; the hand blocks are search-seed blocks, not independent acquisitions. The historical log half-width of about 0.13 is a useful sizing anchor, not a guarantee for the new contrast. At R-hand/R-TS ≈ 1.15 it implies approximately [1.01, 1.31]: neither registered branch resolves, despite lying inside the stated expectation range. A result around 1.2–1.3 would bound the effect but leave the practical comparator undecided. Keep the fixed size and the return-to-strategy rule; do not inflate the queue merely to chase the threshold. A hand arm that fails to beat R-tree rejects this substitution even if the primary margin remains unresolved—it does not identify why it failed.

5. **note — Prior-work framing is broadly right; correct one attribution.** The implemented mechanism is a biased subtree-mutation donor distribution, with expanded ordinary primitives, rather than new callable primitives or inherited map adaptation. [Keijzer, Ryan & Cattolico's Run Transferable Libraries](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/papers/3103/31030531.pdf) is an appropriate cross-run functional-bias precedent. However, [Dinh, Chu & Nguyen (2015), §IV](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/308873115_Transfer_learning_in_genetic_programming) transfers sampled source subtrees into the target's **first generation**, not into mutation. Correct that label in plan.md. The contribution here is a controlled, acquisition-priced comparison against a fixed prior on this bank, not a new library-learning technique; the cited precedents adequately establish that distinction.
