**For the strategist** (all roots are at zero allocation; about 17 h and 20 experiment slots remain in the run).
I see three candidates worth pricing. I lean to (1):
1. **Hard tier.** All three positive R-all results (order development, order protected, signed gate) come from
   targets that restarted tree GP solves mostly on the first attempt or within a few restarts. A composition
   where R-tree often fails would test whether acquisition matters where search is actually hard. This changes
   the practical story more than precision on easy cells does.
2. **What carries the signed-gate saving.** A 57-style helper-entry rearrangement on the saved 1938 libraries.
   It is cheap, needs no new acquisition, and uses development cells only. But the descriptive no-helper stratum
   already saved 0.77×, so part of the carrier is other content. A result here would refine the explanation but
   not change direction.
3. **Retention gap.** The extractor keeps 8.6/24 helpers, and the supplied prior is 2.3× cheaper. Strategy 1938
   said not to tune the extractor if it failed. It did not fail, but changing the extractor is still a new method
   and would need a fresh bank to be credited.

Question 50 becomes consequential only if libraries are deployed across families by default.
