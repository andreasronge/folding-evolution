The measured source yield and destination costs make this a feasible, worthwhile test of the frozen acquisition recipe on a second content family, provided plan.md bounds its interpretation and completes the freeze and precision checks below.

1. **note — Feasibility is credible; finish admission before protected search.** Stage 0 reports 192/192 source solves on attempt one, about 200 k evaluations per build, and 12/12 first-attempt R-tree destination solves; two acquired libraries retain 11 and 6 of 24 helpers. Those are useful feasibility anchors, not population hit-rate guarantees. The job arithmetic is correct: 2,304 acquisition plus 8,064 scoring jobs. At the reported worker rates, ordinary search consumes only a few wall minutes on 11 workers; a ten-minute estimate allows overhead, and 110 minutes of queue timeouts provides substantial reserve. In plan.md include screening, extraction, validation and capped-job throughput in the cost check, and reconcile the source timing estimates (proposal ~0.05 worker-s/job versus stage-0 notes ~10 worker-s/96-job build). Freeze the admitted source roster, actual protected count, bank/method hashes and fresh seeds before protected smoke or scoring. If screening leaves 8–17 protected cells, explicitly recalculate the grid and precision rather than retaining the 18-cell forecast.

2. **note — Worth running for recipe portability, with limited practical upside.** This changes a consequential belief: whether unchanged semantic recurrence extraction can acquire useful bias outside the order family. The transported-library arm makes it more informative than another within-order attribution study; the supplied gate prior exposes how far acquisition remains from family-informed knowledge. About 80 searches to repay acquisition against R-tree is plausible from 200 k / 2.5 k, but repayment against an already-owned R-order library will be later, or nonexistent. Report that comparison with uncertainty too. The four-arm design is proportionate; I see no clearly cheaper design that answers both portability and the value of new acquisition. Additional development scoring is expendable if preparation runs long, but do not cut protected information after seeing protected performance.

3. **note — Specify precision and what an unresolved result buys.** Twenty-four independent acquisitions and eighteen cells are sensible, but two pilot builds cannot establish between-build precision, especially with helper coverage varying substantially. Plan.md should give a conservative precision scenario using historical build variation and the four-cell development data, with cells and builds resampled jointly across paired arms. State whether inference concerns the frozen roster or a broader cell population; these cells are input rebindings of one construction, not eighteen independent content families. An interval spanning 0.80 would leave the material-saving decision unresolved. A resolved R-all/R-tree saving with a broad R-all/R-order interval would establish useful acquisition on this family while leaving the reason to buy new content unresolved; price resolution at review rather than calling that generic transfer.

4. **note — Correct the secondary interpretation before running.** “Below 1 means acquisition adds” needs an interval, not merely a point estimate. “About 1 means the saving is generic” and “R-order matching R-all, meaning the saving is generic” are too strong even if costs are tightly similar: distinct content can deliver similar savings, and these libraries also differ in size and composition. Use “no incremental advantage over transported content resolved” when appropriate. A win identifies a better acquisition procedure for these targets, not signed-helper causality or symmetric family specificity. The helper census remains descriptive. No extra causal-control arm is necessary for the stated primary question.

5. **note — Closest technique is correctly identified; distinguish its mechanism from ours.** [Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico's Run Transferable Libraries (2004)](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/papers/3103/31030531.pdf) already transfers functional bias across GP runs, including training on simpler instances. Thus the contribution is a replication/boundary result for this fixed semantic extractor, expanded mutation donors and charged acquisition, not invention of library transfer. “External fitting with no inheritance” describes this repository's procedure; do not present it as an exact description of the original RTL implementation. No missing predecessor changes the experiment's value.

6. **note — Question 50's reopen condition is newly met, but a gate is not needed here.** The observed 12/12 first-attempt solves trigger its approximately 90% condition. Keep the unchanged mix for this acquisition comparison and report first-attempt solves and cost tails by arm. A library-on-failure arm would receive almost no exposure on these easy targets and would mainly reproduce R-tree. The proposed primary comparison can detect pooled harm, although pooled benefit cannot exclude harm on individual cells. A policy experiment becomes useful if this run reveals consequential harm; it need not displace this test now.
