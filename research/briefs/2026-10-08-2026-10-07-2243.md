# Brief: run 2026-10-07-2243 (root 23, slot 1 of 2): stopped at the cost gate

**What was tested and why.** Your heritable-map note, step 1: can a token-frequency vector that
each program carries and passes on learn a useful bias through ordinary program selection, and
does that vector help fresh populations once frozen? The design compared inherited vectors with a
control that shuffles vectors across the population every generation (breaking the link to
ancestry), on the sum/max threshold-1 and -5 targets. Uniform, your hand-set scaffold and the
1558 fit were the references. 16 acquisition runs per family × arm were planned.

**What happened.** No experiment ran. The code was built and validated (commit `25f929e`; 151
tests; all four targets checked exhaustively; the old search is reproduced exactly with
modifier mutation off). The pre-run cost gate then failed: 569 min against a 210-min ceiling.
That gate charged every scoring batch twice the single worst search seen (one 170 s max1 run
caught up in shortcut verification). At the measured means the same roster takes about 1 h of
queue, or 2.3 h with a 2× margin. So the gate was too strict; the cost itself was affordable.

Stage-0 timing runs (one acquisition per family × arm, 4 paired searches; **observations only**):
- **Sum:** inherited solved 47/48 training episodes, broken 17/48. Frozen geo-mean evaluations
  on sum1 were 26k (inherited), 163k (broken), 42k (uniform) and 4.5k (scaffold). The
  inherited vector raised INPUT to 0.36; the broken one drifted onto irrelevant tokens.
- **Max:** both arms solved 6–7/48 episodes. Both learned vectors failed all 8 frozen
  searches, which is worse than uniform. The inherited max vector *lost* INPUT.

**What we learned.** Nothing yet about the hypotheses. The design itself has a problem: episodes
stop at the first solve, so the arm that solves less runs about 3× more generations and drifts
further. Inherited-vs-broken would then partly measure how much the control degraded. The
right primary reference is uniform, with equal generation counts in both arms.
