# Brief: run 2026-10-07-0315 (question 17, slot 2)

**What was tested and why.** Run 2331 crossed the 20 learned token maps (M) with G4 (G) on the
three withheld cells. It found that M helps both through the programs a search starts from and
through its use during search. On the single BE cell the start mattered more; on the two PA cells
the decoder during search mattered more. This run repeated the same frozen 2×2 (GG, MM, MG, GM,
with generation-0 programs held identical between paired arms) on the ten training cells: 4 BE
and 6 PA, 200 fresh seeds. It asked two things. Do both parts replicate? Does the balance between
them follow cell family?

**What happened.** The run was clean: 122 000 searches in 4.3 h, all validation passed, and 786
historical rows reproduced bit-exactly.
- Searching under M rather than G, from identical starting programs: **1.28× [1.22, 1.34]** faster.
- Starting from M's programs rather than G's, both searching under M: **1.33× [1.26, 1.40]** faster.
- The full gain is 2.44× [2.23, 2.66]. The two parts are strongly sub-additive (interaction
  −0.52 log2 [−0.62, −0.42]).
- Balance, measured as log2(MG/GM) per cell: negative on all four BE cells (start heavier) and
  −0.05 to +0.54 on the PA cells. **BE minus PA = −0.45 log2 [−0.68, −0.22]** over cells. Every
  one of the 20 maps points the same way, and the result is robust to the cap and to dropping
  any one cell.

**What we learned.** The learned map works through both channels on all 13 cells tested, each
worth about 1.3× given the other, and the two overlap heavily. On the training cells the start
weighs relatively more on branch-else cells and the ongoing decoder on plus-arg cells. Neither
channel vanishes in either family.
