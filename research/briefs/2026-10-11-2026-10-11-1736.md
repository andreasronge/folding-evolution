# Brief: run 2026-10-11-1736: can program selection evolve a useful frequency of an inherited library choice?

**What was tested and why.** The earlier probe (1427) gave each program an inherited bit that switches its mutation
donors to the acquired R-all order library. Selection favoured the bit, but it fixed or vanished within about 15
generations, so no useful law came out. This redesign tested whether selection can tune a useful variation law
through inheritance alone once loss is reversible:
- **Bit mutation:** each child's bit is redrawn from Bernoulli(1/16) with probability 1/32.
- **Low start:** f₀ = 1/16, where the dose curve is steep.
- **Readout:** each population's own final law, deployed alone at q = f/2 in fresh searches.
- **Arms:** L linked, S shuffled before selection, N neutral bit (no library). 24 libraries × 6 populations each,
  six order development cells, 8,640 readout jobs. Commit `3a18ef5`; the queue took 72 of the 150 minutes allowed.

**What happened.**
- **The bit rose under linked selection only.** Mean final frequency: L 0.29 [0.23, 0.35], N 0.06, S 0.05
  (L − N 0.23 [0.16, 0.30]; 24/24 libraries). Nothing absorbed.
- **The rise was bimodal.** 46/144 L populations ended above 0.5 and 55/144 at or below the start. Selection is
  nearly flat while the bit is rare (+0.0017 per generation) and about ten times stronger once it is common.
- **Primary: evolved law / starting law 0.805 [0.704, 0.913]** in evaluations. The pre-set "useful" bar was an
  upper bound below 0.90, so it narrowly missed: **unresolved** by the frozen rule. The interval excludes 1.
- It also beat the neutral (0.75) and shuffled (0.70) endpoints, with upper bounds below 1. Headroom was present
  (starting law 2.12× costlier than R-all).
- **The law is just a dose.** Each endpoint's cost sits within 6% of the dose curve at its own q. Simply setting
  q = 0.5 by hand is 1.71× [1.45, 2.01] cheaper and needs no exposure. The exposure repays only after about 16
  searches.

**What we learned.** With reversible loss, program selection alone can raise the frequency of a useful supplied
variation law and make fresh search somewhat cheaper. It does this unreliably: rare carriers are barely selected,
so only populations that escape the low-frequency band get the benefit. The acquired law is far short of the
obvious hand setting. It is the program's first inherited law whose interval against its starting law lies below 1, but
it is a dose of supplied content, not learned content.
