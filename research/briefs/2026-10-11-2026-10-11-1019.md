# Brief: run 2026-10-11-1019: does a hand-written sum prior do as well as a learned tree library?

**What was tested and why.** Libraries learned from TS sources and from DG sources both cut gate-sum B/C cost
about 2.6×, yet they share little beyond small `ADD(input, input|const)` donors. That raised a question: do we
need to learn anything at all? This run wrote a fixed prior from the alphabet alone, with no acquisition. It
holds all 12 ordered `ADD(Xi,Xj)` and all 32 `ADD(Xi,c)`/`ADD(c,Xi)` forms, each class getting half the draws.
We used it as half the mutation donors in restarted tree GP (R-hand). It was compared on B/C with the 24 saved
TS libraries (R-TS) and plain R-tree: 1,728 paired jobs per arm, fresh seeds, commit `e29219b`. This is a
control of a known method (hand-chosen building blocks against mined ones).

**What happened.**
- Primary: R-hand ÷ R-TS **0.85 [0.76, 0.96]** in mean evaluations. That passes the pre-set "retained" rule
  (upper < 1.25), and the interval excludes 1 in the prior's favour.
- R-hand ÷ R-tree **0.32 [0.29, 0.35]**. R-TS ÷ R-tree was 0.37, which replicates 0833's 0.38.
- Both donor arms solved 1,728/1,728 jobs; R-tree solved 1,723. Worker-seconds agree with evaluations
  (0.84 [0.75, 0.95]).
- Charged with its acquisition (0.245 M evaluations per library), TS never pays for itself against the prior
  within 200 searches.
- The result is not uniform. 19/24 builds favour the prior, but one build and one cell clearly favour TS. The
  gain sits mostly on split (03|12) (0.75), where TS libraries rarely contain the needed pair sum, and not on
  split (02|13) (0.98).
- The queue took 31 min against a 150 min timeout. There were no failures.

**What we learned.** On B/C, the learned libraries have not been shown to add anything over a free,
hand-written set of small sums, which is slightly cheaper. The practical comparator for this bank is now
"tree GP + sum prior", not plain tree GP. Any later learner has to beat that.

should *not* suffice, with three arms: R-tree, a prior built by the same rule from the new alphabet, and a
freshly learned library. It is the first design that could show learning beating a supplied prior. It would
also drop the development-bank caveat. A PIPE-style generator should wait until a destination exists where the
prior fails. Stopping the library line and returning to root 23 (inheritance) is also defensible.

**Failures and uncertainty.**
- This is a development bank and one alphabet (`v2_x4`). The prior was written after seeing that the family
  sums inputs, so it is source-free but not task-blind.
- It is a substitution result, not an isolation. All six pairs, constants 2/5, 44 entries and no ANY/GT
  entries changed at once. "Pair coverage matters" is a post-hoc reading of 12 cells.
- Some TS builds do beat the prior. The mean says only that acquisition does not pay here.
- Split A, where the learned `GT(ADD,ADD)` predicate gave a 5× extra gain, was not tested. The prior cannot
  express that predicate.
- Horizons beyond 200 searches were not computed.
