# Brief: run 2026-10-07-1924 (question 21; root 10 slot 14 of 14)

**What was tested and why.** Run 1707 fitted previous-token tables C to exact solver tapes from
G4 searches. They beat a token-only fit by about 1.3× on withheld cells. This run asked whether
one *feedback* step helps further. Each of the 32 saved tables collected new exact training
solvers under itself, and the unchanged fitting rule was applied to them, giving **C2**. C2 was
compared with:
- its parent C;
- **C'**, a fresh one-shot refit to a new G4 corpus with matched attempts.

C' tests whether "just refitting" explains any gain. All three were scored on fresh training
seeds, then on the three withheld cells.

**What happened.** The run was clean: 39 976 searches in 73 min, the replay was bit-identical,
all checks passed, and every stage ran at full size.
- **C2 beats its parent: 1.40× [1.35, 1.46]** on training, faster in 32/32 lineages (BE 1.56×,
  PA 1.27×).
- **The gain transfers: 1.29× [1.20, 1.38]** on the withheld cells (28/32).
- **It is the source, not the refit.** C2/C' is 1.41× (training) and 1.33× (withheld), with
  lower bounds 1.34 and 1.26. C'/C is 0.997× [0.94, 1.06], not resolved from 1.
- C2 is sharper than C in every lineage (row entropy 3.91 → 3.80 bits). At this step it showed
  no sign of harming transfer.
- Collecting under C cost about a third as much per search as under G4 (0.63 against 2.17
  worker-s).

**What we learned.** A decoder refitted to the solvers it helped find improves again, about
1.3–1.4×, and the improvement carries to unseen compositions. A second refit from G4 tapes adds
nothing resolved. So the map can adapt usefully from its own discoveries, at least for one
external step.
