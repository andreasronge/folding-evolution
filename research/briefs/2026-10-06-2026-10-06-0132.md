# Brief: run 2026-10-06-0132, decoder learning on post-addition (root 10, 13)

**What was tested and why.** This is root 10's first try at evolving a decoder. Earlier runs
found one usable split: six post-addition tasks `(S>0 ? X : Y) + Z` for training, and two
withheld compositions whose then/else pair never appears in training. An outer loop evolved
decoder tables on the training tasks. The tables were then tested on fresh searches of all
eight tasks. There were three learners: C (all 552 contextual weights, starting from the
hand-set grammar G), M (23 token multipliers on G's rows) and T (23 context-free weights,
starting from G-marg). Each got 6 independent trajectories of 25 generations.

**What happened.** Clean, complete run: 5 h 46 min, commit `02cf76f`. Outcome **row 1**: C did
not improve training. Speed vs frozen G (> 1 = faster, 95% interval, 524k cap):

| learner | fresh training | holdout (S?M:S)+M | holdout (S?M:S)+m |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | 0.92 [0.78, 1.09] | 1.16 [0.86, 1.54] | 0.80 [0.60, 1.07] |
| M | 2.23 [1.82, 2.75] | 2.01 [1.47, 2.81] | 1.71 [1.26, 2.32] |
| T | 0.47 [0.37, 0.58] | 0.57 [0.41, 0.80] | 0.41 [0.31, 0.55] |

- All six M trajectories were faster than G on every set, and M was still improving at
  generation 25.
- C's learning curves were flat. It drifted about a tenth as far as M did.
- T beat its own start (G-marg) by 1.7–2.1× but stayed well behind G.
- The learned change agrees across trajectories: more INPUT, less DUP, more IF_GT and reducers.

**What we learned.** Selecting on six tasks found a token re-weighting of G that speeds the two
unseen compositions by about 2×. So G was not near an optimum, contrary to my pre-run
expectation. The full contextual learner never left G. Each child changed three table cells by
about e^±0.5, which is far below the per-run noise. That is a fault of the operator. It is not
evidence against learning context.
