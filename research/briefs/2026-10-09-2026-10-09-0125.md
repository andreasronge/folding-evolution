# Brief: run 2026-10-09-0125, are the context tables' gains just their token frequencies? (root 10, question 29)

**What was tested and why.** On the fresh then-addition bank (run 1548), context tables C fitted
to G4 solver tapes beat token-only fits T to the same tapes by 2.12×. C and T also emit different
token frequencies. So the question was whether C's advantage is simply its token mix. The test
scored the frozen control K. K is G4 reweighted by 24 token multipliers so that its pooled emitted
frequencies match C's (within 3e-5). It ran on the same 16 cells, 16 corpora and paired seeds as C
and T. If K came close to C, a much simpler frequency map would be the target for an evolved bias.
This is a mechanism test of an EDA-style external fit (cf. N-gram GP), not a new technique.

**What happened.** The run was complete and clean: 2 048 searches in 42 min, bit-exact replays,
and every hash and pairing check passed.
- Primary **C/K 2.48× [2.17, 2.83]**. It was 2.25× at 1 × cap and 1.92× on both-solved pairs.
  All 16 corpora (1.44–3.67×) and all 16 cells favour C. The pre-stated rule says this
  pooled-frequency replacement is **insufficient**.
- K is slower than the token-only fit: **K/T 0.86× [0.77, 0.96]**. That matches the old bank
  (0.83×).
- Solves at the cap: C 86.5%, T 75.5%, K 72.5%, G4 63.3%. K beats G4 about 1.6× (descriptive).
- The result landed at the top of my expected 1.5–2.5×. Neither named surprise occurred.

**What we learned.** Matching C's token mix on G4's template recovers none of C's edge over the
token-only fit, now on two banks. The useful content of the fitted tables is structure beyond
pooled frequency. We have not yet separated whether that structure is the previous-token context
or simply which tokens sit at which tape positions.
