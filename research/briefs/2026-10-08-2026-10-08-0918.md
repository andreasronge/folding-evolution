# Brief: run 2026-10-08-0918 (root 23, inherited token bias): acquisition ran, one run cut by a hidden deadline, no result

**What was tested and why.** Root 23 asks whether a token-frequency vector carried by each
program and inherited by its offspring can learn a useful bias through program selection
alone, then help fresh populations once frozen. This was the approved 0843 design with the
reference seeds cut to the 16 shared contrast seeds, which was enough to pass the cost gate
(10 603 s of timeouts against a 10 800 s ceiling). Design: 20 acquisition runs per family
(sum, max) × arm (inherited, broken ancestry), each 48 episodes × 128 generations. Every learned
vector is then frozen and scored on fresh searches against uniform, the hand scaffold and the
1558 fit.

**What happened.** Acquisition ran: 79 of 80 runs completed in 62 min. One max run was cut at
episode 31 by a 1 200 s per-job default deadline in the code. That deadline appears in no plan.
The code review saw it and judged it unlikely to bind. The completeness check then blocked all
scoring, which is the correct behaviour, so **no frozen search ran and there is no primary
result**. At 10 workers, runs were 1.3–1.9× slower than the single 4-worker timing runs. Max runs
averaged ≈ 600 s, about 60% of it in the exact verifier, with a tail above 1 000 s.

Acquisition observations (not evidence):
- Within-acquisition solves show no resolved arm difference: sum 30.6 vs 31.1 of 48, paired
  difference −0.6 [−7.5, +6.0]; max 22.8 vs 18.9, +4.1 [−1.3, +9.5].
- Solve rate fell over the episodes in every cell.
- Both arms drifted equally far from uniform (L1 ≈ 0.9), which the σ = 0.03 random walk alone
  explains.

**What we learned.** Nothing about the hypotheses. The observations suggest the learned vectors
are mostly drift, so a completed run will most likely show "Bounded" (no gain over uniform) for
this step size. That is a prediction, not a finding. Process lesson: per-job time limits must
appear in the plan, and in a main stage they should equal the stage timeout.
