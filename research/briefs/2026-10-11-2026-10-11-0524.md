# Brief: run 2026-10-11-0524 — do tree-library donors need their learned arrangements?

**What was tested and why.** Frozen subtree libraries learned on DG cut restarted tree GP to about 0.35× of
plain tree GP on the two gate-sum splits that no learned predicate covers (B, C). The question was whether
the libraries' specific expressions matter there, or only their shapes and primitive counts. If only shapes
and counts matter, a simpler statistical learner could replace them. Arms: intact libraries (R-lib), the
same libraries with labels shuffled within size/arity/width pools (R-perm, 3 frozen shuffles per library),
and plain restarted tree GP (R-tree). 24 libraries × 18 cells × 6 repeats, shared seeds, 7,776 jobs, commit
`7c0e5df`. Split A, where the learned predicate fits, served as the positive control.

**What happened.**
- B/C (primary, 1,728 jobs/arm): cost R-perm ÷ R-lib **1.03 [0.85, 1.23]**, inside the 1.25 retention
  margin, though only by 0.025. R-perm ÷ R-tree 0.36 [0.30, 0.43]. Both donor arms solved all 1,728 jobs.
- Replication: R-lib ÷ R-tree 0.35 [0.30, 0.41], matching 0348.
- Split A (control): shuffling cost **5.1× [3.7, 7.1]**. A then behaved like B/C (0.33× of R-tree).
- The shuffle removed the learned predicate almost entirely (0–1 of 63 copies kept), but it also tripled
  the supply of the pair sums that B/C need. Shuffled entries appeared verbatim in solvers more often (10%
  vs 2%), with no change in mean cost.
- Price held: scoring took 45 min of the 2 h allowance, 27k worker-seconds.

**What we learned.** Where the learned predicate fits the target, the libraries' advantage depends on that
exact arrangement. Elsewhere, donors with the same shapes and label counts work as well at this resolution.
What carries that second gain is still open. The result may be a cancellation (arrangement lost, useful
pairs gained), and an intact advantage of up to about 20% is not excluded.

**How the tree changed.** 47 closed. Root 10 is at 39/39 used. Digest: one new bullet. The 46 heading no
longer says the gain is "mostly" from the predicate split, because split A carries only about 43% of the
savings (critique corrections 6–8 applied). No parked question reopens.

**Next.** No proposal: `next: strategy`, because the budget is spent and strategy 0524 kept the follow-up for
its own review. My recommendation is a cheap check before pricing any statistical learner: keep the
libraries' shapes but draw labels uniformly, with no learned counts, and score on B/C. Full cost is about
4 h, with no new acquisition. This tells whether the unmatched-split gain needs anything learned beyond shape
and size. Alternatives still on the table are different-source acquisition (7–10 h) and an
unfamiliar-content boundary (5–8 h).

**Failures and uncertainty.** No run failures; all validation and seed audits were clean. The retention bound
passed narrowly. The control is a single construction, applied to one acquisition cohort on a development
bank. Two plan details were wrong but harmless. The proposal expected about a third of the predicates to
survive shuffling, and almost none did, which made the control stronger. The plan's predicate-survival
counts were for the reversed orientation.
