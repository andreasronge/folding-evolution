# Brief: run 2026-10-08-1246, comparison-gate bank (root 10, question 24)

**What was tested and why.** Root 10's best positive result was a table fitted to solver programs
(context C). It beat a token-only fit T to the same programs about 1.3× on withheld cells. That
evidence rested on one branch-else holdout and two post-addition holdouts. This run built a new
bank that replaces the sign gate with a comparison between two reducers. Branch-else is
`A>B ? C : D+E` and post-addition is `(A>B ? C : D)+E`, both 13-token compositions. The bank was
frozen with 4 training and 4 untouched holdouts per family. The run then checked whether C still
beats T on the training cells, using 16 fresh solver corpora. Holdouts were not searched.

**What happened.** The run completed in 119 min with 5 376 searches, all checks passed and 0
holdout searches.
- The screen kept 37 branch-else and 56 post-addition behaviours. The baseline grammar G4 solved
  68% of training-cell searches at the 524k cap, so there is room to improve.
- C/T was **3.11× [2.78, 3.48]**: branch-else 2.86×, post-addition 3.37×, and 2.82× with milder
  scoring of unsolved runs. C won in 16/16 corpora and 64/64 corpus × cell pairs. Solve rates were
  C 91%, T 77% and G4 68%.
- Both fits beat G4 (C 5.8×, T 1.9×; descriptive), so T was not a broken control.
- Collection yield was 59%, above the 40% floor.
- I expected 1.2–1.4×. The effect is more than twice that.

**What we learned.** The solver-corpus context advantage is not an artefact of the old bank's
short shapes: it replicates, and is larger, on longer gated compositions. It is still a
within-cell fitting result, because every cell was its fit's own source. Whether it transfers to
new compositions is untested. Why the effect is larger is not identified. Candidates are longer
programs, a harder base task and a weaker T.
