# Brief — run 2026-10-04-1839: self-mate establishment

**What was tested and why.** A rare shared-helper form gets wiped out by crossover with a
selected mate, yet crossover is also what finds solutions. §32 J showed that crossing a parent
with *itself* still finds solutions, just slower. So we asked: under self-mating, can a rare
shared form establish? If it can, finding solutions and keeping shared forms are not in
conflict. We re-ran the §31 D contests (shared at 1/32 and 1/10 vs duplicated or partly
shared, L 64, seeds 0–29) with `crossover_mate: self` at crossover 0.3 and 0.7: 240 runs.
We also ran a one-step self-crossover census of 10,000 children per hand-built form.

**What happened.**
- **Wins:** shared won 183/240 under self-mate, against 34/240 with a selected mate on the same
  seeds and 75/120 with crossover off.
- **Per cell (self 0.3 / 0.7):** vs duplicated 27/27 and 30/30; vs partly 12/13 and 26/18. The
  off-crossover baselines are 19, 29, 8 and 19. All 8 cells cleared the pre-registered
  "two thirds of crossover off" bar.
- **No early loss:** the shared share rises from generation 0. With a selected mate it halves
  within 5 generations.
- **Census:** no child ever changed form. Shared breaks 20% of the time against 19% for the
  other forms.

**What we learned.** The establishment barrier is mixing between lineages, not crossover
itself. Self-mating lets both discovery and establishment work under one operator. A new side
fact: when shared loses to "duplicated", the population actually ends *partly shared*, a form
nobody seeded. Shared is replaced by a third form; duplicated never wins.
