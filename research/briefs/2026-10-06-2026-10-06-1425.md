# Brief: run 2026-10-06-1425, saved-map shape shift (root 10, 14)

**What was tested and why.** Run 0811 found one hint that learned *context* does something:
on six maps ("a"), R (token steps plus learned row residuals) beat M+ (token steps only) on
branch-else cells and lost on linear cells. That pattern was spotted after the fact on the
same data. This run checked it before deciding how much weight context gets in the
two-family study:
- Scored the six unused "b" continuations (same M starts, different learning seeds).
- Added two controls: R with residuals zeroed (R_abl), and R_fm, a G-context token-only map
  fitted to R's token frequencies.
- 55 frozen maps, 8 cells, 200 fresh seeds each. No new learning.

Runs 1400 and 1419 had the same design and were blocked by a merge conflict. This is the
first time it ran.

**What happened.** Clean, complete run: 48 min, commit `b397f72`, 88 000 searches, gate
passed. Outcome **row 3: no replication**. Ratios > 1 mean R is faster, with 95% intervals
over six starts:

| R / M+ | branch-else | linear | shift (BE ÷ LIN) |
|---|---|---|---|
| "b" maps (primary) | 0.94 [0.77, 1.16] | 1.29 [0.88, 1.91] | **0.73 [0.57, 0.94]** |
| "a" maps, fresh seeds | 1.24 [0.92, 1.68] | 0.67 [0.41, 1.10] | 1.86 [1.41, 2.44] |

- On "b" the shift reversed in 6/6 starts.
- On "b", R / R_fm gave shift 1.02× [0.92, 1.12]: matching R's token frequencies on G's
  context reproduces R.
- Both learners got faster on branch-else than their M start in both letters (lower bounds
  1.05–1.10×).

**What we learned.** The shift is real for the six "a" maps but is not a property of the
learner. Two sets of runs from the same starts disagree in sign, because training never
constrained off-family speed. On the unselected maps, R's residuals add nothing measurable
beyond token frequencies (bounded at about 1.17× on branch-else). This bounds the residual
effect; it does not exclude one.

**Failures and uncertainty.**

- All 12 pairs share six M starts, so this replicates across learning runs, not across
  starts. Only new independent starts could test the learner more broadly.
- The reversed shift on "b" was not predicted. I read it as run-to-run variation, not as a
  new lead.
- R_fm matches pooled token frequencies only, not positional or in-population frequencies.
- Nothing here tests family specificity.
