# Brief: run 2026-10-08-1046 (root 23, inherited token bias): frozen scoring ran — no useful bias, root parked

**What was tested and why.** Root 23 asks whether a token-frequency vector carried by each
program and inherited by its offspring can learn a useful bias through program selection, then
help fresh populations once frozen. Run 0918 had acquired 79 of 80 vectors, but a hidden
deadline cut one, which blocked all scoring. This run recovered it with no change to the science.
It reused the 79 valid acquisitions, reran the cut one from its original seed, and checked replays
against 0918. It then scored every frozen vector against uniform, the hand scaffold and the 1558
fit on 16 shared seeds per training target.

**What happened.** The recovery worked. All replay gates matched exactly, and all 80 acquisitions
and 2 752 searches completed in 75 min of queue. The primary ratio is uniform ÷ inherited cost
(above 1 means inherited helps):

| Family | uniform ÷ inherited | broken ÷ inherited | inherited ÷ scaffold |
|---|---|---|---|
| sum | **0.33 [0.21, 0.53]** | 1.00 [0.69, 1.41] | 11.9 [7.0, 20.4] |
| max | **0.73 [0.50, 1.06]** | 1.58 [1.04, 2.36] | 5.75 [3.66, 8.95] |

The result is **Bounded in both families**:
- On sum, the inherited vectors make search 2–5× *costlier* than uniform.
- On max, a gain above 1.06× is excluded.
- The scaffold is far better in both families.
- Persistent ancestry beat shuffled ancestry on max, but only by making the vectors less harmful.
  Inherited vectors were still no better than uniform.

**What we learned.** In this setup, inheriting token frequencies did not produce a vector that
helps fresh search on its own training targets. The vectors end concentrated on run-specific,
mostly unhelpful tokens. This bounds one procedure (σ = 0.03, 48 × 128 generations, development
bank). It does not rule out self-adaptation in general.
