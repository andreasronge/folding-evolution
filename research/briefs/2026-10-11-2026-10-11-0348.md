# Brief: 2026-10-11, run 0348: do the learned subtree libraries transfer to a fresh shape?

**What was tested and why.** In run 0150, 24 frozen libraries of recurring source subtrees made restarted tree
GP about 2.6× cheaper on DG. DG is development data. Strategy 0348 asked whether the same unchanged libraries help
on a new composition. The new bank, `x4-gate-sum-v1`, has the shape `(Xa+Xb)>(Xc+Xd) ? Xe+Xf : Xg`. It was built
performance-blind and frozen with the method before any protected search: 4 development cells and 18 protected
cells, 6 for each of the three ways to split X0–X3 into two pairs. The arms were R-tree (plain), R-lib (half of
the mutation donors drawn from a library) and R-size (size-matched random donors), with 1,728 jobs each.
Commit `1f02bb7`; queue 43 min; all gates passed.

**What happened** (mean evaluations, failures charged at 524k, 95% intervals over the 24 libraries):
- **R-lib ÷ R-tree 0.25 [0.22, 0.29]**, about 4× cheaper (69k → 17k evaluations). R-lib solved 1,728/1,728 and
  R-tree 1,726/1,728.
- **R-lib ÷ R-size 0.29 [0.23, 0.37].** R-size ÷ R-tree was 0.86 [0.72, 1.01], unresolved.
- R-lib was cheaper in all 18 cells and all 24 libraries. Worker seconds agree (0.23×).
- **The gain differs by split:**
  - 0.055× (≈ 18×) on the split whose predicate the libraries learned on DG; about half of those solvers
    contain that predicate verbatim;
  - 0.36× and 0.34× (≈ 3×) on the two splits no learned predicate covers; large entries are almost never
    reused verbatim there.
- With acquisition charged (2.9M evaluations per library), a library repays after about 55 [49, 62] searches.

**What we learned.** A library acquired on one family transfers as a large search-cost saving to a new
composition of its parts, beyond random donors of the same size. Most of the headline number comes from reusing
one familiar predicate. A real ≈ 3× gain remains where no predicate matches, and what carries it is unknown:
pair sums, small entries, or simply more inputs and `ADD` in the donors.
