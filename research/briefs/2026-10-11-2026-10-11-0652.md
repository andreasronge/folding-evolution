# Brief: run 2026-10-11-0652 — do tree-library donors need their learned labels, given their shapes?

**What was tested and why.** Run 0524 showed that on gate-sum's B/C splits, the frozen DG subtree libraries
keep their gain when labels are shuffled while preserving counts (R-perm). This run asked whether the labels
matter at all once the shapes are kept. If they did not, a later statistical learner would only need to
learn shapes. New arm R-unif: the same skeletons, widths, sizes and multiplicities, with every node relabelled
uniformly within its class (binary {ADD, GT}, inputs {X0–X3, ANY}, constants {0, 1, 2, 5}), using 3 frozen
realizations per library. It was scored against R-perm, intact libraries (R-lib), size-matched random donors
(R-size) and plain restarted tree GP (R-tree). Setup: 12 B/C cells × 24 builds × 6 repeats, shared seeds,
8,640 jobs, commit `163f005`.

**What happened.**
- Primary: cost R-unif ÷ R-perm **2.07 [1.68, 2.61]**, a registered worthwhile loss. R-unif ÷ R-lib was
  1.95 [1.64, 2.32]. R-unif was worse in 23/24 builds and all 12 cells.
- R-unif was still cheaper than plain tree GP, at 0.755 [0.645, 0.884], but kept only about 40% of the
  learned donors' per-search saving. Repaying acquisition takes about 169 searches, against about 65.
- R-unif was not resolved from random size-matched donors (R-size ÷ R-unif 1.10 [0.86, 1.37]). Tails,
  budget curves and how often donor material ended up in solvers were all similar.
- Controls replicated within a few percent. 0524's retention held: R-lib ÷ R-perm 1.06 [0.88, 1.29].
  Solves were 1,725 vs 1,727 of 1,728.
- Price held: scoring took 61 min against a 70 min estimate; all five arms were admitted.

**What we learned.** Once the shapes are fixed, the learned label law carries most of the B/C donor gain.
With flat labels, the learned skeletons perform like random trees of the same size. Which part of the law
matters is still open, because the uniform draw changed four things at once: ADD share (84% → 49%), ANY
share (5% → 20%), the constants, and the dependencies between labels.

**How the tree changed.** 48 is closed. Root 10 is at 40/40. Digest: one new bullet, a shortened 47 bullet,
and an updated Overall paragraph. Critique wording fixes were applied: "almost entirely" in the digest, and
a dated correction in 47's log ("no resolved difference", not "no change"). No parked question reopens.

**Next.** No proposal: `next: strategy`, because the budget is spent and the strategy reserved this choice.
My recommendation is to keep empirical label supply in any next acquisition, and to test portability
first: build libraries from sources other than DG (7–10 h, unmeasured). The second choice is the
unfamiliar-content boundary (5–8 h). Single-label-component ablations should wait until a generator is
actually proposed.

**Failures and uncertainty.** There were no run failures, and validation and seed audits were clean. The
result covers one uniform law, one acquisition cohort, one extractor, and a development bank, so it makes
no transfer claim. A skeleton effect of up to about 30% under flat labels is not excluded. One error in
the analysis: it says about 85% of the saving was lost, but its own numbers give about 60%. The corrected
figure is used everywhere, and the decision does not depend on it.
