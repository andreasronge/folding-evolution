# Brief: run 2026-10-06-1723, crossed BE/PA token learning, stage 1 (root 10, 16)

**What was tested and why.** Root 10 asks whether a learned decoder carries a task family's
preferences, or just a generic speed-up. Strategy 1723 allowed a narrower split on the
four-reducer bank: one branch-else (BE) holdout and two post-addition (PA) holdouts. This first
stage learned token-weight maps from G4, independently for each family: 10 runs on the 4 BE
training cells and 10 on the 6 PA training cells. It then scored all 20 maps on every training
cell of both families. No holdout was touched.

**What happened.** Clean run: 4.3 h, commit `db96645`, 216 420 searches, no holdout searched.
Outcome **row 4**: both families learn, and the family difference is unresolved.

| fresh training cells, speed over G4 (95% CI) | BE-trained maps | PA-trained maps |
|---|---|---|
| own family | **2.18× [1.98, 2.40]** | **2.27× [1.95, 2.65]** |
| other family | 2.07× [1.94, 2.21] | 1.93× [1.61, 2.30] |
| matched over mismatched on own cells | 1.13× [0.93, 1.37] | 1.10× [0.93, 1.29] |

- All 20 maps beat G4. Learning took 10–14 min per run.
- The matched arm was ahead on 8 of 10 cells, with the other 2 tied.
- A post hoc combined contrast is 1.24× [1.09, 1.41]. Exploratory.

**What we learned.** The token learner works on both families. Most of what it learns is generic:
INPUT, IF_GT and ADD go up and DUP goes down, whichever family it trains on. Any family-specific
part is small, about 1.1×, and unresolved even on the cells each map trained on.
