# Brief: run 2026-10-11-1938: does the frozen R-all library recipe work on a fresh content family?

**What was tested and why.** The R-all recipe keeps every recurring behaviour class from evolved source solvers
as mutation donors. It cut tree-GP search cost about 2.5× on the order bank, including its held-out cells. But
that is one bank. The rectifier attempt (54) died because its sources rarely built the needed helper. The
strategist funded one complete test on new content: the signed gate `p(a,b) = Xa>0 ? Xb : 0` and its complement
`n`.
- **Sources:** `p/n(a,b)+(Xc+Xc)`.
- **Destinations:** `p(a,b)+n(c,d)`.
- **Freeze:** the bank and 18 protected cells were frozen with the method before any search.
- **Arms:**
  - R-tree: no library.
  - R-all: 24 new acquisitions.
  - R-order: the saved order libraries, transported.
  - R-gate: the 24 helpers supplied by hand.
- **Code and runtime:** commit `9da9c2c`. The queue took 6 minutes of the 110 allowed.

**What happened** (protected cells, 1,728 jobs per arm, every job solved, none capped):
- **Primary: R-all / R-tree 0.577 [0.47, 0.72]** in evaluations. The rule was upper bound < 0.80, so it is met.
  - Below 1 in 18/18 cells and 22/24 builds.
  - Worker seconds 0.535.
  - The six development cells agree (0.583).
- **New beats transported:** R-all / R-order 0.516 [0.44, 0.61].
- **The order libraries showed no saving here:** 1.12 [0.94, 1.34] of R-tree. A 20% saving is excluded; harm is
  unresolved.
- **The supplied prior is still far ahead:** R-gate / R-tree 0.246 [0.21, 0.30], 2.3× cheaper than R-all.
- **Libraries kept 8.6 of 24 helpers on average.** The saving was largest where both needed helpers were present
  (0.31), but it persisted where none were (0.77). This is descriptive and confounded.
- **Repayment:** acquisition (~216 k evaluations) pays back after about 66 [46, 110] searches.

**What we learned.** The unchanged acquisition recipe is not tied to the order family. On a second, freshly
frozen content family it cut search cost by about 40%, and order content did not substitute for it. It still
recovers only a third of the useful helpers, and a hand-supplied prior beats it 2.3×. This is useful acquisition,
not near-optimal acquisition.
