# Brief: run 2026-10-10-0145. Does the cheap acquisition recipe (A8) still work from different source tasks? (root 10, question 38) — yes: rebuilt from the other four cells per family it is again about 2.5× faster than no fit

**What was tested and why.** A8 is the cheap acquisition procedure. Per source cell it runs four
G4 attempts, fits a decoder and fragment library, runs four more attempts under that fit, and
refits. Until now it had only been built from the comparison-gate *training* cells, the same
cells its settings were developed on. Its 2.73× lead over G4 on `two-sum-v1` could therefore have
come from favourable sources. Here the unchanged recipe was rebuilt 16 times (8 per family) from
fresh searches on the **other** four cells per family (the former holdouts). The builds were
scored on the same two-sum targets and keys as run 2303. Margin set in advance: 1.5× over G4.

**What happened.** The run is complete and valid: 1 024 searches, 32 min of scoring, legacy build
replayed exactly, code review pass. Intervals are 95% over 16 builds.
- **Primary: cost(G4)/cost(A8′) 2.50× [2.17, 2.85].** It clears 1.5× per family (BE 3.14×, PA
  1.99× [1.66, 2.32]) and with a 1× cap penalty (2.06×). All 16 builds beat G4.
- **Against the original A8 builds on identical keys: 1.09× costlier [0.90, 1.32].** This is
  unresolved. PA lost ground (217 vs 249 of 512 solved), and three PA builds cost about 2× more;
  BE was unchanged.
- **Sources and cost.** The first batch solved 75% and the adaptive batch 255/256. Acquisition
  was 4.5 M evaluations per build (original 6.5 M). Measured in evaluations, it repays G4 after
  about 35 searches.

**What we learned.** A8's usefulness did not depend on the particular source cells it was
developed on. Within these two families, the *procedure*, not just the saved artifacts, is what
to carry forward. Whether the rebuilt version is as good as the original is open, especially for
PA.
