# Brief: run 2026-10-10-1717 — does cheap acquisition prefer its own task family?

**What was tested and why.** Every earlier family-specificity test compared two close output-addition
families and stayed unresolved near 1.0–1.1×. So we did not know whether the A8 recipe learns
something about its family or just repairs the weak G4 prior. This run crossed two families on one alphabet
(`v2_x4`, same executor and recipe). The first was double-gate DG `(Xa+Xb)>(Xc+Xd) ? Xe:Xf`, reusing 1536's
24 builds. The second was branch-sum TS `Xa>Xb ? Xc+Xd : Xe+Xf`, with a new performance-blind bank and 24
fresh builds. Both cohorts and G4 were scored on 8 never-searched cells per family (2 304 searches).

**What happened.**
- On DG cells, TS-built builds cost **2.98× [2.07, 4.33]** what DG-built ones did (2.40× [1.76, 3.30]
  with failures charged once). This held on all 8 cells. Solved: DG-built 307/384, TS-built 187/384, G4 59/384.
- On TS cells, DG-built cost 1.29× [0.94, 1.78] what TS-built did, so the direction is **unresolved**.
  Both cohorts solved 95–97 % here, so this roster could barely separate them.
- The pre-set interaction was 1.96× [1.58, 2.44]. It cleared its 1.5× margin with failures charged at 2 × cap,
  but not at 1 × cap (lower bound 1.45). It was not reciprocal, and no cohort was best everywhere.
- Every cohort beat G4 on both families by at least 2.75×. DG-built builds replicated 8.2× over G4 on the new DG
  cells (1536: 10.1×).
- TS sources were far easier (G4 solved 63 % on the first batch vs 17 % for DG), so a TS build costs half as
  much to acquire.

**What we learned.** For the first time in root 10, an acquired bias shows a resolved preference for its own
family: acquiring on the hard double-gate family is worth about 3× there compared with acquiring on a
neighbouring family. Whether that comes from the join content (`(a+b)>(c+d)`) or from learning on harder
sources is not separated, and the reverse direction is still open.
