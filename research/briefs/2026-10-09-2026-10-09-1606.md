# Brief: run 2026-10-09-1606. Do chain blocks need to keep the rest of the program unchanged? (root 10, question 34) — no: W/R 0.95×

**What was tested and why.** Inserting short blocks sampled from C's own previous-token chain
(W) speeds search over plain C by 1.23× on then-addition. W also repairs one allele after each
block so that the rest of the decoded program (the suffix) stays the same. The question was
whether that containment is part of W's gain. If it is, later acquisition baselines must keep the
repair. We built R, which uses the same blocks and draws without the repair, so the suffix can
re-decode ("ripple"). R was paired with 1036's W and C runs on the same seeds.

**What happened.** The run is complete and valid: 4 096 paired searches in 47 min. 15 360
historical rows were re-matched, 32 replays were bit-exact, and a 10 000-edit audit passed.
95% intervals over 16 corpora:
- **W/R 0.954× [0.903, 1.007]**, where > 1 would favour the repair. The upper bound is at most
  1.061 under every sensitivity and in both families.
- **R/C 1.286× [1.208, 1.370]**, 16/16 corpora. W/C was 1.227×.
- Solves: R 90.0%, W 88.9%, C 87.1%.
- The ripple is local. 64% of edits change the suffix, by about 3 tokens when they do.

Pre-registered rule 3 fired: *repair not needed at this resolution*. I expected W/R of 1.03–1.10;
it came out on the other side of 1.

**What we learned.** Keeping the suffix does not carry W's advantage over C. The coordinated chain
proposals help without it. In this decoder an edit's effects die out after a few tokens, so
"C's mutation re-decodes the whole suffix" (from question 32) was wrong and has been corrected.
