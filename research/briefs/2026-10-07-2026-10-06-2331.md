# Brief — run 2026-10-06-2331 (question 17, slot 1)

**What was tested and why.** The 20 learned token maps from 1723 (M) make fresh search 2–3×
faster than G4 (G) on the three withheld cells. A map changes two things at once: the programs
a search starts from, and what mutation and crossover produce afterwards. This run separated
the two. It used no new learning, 400 fresh seeds and the same three cells. GG and MM are the
normal arms. MG starts from *exactly* MM's generation-0 programs but searches under G. GM
starts from exactly GG's programs but searches under M. Programs were carried across by
re-encoding the alleles.

**What happened.** Clean run: 79 200 searches in 3.0 h, all validation passed, and 630
historical rows reproduced bit-exactly.
- Searching under M rather than G, from identical starting programs: **1.39× [1.31, 1.47]** faster.
- Starting from M's programs rather than G's, both searching under M: **1.30× [1.24, 1.36]** faster.
- Full M-over-G gain 2.29× [2.05, 2.54]. Starting from G, either part alone gives 1.65–1.76×
  (60–68% of the gain in log units). Interaction −0.34 log2 [−0.40, −0.29], negative in 20/20 maps.
- Per cell: the learned start carries more on the BE cell (decoder-during-search increment only
  1.11×), and the decoder during search carries more on both PA cells (1.46×, 1.65×).
- Robust to the cap. Only 12/79 200 searches solved at generation 0.

**What we learned.** The learned map does not work through one channel. Both its starting
programs and its use during search speed up search, and they largely supply the same thing:
either one alone gets about two-thirds of the way. The start effect comes from useful partial
programs, not seeded solutions. Which channel matters more may depend on the task shape, but
that rests on one BE cell.

**Failures and uncertainty.** My prediction was wrong. I put "the learned start adds little once
M is used during search" at 45%, misled by a 25-seed probe on three maps. The pooled numbers are
solid, but the scope is three screened, reused cells with hand-supplied G4 context. "During
search" bundles mutation, crossover, inherited alleles and continued supply, so it says nothing
yet about neighbourhood quality. The family test in the next run may come back unresolved: with
only 4 vs 6 cells, it resolves a 2331-sized difference only if cells within a family agree to
within about 0.35 log2. The run took 3.0 h against a 2.5 h plan; the code reviewer's 3.5 h
estimate was closer.
