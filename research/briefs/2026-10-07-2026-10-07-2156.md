# Brief: run 2026-10-07-2156 (question 22; root 10 slot 15 of 15)

**What was tested and why.** Run 1924 found that refitting each solver-corpus context table to
solvers found under it (C2) beat its parent C1 by 1.40×. That alone can't say *what* improved.
Feedback might have made context more valuable, or it might have improved the corpus for any fit.
This run scored the token-only fits to both corpora (T1, T2) on the exact seeds where C1 and C2
had been scored. The quantity is I = (C2/T2)/(C1/T1); I > 1 means feedback raised context's
advantage. It is the rerun of 2129, which had stopped on the deadline.

**What happened.** The run was clean: 26 min of a 75-min window, all hashes, 16 384 saved rows and
64/64 replays validated, and the full holdout stage ran.
- **Training (primary): I = 1.17× [1.09, 1.25]**, with 25/32 lineages above 1 and no single
  lineage carrying it.
- **The token-only fit improved too: T2/T1 = 1.20× [1.15, 1.26].** So of C2's 1.40×, the token fit
  matched about 1.20×, and the extra contextual part is about 1.17×.
- **It is a BE effect in this sample.** BE I = 1.34× [1.19, 1.50]. PA I = 1.02× [0.95, 1.09], which
  is not resolved and lies inside ±10%.
- **Withheld cells: I = 1.09× [0.98, 1.20], unresolved.** C2 still beats T2 there by 1.37×.

**What we learned.** On training cells, one feedback step made the fitted context somewhat more
valuable than a token-only fit to the same tapes, mainly on BE. About half of the feedback gain
(in log terms) is something a token-only refit also captures. Whether the extra contextual part
carries to unseen compositions is open.
