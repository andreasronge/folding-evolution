# Brief: run 2026-10-09-1743. Can four source attempts per cell replace the 48-attempt corpus? (root 10, question 35) — no: about 1.47× slower search

**What was tested and why.** The best pipeline on then-addition is a decoder C plus fragment
library F, both fitted to solvers from 48 G4 searches per training cell. That corpus is
expensive. We rebuilt C4 and F4 from only four capped attempts per cell, with failures charged,
which costs about 8% of the source evaluations. Each of the 16 corpora got four separate
acquisitions. The question was whether this cheap pipeline keeps the full pipeline's search
speed within 20%. A second question was whether F4 still beats chain blocks drawn from C4 (W4).

**What happened.** The run is complete and valid: 8 192 searches in 104 min, every gate passed,
and every replay was bit-exact. Intervals are 95% over 16 corpora:
- **Retention 0.679× [0.614, 0.752].** The cheap pipeline needs about 1.47× the search. Each of
  the 16 corpora was below 1. The 20% tolerance (0.833) is excluded under every sensitivity, in
  both families and in each source block. Solves: 85.9% against 90.8%.
- **C4+F4 is not resolved from full C alone** (0.996× [0.92, 1.08]). The cheap pipeline loses
  roughly the whole fragment advantage, but is still about 4× faster than G4.
- **F4 still beats W4** by 1.12× [1.03, 1.22]. This is unresolved against the worthwhile 1.10×.
- **Economics, counting acquisition plus searches in evaluations.** The cheap pipeline is the
  cheaper choice up to about 1 500 fresh searches [1 250, 2 000]; the full one is cheaper beyond
  that. Both repay G4 within about 30 searches.

Pre-set rule 2 fired: *tight loss*. I expected about 0.95. My small unpaired probe misled me.

**What we learned.** Four attempts per cell are too few to teach the decoder and library what 48
attempts do. The cheap version lands about where C alone does. Cheap sources pay off only when
few searches will follow. Individual builds vary widely (0.21–1.34). Builds with empty source
cells or small libraries did worse, but that pattern is post hoc.
