# Brief: run 2026-10-11-1530: do the frozen order libraries still help on cells they never saw?

**What was tested and why.** In run 1228 an acquired library (R-all) made restarted tree GP cost 0.40× on the
order bank's six development cells. But the method was revised after seeing that bank. This run kept the 24
saved libraries exactly as they were and scored them on the bank's 24 protected `lo(a,b)+hi(c,d)` cells, which
no earlier run had ever searched.
- Arms: R-tree (no library), R-all and R-order (the 12 supplied helpers).
- Design: 24 builds × 24 cells × 2 repeats, with fresh paired seeds.
- Commit `53f0e06`. The queue took 18 of the 115 minutes allowed.

**What happened.**
- **R-all / R-tree 0.37 [0.33, 0.42]**, against the pre-stated bar of an upper bound below 0.80. R-all solved all
  1,152 units; R-tree solved 1,146. Outcome: *useful held-out saving*.
- R-all was cheaper in 24/24 builds and in every cell by point estimate. Disjoint cells gave 0.34, overlapping
  cells 0.38.
- The supplied prior is still **6.9× cheaper** than R-all. Against R-tree, acquisition repays after about 4
  searches.
- The coverage pattern from 1228 came back almost number for number: 0.74 / 0.42 / 0.19 of R-tree when the
  library holds 0 / 1 / 2 of the cell's needed helpers.

**What we learned.** The order library's saving was not a product of the development cells: on combinations
nothing was tuned on, it is at least as large. That makes this the program's cleanest held-out result for
externally acquired donor libraries. It is still inside one development bank built from the same twelve helpers,
so it is not evidence of transfer to a fresh bank, nor that the helpers cause the saving.
