**Can the tree-library acquisition work with a different source family?**

Concept plan for [root 10](../questions/10-compositional-map-transfer/question.md),
allocated by [strategy 0833](../runs/2026-10-11-0833/strategy.md). The steward
supplies the proposal, fixed size and decision rule. This is a direction and
complete cost envelope, not an experiment registration.

The successful tree libraries all come from the same four double-gate (DG)
sources, three sharing one predicate split. Twenty-four independent builds
establish acquisition replication conditional on those sources; they do not
establish portability of the extractor. The latest replacements show that the
learned label law matters on gate-sum B/C. Preserve that information by retaining
the intact extraction procedure and changing its source family.

**Source change and destination.** Use the existing four branch-sum (TS) source
cells from 1717's frozen `banks.json`, `TS.split.source`, without selecting new
sources by yield or library content. TS has shape `A>B ? C+D : E+F`, on the same
`v2_x4` alphabet and D625 inputs. It moves the compound computation into the
outputs while retaining the primitive inventory. This is a different acquisition
family for the tree recipe, not a new bank or unfamiliar primitives.

The primary destination is **all twelve gate-sum B/C cells**, equally weighted,
where DG donors helped without a matching learned predicate. Holding this
destination fixed makes the acquisition change interpretable. Include the
existing eight TS target cells as a separately reported native boundary: if TS
libraries fail on gate-sum, useful native performance would distinguish a reuse
limit from a broader failure of this recipe on TS. Do not pool the destinations.
Both rosters are development data, excluded from collection. Recover their
manifests and verify source/target separation; fresh seeds do not restore bank
freshness. No new bank is needed for this acquisition question.

**Bounded build.** Reuse `research/main` at `163f005`, especially
`tree_library.py`, `tree_library_run.py`, `tree_gp_search.py`,
`restart_gp_run.py` and `gate_sum_run.py`. These runners are absent from the
current `main` checkout. Read-only inspection confirms that `extract` accepts
an explicit source roster and that search supports empty-library ordinary-donor
fallback. The 0150 orchestration hardcodes DG, schedules and cohort counts;
generalize those interfaces explicitly, including validators and reports.
Do not relabel TS jobs as DG to pass old checks. No executor, grammar or online
learning engine is required.

Collect new exact **tree** solvers under unchanged R-tree on the four TS sources.
Use the same fixed acquisition policy as 0150: eight scheduled jobs per source,
each with up to eight 65,536-evaluation attempts and exact D625 verification.
This is 32 jobs per build, not 32 successful solvers. Preserve all failures and
charge every attempt. Do not substitute saved tape solvers or collect until a
desired library is obtained.

Keep the current proper-subtree rule, input requirement, 3–9-node bounds,
recurrence across at least two source cells, deterministic ranking and 16-entry
maximum. Freeze it before collecting confirmation sources. Smaller or empty
libraries stay in the cohort with the existing fallback. Audit what changes
in shapes, labels, recurring expressions and library sizes, but never select
builds or alter extraction to obtain a desired contrast. Similar libraries from
different sources would itself be informative about shared supply; it is not
grounds to reject the acquisition.

Transfer only frozen libraries into fresh populations. Preserve initialization,
lexicase, crossover, the half-of-mutation-events donor rule, ordinary mutation
support, 32-token capacity and common restart schedule. Fully expand donors into
ordinary primitives. Keep actual insertion and oversize rejection as outcomes,
not parameters to retune. Validate compiler/interpreter/VM agreement, provenance,
empty fallback, ordinary-path replay and complete attempt accounting. Audit the
actual seed sets against source, calibration and historical scoring schedules.

**First experiment and what counts as an answer.** Compare new TS-library search
with contemporaneous R-tree. Retain all 24 saved DG libraries as the current
acquired reference, scored on the same destinations and fresh seeds. The primary
question is useful acquisition beyond R-tree on gate-sum B/C, not a requirement
that TS beat DG. The source cohorts are independent, not paired ancestors.
Propagate acquisition and search uncertainty; shared baseline rows must not
be counted repeatedly as independent replications. Use a fixed comparison with
one 95% cost interval, a decision-relevant effect and a reliability guard. A
roughly 20% evaluation saving is a sizing scenario, not a registered threshold.

Arithmetic capped evaluations remain the scientific cost unit; measure worker
time under comparable sustained load separately. Report each destination and
cell, solve fractions, failure sensitivity and acquisition variation. Near-complete
TS solving still permits cost differences. Conversely, its faster R-tree baseline
may leave less absolute saving: do not declare no headroom from solve fractions,
or lengthen tasks simply to manufacture it.

Useful gate-sum performance supports portability of this extraction recipe to
one other source family. Native-only usefulness bounds cross-family reuse.
A tightly small or harmful result on both destinations limits the unchanged
source/extractor policy; it does not reject library learning in general. A
broad interval is unresolved and needs a priced resolution. Any source comparison
bundles source content, difficulty, solve yield and resulting donor distributions.
It cannot attribute an effect solely to ADD placement or label frequencies.

The previous DG size-matched control does not match the new TS libraries.
If distinguishing learned content from new donor sizes would change the immediate
decision, price a TS-specific R-size control within the same envelope. Otherwise
keep the claim at complete-procedure usefulness; do not import the old content
attribution. There is no obligatory uniform/permuted/single-label matrix, and no
claim that external learning is required rather than a suitable supplied prior.

**Acquisition economics.** Charge source searches, failures, extraction and
verification once per newly deployed library. Show arithmetic acquisition-plus-
search curves in both units against R-tree and the DG recipe. Old DG acquisition
is sunk for execution, not free for deployment. New TS sources may be easier,
but their acquisition price and repayment must be measured. Do not transplant
the old 54–55-search repayment or assume an owner reuse horizon. A useful search
gain with an unattractive repayment remains a scientific result, not a reason
to expand the recipe automatically.

**Full price and exit.** One substantive experiment, **7–10 h total**: at most
two hours preparation/validation, roughly one to four hours collection/scoring,
and the remaining time for proposal, reviews, analysis and contingency. Summed
queue timeouts at most four hours; preparation at most 120 minutes. These are
planning allowances, not measured TS-library rates or additive entitlements.

The measured anchors are 0150's 768 DG source jobs plus preparation in 14 minutes
and 4,608 scoring jobs in 52 minutes, and 0652's 8,640 B/C scoring jobs in
61 minutes. A scenario with 24 new builds, four repeats, twenty target cells and
three arms has 5,760 scoring jobs plus 768 new source jobs. This illustrates a
plausible complete price, not an adequate sample-size prescription. New-source
yield, extraction, donor tails, optional controls and uncertainty need sustained
source/development calibration before fixing the confirmation size. Keep pilot
builds separate and do not tune on destination efficacy.

Return to strategy after the result or a measured build, validity, precision or
complete-cost obstacle. The comparison answers its question in one cycle; it
does not need an unfunded fresh-bank stage to become interpretable. A probe is
a fallback only when it can price a consequential complete answer; it consumes
this slot, has at most 60 minutes of queue timeouts and returns before confirmation.
Finish analysis before **2026-10-12T14:19:15 Europe/Stockholm**. No second source
roster, larger corpus, generator or feedback stage is allocated.

The nearest library precedent found in this review is
[Keijzer, Ryan and Cattolico, *Run Transferable Libraries* (2004)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-24855-2_63).
The different candidate mechanism is distribution learning as in
[Salustowicz and Schmidhuber, *PIPE* (1997)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10021756/).
This plan tests frozen external extraction and reuse, not PIPE updates or
per-individual map inheritance.
